> **Basic Knowledge: Solid Figure**<br>A geometric figure whose parts do not all lie in the same plane is called a solid figure.

> **Basic Knowledge: Net**<br>If the surface of a solid figure is cut open appropriately, it can be unfolded into a plane figure, called the net of the corresponding solid figure.

> **Basic Knowledge: A Moving Point Forms a Line, a Moving Line Forms a Plane, a Moving Plane Forms a Solid**<br>A moving point forms a line, a moving line forms a plane, and a moving plane forms a solid.

> **Basic Knowledge: Angle**<br>The figure formed by two rays with a common endpoint is called an angle.

> **Basic Knowledge: Measuring Angles**<br>$1^{\circ}=60'$, $1'=60''$. A full angle $=360^{\circ}$.

> **Basic Knowledge: A Moving Plane Forms a Solid**<br>A plane moving through space forms a solid, e.g. a rectangle rotating about one side forms a cylinder.

### Solution to Activity 2

By **Basic Knowledge: Solid Figure** and **Basic Knowledge: A Moving Plane Forms a Solid**, several cubes connected by tape can be flipped along the seams.

At each connection it can flip $180^{\circ}$ about the tape; combining several connections yields many different solid shapes. Different flip directions and angles give different shapes. $\blacksquare$

## Activity 3

(Mathematical Activity, Activity 2, Problem 1) Follow the steps to draw a five-pointed star:

(1) Draw any circle;

(2) With the center as vertex, successively draw angles of $72^{\circ}$, intersecting the circle at five points;

(3) Connect every other pair of points;

(4) Erase the extra lines to obtain a five-pointed star.

Explain the reasoning behind this construction.

### Solution to Activity 3

By **Basic Knowledge: Angle** and **Basic Knowledge: Measuring Angles**.

A full angle is $360^{\circ}$; dividing it into $5$ equal parts gives $72^{\circ}$ each. Successively drawing $72^{\circ}$ angles with the center as vertex gives five equally spaced points on the circle, namely the five vertices of the inscribed regular pentagon.

Connecting every other pair of points means drawing the diagonals of the regular pentagon; the five diagonals intersect to form a five-pointed star. This is because the diagonals of a regular pentagon exactly form a regular pentagram, with each sharp tip $36^{\circ}$.

The figure shows a circle with its inscribed regular pentagon: five equally spaced points on the circle (adjacent points separated by $72^{\circ}$) are arranged in order, and connecting every other pair of points (the five diagonals of the regular pentagon) yields a five-pointed star.

$\blacksquare$

## Activity 4

(Mathematical Activity, Activity 2, Problem 2) Make a five-pointed star by folding paper and cut it along different $\angle\alpha$; are the resulting five-pointed stars the same shape? Which one looks nicer?

### Solution to Activity 4

By **Basic Knowledge: Angle** and **Basic Knowledge: Measuring Angles**.

Fold a circular piece of paper twice (into five layers), then cut along $\angle\alpha$ at different angles. Unfolding gives a five-pointed star; the sharp-tip angle of the star is determined by $\angle\alpha$. When $\angle\alpha$ takes an appropriate value (about $36^{\circ}$ to $40^{\circ}$), the star's angles are uniform and its shape looks nicer; when it deviates from this angle, the angles vary and the shape is uneven. $\blacksquare$
