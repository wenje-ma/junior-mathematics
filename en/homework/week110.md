> **Definition 22.1.2** (Function) <br>Generally, in a process of change, if there are two variables $x$ and $y$, and for each definite value of $x$, $y$ has a unique definite value corresponding to it, then we say $x$ is the independent variable and $y$ is a function of $x$.
>
> **Conclusion 22.2.3** (Three Ways of Representing a Function) <br>Analytic method: express the functional relation by a mathematical expression; Tabular method: list the corresponding values of the independent variable and the function in a table; Graphical method: express the functional relation by a graph.

### Activity 1

(Mathematical Activity, Activity 1, Problem 1) Body fat percentage is the proportion of fat mass in body weight. At different times, a person's waist circumference (denoted $l$, unit: cm) and weight (denoted $w$, unit: kg) change; from these variables, the body fat percentage at different times can be calculated. The specific calculation process is as follows:

(1) Calculate $a$; $a$ is a function of the waist circumference $l$, $a=0.74l$;

(2) Calculate $b$; $b$ is a function of weight $w$; for males $b=0.082w+44.74$, for females $b=0.082w+34.89$;

(3) Calculate total fat mass $d$, $d=a-b$;

(4) Calculate body fat percentage $p$, $p=\frac{d}{w}\times100\%$.

Measure and record each student's waist circumference $l$ and weight $w$, fill in the body fat measurement table, and analyze the results.

### Solution to Activity 1

By **Definition 22.1.2** (Function), $a$ is a function of $l$, $b$ is a function of $w$, $d$ is a function of $a$ and $b$, and $p$ is a function of $d$ and $w$.

(1) From $a=0.74l$, substitute each student's waist circumference $l$ to obtain the corresponding value of $a$.

(2) For males, $b=0.082w+44.74$; for females, $b=0.082w+34.89$; substitute each student's weight $w$ to obtain the corresponding value of $b$.

(3) From $d=a-b$, substitute the $a$ and $b$ obtained in (1) and (2) to obtain the total fat mass $d$.

(4) From $p=\frac{d}{w}\times100\%$, substitute $d$ and $w$ to obtain the body fat percentage $p$.

Fill the above results into the body fat measurement table; comparing with the ideal body fat percentage of $14\%\sim20\%$ for males and $17\%\sim24\%$ for females, analyze whether each student's body fat percentage is within the ideal range. $\blacksquare$
