> **Definition 18.1.1** (Fraction) <br>If $A,B$ denote two integral expressions and $B$ contains a letter, then the expression $\frac{A}{B}$ is called a fraction. A fraction is meaningful only when $B\ne0$.

> **Definition 18.1.2** (Basic Property of Fractions) <br>Multiplying (or dividing) both the numerator and denominator of a fraction by the same integral expression not equal to $0$ does not change the value of the fraction, i.e. $\frac{A}{B}=\frac{A\cdot C}{B\cdot C}=\frac{A\div C}{B\div C}$ ($C\ne0$).

> **Definition 18.1.3** (Reducing a Fraction and Simplest Fraction) <br>According to the basic property of fractions, canceling the common factor of the numerator and denominator of a fraction is called reducing the fraction. A fraction whose numerator and denominator have no common factor is called a simplest fraction.

> **Definition 18.1.4** (Finding a Common Denominator and Least Common Denominator) <br>According to the basic property of fractions, converting several fractions with different denominators into fractions with the same denominator equal to the original ones is called finding a common denominator. Generally, the least common denominator is taken as the product of the highest powers of all factors of the denominators.

### Exercise 1

(Exercise 18.1, Problem 1) Write expressions for the following quantities:

(1) A village has $n$ people and $40\ \text{hm}^2$ of arable land; then the per capita arable area is \;_\_\; $\text{hm}^2$;

(2) The area of $\triangle ABC$ is $S$, and the base $BC$ has length $a$; then the height $AD$ is \;_\_\; ;

(3) A car travels $s\ \text{km}$ in $t\ \text{h}$; then its average speed is \;_\_\; $\text{km/h}$; a train travels $s\ \text{km}$ taking $1\ \text{h}$ less than the car; then its average speed is \;_\_\; $\text{km/h}$.

### Solution to Exercise 1

(1) Per capita arable area $=\frac{40}{n}\ \text{hm}^2$.

(2) From $S=\frac12 a\cdot AD$, we get $AD=\frac{2S}{a}$.

(3) The car's average speed $=\frac{s}{t}\ \text{km/h}$; the train takes $t-1\ \text{h}$, so its average speed $=\frac{s}{t-1}\ \text{km/h}$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 4

(1) $\frac{x-1}{x}$;

(2) $\frac{x-2}{x-1}$.

### Solution to Exercise 4

### Exercise 5

(Exercise 18.1, Problem 1) Reduce:

(1) $\frac{2bc}{ac}$;

(2) $\frac{(x+y)y}{xy^2}$;

(3) $\frac{x^2+xy}{(x+y)^2}$;

(4) $\frac{x^2-y^2}{x-y}$.

### Solution to Exercise 5

By **Definition 18.1.3** (reducing a fraction).

(1) $\frac{2bc}{ac}=\frac{2b}{a}$.

(2) $\frac{(x+y)y}{xy^2}=\frac{x+y}{xy}$.

(3) $\frac{x^2+xy}{(x+y)^2}=\frac{x(x+y)}{(x+y)^2}=\frac{x}{x+y}$.

(4) $\frac{x^2-y^2}{x-y}=\frac{(x+y)(x-y)}{x-y}=x+y$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 7

(Exercise 18.1, Problem 3) Find a common denominator:

(1) $\frac{z}{2x^2 y}$ and $\frac{5}{6xy^2}$;

(2) $\frac{x}{x+y}$ and $\frac{y}{x-y}$;

(3) $\frac{3}{2a^2 b}$ and $\frac{a-b}{2ab^2}$;

(4) $\frac{1}{a^2-2a+1}$ and $\frac{1}{a^2-1}$.

### Solution to Exercise 7

By **Definition 18.1.4** (finding a common denominator).

(1) Least common denominator $6x^2 y^2$: $\frac{z}{2x^2 y}=\frac{3yz}{6x^2 y^2}$, $\frac{5}{6xy^2}=\frac{5x}{6x^2 y^2}$.

(2) Least common denominator $(x+y)(x-y)$: $\frac{x}{x+y}=\frac{x(x-y)}{(x+y)(x-y)}$, $\frac{y}{x-y}=\frac{y(x+y)}{(x+y)(x-y)}$.

(3) Least common denominator $2a^2 b^2$: $\frac{3}{2a^2 b}=\frac{3b}{2a^2 b^2}$, $\frac{a-b}{2ab^2}=\frac{a(a-b)}{2a^2 b^2}$.

(4) $a^2-2a+1=(a-1)^2$, $a^2-1=(a-1)(a+1)$, least common denominator $(a-1)^2(a+1)$: $\frac{1}{(a-1)^2}=\frac{a+1}{(a-1)^2(a+1)}$, $\frac{1}{(a-1)(a+1)}=\frac{a-1}{(a-1)^2(a+1)}$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 1

(Exercise 18.1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 1) Write expressions:

(1) A place has an afforestation area of $m$ hectares, originally planned to be completed in $n$ years ($n>1$); now it must be completed $1$ year ahead of schedule, so the actual annual afforestation must be \;_\_\; hectares more than the original plan;

(2) A car travels from place A to place B at speed $a\ \text{km/h}$; the distance between A and B is $120\ \text{km}$; then the time taken from A to B is \;_\_\; $\text{h}$;

(3) The total number of students in a school is $m$, among which boys make up $\frac35$ of all students; then the number of girls is \;_\_\; .

### Solution to Problem 1

(1) The original plan is $\frac{m}{n}$ per year, and the actual is $\frac{m}{n-1}$ per year; the extra afforestation is $\frac{m}{n-1}-\frac{m}{n}=\frac{m}{n(n-1)}$ hectares.

(2) Time $=$ distance $\div$ speed $=\frac{120}{a}\ \text{h}$.

(3) Girls make up $1-\frac35=\frac25$, so the number of girls is $\frac{2}{5}m$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 4

(Exercise 18.1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) Reduce:

(1) $\frac{8a^2}{12a^3}$;

(2) $\frac{3(a-b)^2}{6(b-a)}$;

(3) $\frac{a^2-b^2}{a^2+ab}$;

(4) $\frac{x^2-25}{x^2+10x+25}$.

### Solution to Problem 4

By **Definition 18.1.3**.

(1) $\frac{8a^2}{12a^3}=\frac{2}{3a}$.

(2) $\frac{3(a-b)^2}{6(b-a)}=\frac{3(b-a)^2}{6(b-a)}=\frac{b-a}{2}$.

(3) $\frac{a^2-b^2}{a^2+ab}=\frac{(a+b)(a-b)}{a(a+b)}=\frac{a-b}{a}$.

(4) $\frac{x^2-25}{x^2+10x+25}=\frac{(x+5)(x-5)}{(x+5)^2}=\frac{x-5}{x+5}$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 5

(Exercise 18.1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 5) Find a common denominator:

(1) $\frac{z}{3x^2 y}$ and $\frac{x y}{2 z^2}$;

(2) $\frac{5}{a^2 b}$ and $\frac{3}{a b^2}$;

(3) $\frac{x-1}{-2 x}$ and $\frac{1}{x^2-2 x}$;

(4) $\frac{1}{x^2-9}$ and $\frac{1}{2 x+6}$.

### Solution to Problem 5

By **Definition 18.1.4**.

(1) Least common denominator $6x^2 y z^2$: $\frac{z}{3x^2 y}=\frac{2z^3}{6x^2 y z^2}$, $\frac{xy}{2z^2}=\frac{3x^3 y^2}{6x^2 y z^2}$.

(2) Least common denominator $a^2 b^2$: $\frac{5}{a^2 b}=\frac{5b}{a^2 b^2}$, $\frac{3}{ab^2}=\frac{3a}{a^2 b^2}$.

(3) $x^2-2x=x(x-2)$, least common denominator $2x(x-2)$: $\frac{x-1}{-2x}=\frac{(x-1)(x-2)}{-2x(x-2)}$, $\frac{1}{x(x-2)}=\frac{-2}{-2x(x-2)}$.

(4) $x^2-9=(x+3)(x-3)$, $2x+6=2(x+3)$, least common denominator $2(x+3)(x-3)$: $\frac{1}{(x+3)(x-3)}=\frac{2}{2(x+3)(x-3)}$, $\frac{1}{2(x+3)}=\frac{x-3}{2(x+3)(x-3)}$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 6

(Exercise 18.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) Fill in the blanks:

(1) $\frac{3a}{5xy}=\frac{(\;\;)}{10 a x y}$ ($a\ne0$);

(2) $\frac{a+2}{a^2-4}=\frac{1}{(\;\;)}$;

(3) $\frac{2 x}{x^2-3 x}=\frac{2}{(\;\;)}$.

### Solution to Problem 6

By **Definition 18.1.2** (Basic Property of Fractions).

(1) Multiply the denominator by $2a$, and the numerator by $2a$ as well: $\frac{3a}{5xy}=\frac{6a^2}{10axy}$.

(2) $a^2-4=(a+2)(a-2)$, reduce: $\frac{a+2}{(a+2)(a-2)}=\frac{1}{a-2}$.

(3) $x^2-3x=x(x-3)$, reduce: $\frac{2x}{x(x-3)}=\frac{2}{x-3}$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 7

(Exercise 18.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) Without changing the value of the fraction, make both the numerator and denominator of the following fractions contain no negative sign:

(1) $\frac{-5 y}{-x^2}$;

(2) $\frac{-a}{2 b}$;

(3) $\frac{2 m}{-3 n}$;

(4) $-\frac{-x}{2 y}$.

### Solution to Problem 7

By the basic property of fractions, when numerator and denominator have the same sign the result is positive.

(1) $\frac{-5y}{-x^2}=\frac{5y}{x^2}$.

(2) $\frac{-a}{2b}=-\frac{a}{2b}$.

(3) $\frac{2m}{-3n}=-\frac{2m}{3n}$.

(4) $-\frac{-x}{2y}=\frac{x}{2y}$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 8

(Exercise 18.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 8) Without changing the value of the fraction, make the coefficient of the highest-degree term in both the numerator and denominator of the following fractions positive:

(1) $\frac{-x^2}{-x}$;

(2) $\frac{1-x^2}{1+x}$;

(3) $\frac{-x-1}{x-1}$.

### Solution to Problem 8

(1) $\frac{-x^2}{-x}=\frac{x^2}{x}$.

(2) $\frac{1-x^2}{1+x}=\frac{-(x^2-1)}{x+1}=\frac{1-x}{1}$... rearranging gives $\frac{-(x^2-1)}{x+1}=-\frac{x^2-1}{x+1}$.

(3) $\frac{-x-1}{x-1}=-\frac{x+1}{x-1}$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 9

(Exercise 18.1, Extension and Exploration, Problem 9) A and B are driving on a highway; A travels $20\ \text{km}$ more per hour than B. Given that the time A takes to travel $400\ \text{km}$ is the same as the time B takes to travel $300\ \text{km}$, find the average speeds of A and B.

### Solution to Problem 9

Let B's average speed be $x\ \text{km/h}$; then A's is $(x+20)\ \text{km/h}$. Since the times are equal:

$$\frac{400}{x+20}=\frac{300}{x}.$$

Cross-multiplying: $400x=300(x+20)=300x+6000$, solving gives $x=60$.

Hence B's average speed is $60\ \text{km/h}$, and A's is $80\ \text{km/h}$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 10

(Exercise 18.1, Extension and Exploration, Problem 10) For a certain job, A alone needs $a$ days to finish it, and B alone needs $b$ days. What is the amount of work completed when A and B work together for $3$ days?

### Solution to Problem 10

A completes $\frac1a$ per day, B completes $\frac1b$ per day; together they complete $\frac1a+\frac1b$ per day.

The amount of work completed in $3$ days of joint work is

$$3\left(\frac1a+\frac1b\right)=\frac{3(a+b)}{ab}.$$

$\blacksquare$
