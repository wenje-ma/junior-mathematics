> **Definition 26.1.1** (Inverse Proportional Function) <br>In general, a function of the form $y=\frac{k}{x}$ (where $k$ is a constant and $k\ne0$) is called an inverse proportional function, where $x$ is the independent variable and $y$ is the function; the range of the independent variable $x$ is all real numbers except $0$.

> **Definition 26.1.2** (Graph and Properties of the Inverse Proportional Function) <br>The graph of the inverse proportional function $y=\frac{k}{x}$ is a hyperbola. When $k> 0$, the two branches of the hyperbola lie in the first and third quadrants, respectively, and in each quadrant, $y$ decreases as $x$ increases; when $k< 0$, the two branches of the hyperbola lie in the second and fourth quadrants, respectively, and in each quadrant, $y$ increases as $x$ increases.

> **Definition 26.1.3** (Method of Undetermined Coefficients) <br>Given the coordinates of a point on the graph of an inverse proportional function, substituting into $y=\frac{k}{x}$ yields $k$, thereby determining the function expression.

### Exercise 1

(Exercise 26.1, Problem 1) Express the correspondence between the variables in the following problems using function expressions:

(1) The volume of a swimming pool is $2000\ \text{m}^3$, and the time $t$ (unit: $\text{h}$) taken to fill the pool varies with the water-filling rate $v$ (unit: $\text{m}^3/\text{h}$);

(2) The volume of a cuboid is $1000\ \text{cm}^3$, and its height $h$ (unit: $\text{cm}$) varies with the base area $S$ (unit: $\text{cm}^2$);

(3) An object weighs $100\ \text{N}$, and the pressure $p$ (unit: $\text{Pa}$) it exerts on the ground varies with the contact area $S$ (unit: $\text{m}^2$) between the object and the ground.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 26.1.1** (Inverse Proportional Function), two variables are inversely proportional when their product is a constant.

(1) The volume equals the filling rate times the time, i.e. $vt=2000$, so

$$
t=\frac{2000}{v}.
$$

(2) The volume equals the base area times the height, i.e. $Sh=1000$, so

$$
h=\frac{1000}{S}.
$$

(3) The pressure equals the force divided by the area acted upon; the force is $100\ \text{N}$, i.e. $pS=100$, so

$$
p=\frac{100}{S}.\ \blacksquare
$$

### Exercise 2

(Exercise 26.1, Problem 2) In which of the following relations is $y$ an inverse proportional function of $x$?

$$
y=4x,\quad \frac{y}{x}=3,\quad y=-\frac{2}{x},\quad y=6x+1,\quad y=x^2-1,\quad y=\frac{1}{x^2},\quad xy=123.
$$

### Solution to Exercise 2

By **Definition 26.1.1** (Inverse Proportional Function), an inverse proportional function has the form $y=\frac{k}{x}$, i.e. $xy=k$ ($k\ne0$).

$y=4x$ is a direct proportional function; $\frac{y}{x}=3$ means $y=3x$, a direct proportional function; $y=-\frac{2}{x}$ fits the form $y=\frac{k}{x}$ with $k=-2$; $y=6x+1$ is a linear function; $y=x^2-1$ is a quadratic function; in $y=\frac{1}{x^2}$, $y$ is inversely proportional to $x^2$, rather than $y$ being inversely proportional to $x$; $xy=123$ means $y=\frac{123}{x}$, which fits the inverse proportional function form.

Thus the relations in which $y$ is an inverse proportional function of $x$ are $y=-\frac{2}{x}$ and $xy=123$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 3

(Exercise 26.1, Problem 3) Given that $y$ is inversely proportional to $x^2$, and when $x=3$, $y=4$.

(1) Write the function expression for $y$ in terms of $x$;

(2) When $x=1.5$, find the value of $y$;

(3) When $y=6$, find the value of $x$.

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Definition 26.1.1** (Inverse Proportional Function) and **Definition 26.1.3** (Method of Undetermined Coefficients), let $y=\frac{k}{x^2}$.

Substituting $x=3$, $y=4$ gives $4=\frac{k}{9}$, and solving gives $k=36$.

(1) The function expression is $y=\frac{36}{x^2}$.

(2) When $x=1.5$, $x^2=2.25$, $y=\frac{36}{2.25}=16$.

(3) When $y=6$, $6=\frac{36}{x^2}$, $x^2=6$, $x=\pm\sqrt{6}$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 6

(Exercise 26.1, Problem 1) The graph of an inverse proportional function passes through the point $A\left(3,-4\right)$.

(1) In which quadrants does the graph of this function lie? On each branch of the graph, how does $y$ change as $x$ increases?

(2) Are the points $B\left(-3,4\right)$, $C\left(-2,6\right)$, and $D\left(3,4\right)$ on the graph of this function? Why?

### Solution to Exercise 6

By **Definition 26.1.3** (Method of Undetermined Coefficients), let $y=\frac{k}{x}$. Substituting $A\left(3,-4\right)$ gives $-4=\frac{k}{3}$, so $k=-12$, and hence $y=\frac{-12}{x}$.

(1) $k=-12< 0$, so by **Definition 26.1.2**, the graph lies in the second and fourth quadrants, and on each branch $y$ increases as $x$ increases.

(2) $B\left(-3,4\right)$: $\left(-3\right)\times4=-12=k$, on the graph; $C\left(-2,6\right)$: $\left(-2\right)\times6=-12=k$, on the graph; $D\left(3,4\right)$: $3\times4=12\ne-12$, not on the graph. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 7

(Exercise 26.1, Problem 2) The points $A\left(x_1,y_1\right)$ and $B\left(x_2,y_2\right)$ lie on the graph of the inverse proportional function $y=\frac{1}{x}$. If $x_1< x_2$ and $x_1$, $x_2$ have the same sign, what is the size relationship between $y_1$ and $y_2$? Why?

### Solution to Exercise 7

By **Definition 26.1.2** (Graph and Properties of the Inverse Proportional Function), $k=1> 0$, so in each quadrant $y$ decreases as $x$ increases.

Since $x_1$, $x_2$ have the same sign, $A$ and $B$ lie in the same quadrant. From $x_1< x_2$, on the same branch $y$ decreases as $x$ increases, so $y_1> y_2$.

This can also be verified from the expression: $y_1-y_2=\frac{1}{x_1}-\frac{1}{x_2}=\frac{x_2-x_1}{x_1x_2}$. From $x_1< x_2$ we get $x_2-x_1> 0$, and from $x_1$, $x_2$ having the same sign we get $x_1x_2> 0$, so $y_1-y_2> 0$, i.e. $y_1> y_2$. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Problem 4

### Problem 5

(Problem 26.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 5) One of the intersection points of the graph of the direct proportional function $y=x$ and the graph of the inverse proportional function $y=\frac{k}{x}$ has y-coordinate $2$.

(1) When $x=-3$, find the value of the inverse proportional function $y=\frac{k}{x}$;

(2) When $-3< x< -1$, find the range of values of the inverse proportional function $y=\frac{k}{x}$.

### Solution to Problem 5

The intersection point has y-coordinate $2$ and lies on $y=x$, so its x-coordinate is also $2$, i.e. the intersection point is $\left(2,2\right)$. Substituting into $y=\frac{k}{x}$ gives $2=\frac{k}{2}$, so $k=4$, and hence $y=\frac{4}{x}$.

(1) When $x=-3$, $y=\frac{4}{-3}=-\frac{4}{3}$.

(2) $k=4> 0$, and in the third quadrant $y$ decreases as $x$ increases. When $x=-3$, $y=-\frac{4}{3}$; when $x=-1$, $y=-4$. Since $-3< x< -1$, we have $-4< y< -\frac{4}{3}$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 6

(Problem 26.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) If $y$ is an inverse proportional function of $z$, and $z$ is an inverse proportional function of $x$, what kind of functional relationship holds between $y$ and $x$?

### Solution to Problem 6

By **Definition 26.1.1** (Inverse Proportional Function), let $y=\frac{k_1}{z}$ and $z=\frac{k_2}{x}$ ($k_1,k_2\ne0$).

Substituting gives $y=\frac{k_1}{k_2/x}=\frac{k_1}{k_2}x$, where $\frac{k_1}{k_2}$ is a nonzero constant. Therefore $y$ is a direct proportional function of $x$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 7

(Problem 26.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) If $y$ is an inverse proportional function of $z$, and $z$ is a direct proportional function of $x$, with $x\ne0$, what kind of functional relationship holds between $y$ and $x$?

Let $y=\frac{k_1}{z}$ and $z=k_2x$ ($k_1,k_2\ne0$).

### Information Technology Application 1

(Information Technology Application: Exploring the Properties of Inverse Proportional Functions) (1) Is the graph of the inverse proportional function $y=\frac{1}{x}$ symmetric about the line $y=-x$?

(2) In the same rectangular coordinate system, as $\left|k\right|$ increases, does the position of the graph of the inverse proportional function $y=\frac{k}{x}$ move farther from or closer to the coordinate origin?

The figure shows multiple hyperbolas of the inverse proportional function $y=\frac{k}{x}$ (with $k$ taking various positive and negative values) plotted on the same rectangular coordinate plane, illustrating the symmetry of the hyperbolas about the lines $y=x$ and $y=-x$, and how the position of the hyperbola relative to the origin changes as $\left|k\right|$ varies.

### Solution to Information Technology Application 1

By **Definition 26.1.2** (Graph and Properties of the Inverse Proportional Function).

(1) The point symmetric to any point $\left(a,\frac{1}{a}\right)$ on the graph of $y=\frac{1}{x}$ about the line $y=-x$ is $\left(-\frac{1}{a},-a\right)$, and $\left(-\frac{1}{a}\right)\cdot\left(-a\right)=1$, so this point still lies on $y=\frac{1}{x}$. Hence the graph is symmetric about the line $y=-x$. In general, the graph of $y=\frac{k}{x}$ is symmetric both about the line $y=x$ and about the line $y=-x$.

(2) For the same $x$, $\left|y\right|=\frac{\left|k\right|}{\left|x\right|}$ increases as $\left|k\right|$ increases; the closest distance from the hyperbola to the origin also increases as $\left|k\right|$ increases. Hence, as $\left|k\right|$ increases, the hyperbola moves farther from the coordinate origin. $\blacksquare$
