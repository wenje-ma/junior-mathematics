> **Conclusion 21.1.1** (Interior Angle Sum of a Quadrilateral) <br> The interior angle sum of a quadrilateral is \(360^{\circ}\).
> 
> **Conclusion 21.1.3** (Interior Angle Sum of a Polygon) <br> The interior angle sum of an \(n\)-gon is \((n-2)\times 180^{\circ}\).
> 
> **Property 21.2.1** (Opposite Sides) <br> Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal.
> 
> **Property 21.2.3** (Diagonals) <br> The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
> 
> **Criterion 21.2.3** (Diagonals) <br> A quadrilateral whose diagonals bisect each other is a parallelogram.
> 
> **Property 21.3.2** (Diagonals of a Rectangle) <br> The diagonals of a rectangle are equal.
> 
> **Property 21.3.4** (Diagonals of a Rhombus) <br> The two diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular.

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 2)

Each interior angle of a polygon is equal, and the ratio of each interior angle to its adjacent exterior angle is \(3:1\). What is the interior angle sum of this polygon?

Interior and exterior angles are supplementary, ratio \(3:1\), so the exterior angle \(=45^{\circ}\).

By **Conclusion 21.1.4**, the number of sides \(n=\frac{360^{\circ}}{45^{\circ}}=8\).

By **Conclusion 21.1.3**, the interior angle sum \(=(8-2)\times 180^{\circ}=1080^{\circ}\).

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3)

As shown in the figure, extend diagonal \(BD\) of \(\square ABCD\) in both directions to points \(E,F\), with \(BE=DF\). Prove that quadrilateral \(AECF\) is a parallelogram.

Connect \(AC\) meeting \(BD\) at \(O\).

By **Property 21.2.3**, \(OA=OC,\ OB=OD\).

Also \(BE=DF\), so \(OB+BE=OD+DF\), i.e. \(OE=OF\).

The diagonals of quadrilateral \(AECF\) bisect each other; by **Criterion 21.2.3**, it is a parallelogram.

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4)

Among the vertical angles formed by the diagonals of a rectangle, one pair consists of two \(50^{\circ}\) angles. What are the angles between the diagonals and the sides?

The diagonals of a rectangle are equal and bisect each other, so half-diagonals form an isosceles triangle.

Apex angle \(50^{\circ}\), base angles \(=\frac{180^{\circ}-50^{\circ}}{2}=65^{\circ}\).

One angle of the rectangle is \(90^{\circ}\), so the angle between the diagonal and the other side \(=90^{\circ}-65^{\circ}=25^{\circ}\).

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 5)

As shown in the figure, can you use a rope to check whether the side of a bookshelf is perpendicular to both the top and bottom edges? Why?

First use the rope to measure the opposite sides of the top and bottom of the bookshelf; if both pairs of opposite sides are equal, the bookshelf frame is a parallelogram.

Then measure the two diagonals; if the diagonals are equal, then by **Criterion 21.3.1** the parallelogram is a rectangle, and the side is perpendicular to the top and bottom edges.

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 6)

As shown in the figure, the diagonals \(AC,BD\) of rectangle \(ABCD\) intersect at point \(O\), and \(DE\parallel AC,\ CE\parallel BD\). Prove that quadrilateral \(OCED\) is a rhombus.

Since \(DE\parallel OC\) and \(CE\parallel OD\), quadrilateral \(OCED\) is a parallelogram.

By **Property 21.3.2**, the diagonals of a rectangle are equal and bisect each other, so \(OC=OD\).

A parallelogram with equal adjacent sides is a rhombus.

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 7)

As shown in the figure, the diagonals \(AC,BD\) of square \(ABCD\) intersect at point \(O\), and \(E,F,G,H\) are the midpoints of \(AO,BO,CO,DO\), respectively. Prove that quadrilateral \(EFGH\) is a square.

By the triangle midline theorem, \(EF\parallel AB\parallel GH\), \(FG\parallel BC\parallel HE\); quadrilateral \(EFGH\) is a parallelogram.

The diagonals of a square are perpendicular and equal, so the midpoint quadrilateral has equal and perpendicular adjacent sides.

Therefore \(EFGH\) is a square.

### Review Problem 2

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 8)

As shown in the figure, the interior angles of hexagon \(ABCDEF\) are all equal, and \(\angle DAB=60^{\circ}\). What is the positional relation between \(AB\) and \(DE\)? Does \(BC\) have this relation with \(EF\)? Prove your conclusion.

Each interior angle of an equiangular hexagon \(=\frac{(6-2)\times 180^{\circ}}{6}=120^{\circ}\).

From \(\angle DAB=60^{\circ}\) and each interior angle \(120^{\circ}\), the consecutive interior angles are supplementary, so \(AB\parallel DE\).

Similarly \(BC\parallel EF\).

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 9)

As shown in the figure, quadrilateral \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram, \(BE\parallel DF\), and they meet diagonal \(AC\) at points \(E,F\), respectively; connect \(ED,BF\). Prove \(\angle 1=\angle 2\).

From \(BE\parallel DF\) and \(AB\parallel CD\), one can prove \(\triangle ABE\cong\triangle CDF\), giving \(BE=DF\).

Therefore quadrilateral \(BEDF\) is a parallelogram, \(ED\parallel BF\).

By equal alternate interior angles, \(\angle 1=\angle 2\).

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 10)

The quadrilateral obtained by joining the midpoints of the sides of any quadrilateral in order is called a midpoint quadrilateral.

(1) What shape is the midpoint quadrilateral of a parallelogram? Why?

(2) What shapes are the midpoint quadrilaterals of a rectangle, a rhombus and a square? Why?

(1) By the triangle midline theorem, both pairs of opposite sides of the midpoint quadrilateral are parallel and equal to half the original diagonals, so it is a parallelogram.

(2) The diagonals of a rectangle are equal, so the midpoint quadrilateral has equal adjacent sides, i.e. a rhombus.

The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular, so the midpoint quadrilateral has perpendicular adjacent sides, i.e. a rectangle.

The diagonals of a square are perpendicular and equal, so the midpoint quadrilateral has perpendicular and equal adjacent sides, i.e. a square.

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 11)

If a quadrilateral is an axisymmetric figure and has two perpendicular axes of symmetry, must it be a rhombus? Must it be a square?

Two perpendicular axes of symmetry show that the quadrilateral is symmetric about both axes, and its diagonals perpendicularly bisect each other.

Therefore it must be a rhombus.

But it need not be a square, because the diagonals need not be equal (a rhombus's diagonals need not be equal).

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 12)

With two congruent triangular pieces of paper, what quadrilaterals can you form? To form a rectangle, what kind of two congruent triangles are needed? What about to form a rhombus or a square?

Two congruent triangles form a parallelogram (by flipping along corresponding sides).

To form a rectangle, two congruent right triangles are needed.

To form a rhombus, two congruent isosceles triangles are needed.

To form a square, two congruent isosceles right triangles are needed.

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 13)

If you have no protractor or set square nearby and need to construct a \(30^{\circ}\) angle, you can use the following method: (1) fold rectangular paper \(ABCD\) so that \(AD\) coincides with \(BC\), obtaining crease \(EF\); (2) fold the paper once more so that point \(A\) lands on \(EF\) and the crease passes through point \(B\), obtaining crease \(BM\) and also segment \(BN\). This gives \(\angle ABM,\angle MBN,\angle NBC\) all equal to \(30^{\circ}\). Write out the proof.

After folding, \(EF\) is a midline, \(EF\parallel AD\parallel BC\).

Folding makes \(A\) land at \(N\) on \(EF\), and the crease passes through \(B\), so \(BA=BN\).

In \(Rt\triangle BNE\), \(BE=\frac{1}{2}AB=\frac{1}{2}BN\), so \(\angle BNE=30^{\circ},\ \angle EBN=60^{\circ}\).

Hence \(\angle ABN=60^{\circ}\), and crease \(BM\) bisects \(\angle ABN\), giving \(\angle ABM=\angle MBN=30^{\circ}\).

Also \(\angle ABC=90^{\circ}\), so \(\angle NBC=90^{\circ}-60^{\circ}=30^{\circ}\).

### Review Problem 3

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 14)

As shown in the figure, through point \(O\), the midpoint of diagonal \(AC\) of \(\square ABCD\), draw two perpendicular lines, meeting \(AB,BC,CD,DA\) at points \(E,F,G,H\), respectively; connect \(EF,FG,GH,HE\). Determine the shape of quadrilateral \(EFGH\) and explain why.

Since \(O\) is the midpoint of \(AC\) and the parallelogram is centrally symmetric, one can prove \(OE=OG,\ OF=OH\).

The diagonals of quadrilateral \(EFGH\) bisect each other, so it is a parallelogram.

Also the two lines are perpendicular, i.e. the diagonals are perpendicular.

Therefore parallelogram \(EFGH\) is a rhombus.

(Review Problem, Extension and Exploration, Problem 15)

As shown in the figure, quadrilateral \(ABCD\) is a square, \(E\) is the midpoint of side \(BC\), \(\angle AEF=90^{\circ}\), and \(EF\) meets the exterior-angle bisector \(CF\) of the square at point \(F\). Prove \(AE=EF\). (Hint: take the midpoint \(G\) of \(AB\) and connect \(EG\).)

Take the midpoint \(G\) of \(AB\) and connect \(EG\).

Since \(E,G\) are midpoints, \(AG=EC,\ BG=BE\); so \(\triangle BEG\) is an isosceles right triangle, \(\angle AGE=135^{\circ}\).

\(CF\) bisects the exterior angle, \(\angle ECF=135^{\circ}\), so \(\angle AGE=\angle ECF\).

Also \(\angle GAE+\angle AEB=90^{\circ},\ \angle CEF+\angle AEB=90^{\circ}\), so \(\angle GAE=\angle CEF\).

Hence \(\triangle AGE\cong\triangle ECF\), so \(AE=EF\).

(Review Problem, Extension and Exploration, Problem 16)

As shown in the figure, the diagonals of square \(ABCD\) intersect at point \(O\), which is also a vertex of square \(A_{1}B_{1}C_{1}O\), and the two squares have equal side length. No matter how square \(A_{1}B_{1}C_{1}O\) rotates about point \(O\), the area of overlap of the two squares always equals \(\frac{1}{4}\) of the area of one square. Think about why.

In square \(ABCD\), \(O\) is the center, and the four small triangles each have area \(\frac{1}{4}\) of the square.

When rotating about \(O\), the two small triangles corresponding to the overlap can be proved congruent (rotational congruence).

Therefore the overlap area always equals \(\frac{1}{4}\) of the original square's area.
