> **Definition 29.2.2** (Front View, Top View, Left View) <br>The view obtained in the front plane by observing from front to back is called the front view; the view obtained in the horizontal plane by observing from top to bottom is called the top view; the view obtained in the side plane by observing from left to right is called the left view.
> 
> **Conclusion 29.2.1** (Projection Rules for the Three Views) <br>Length aligned, height level, width equal. Visible outlines are drawn as solid lines, and invisible outlines as dashed lines.

### Project 1

(Project Learning, Problem 1) Using cardboard as the main material, make solid models represented by the two sets of three views, respectively.

### Solution to Project 1

By **Conclusion 29.2.1** (Projection Rules for the Three Views), imagine the solid shape from the three views: the front view determines the front outline, the top view determines the base shape, and the left view determines the side shape. Cut out the nets corresponding to the three views, fold and glue them, and you obtain the cardboard solid model. $\blacksquare$

### Project 3

(Project Learning, Problem 3) Each of the following groups of plane figures consists of four equilateral triangles.

(1) Which of them can be folded into a triangular pyramid?

(2) Draw the three views of the triangular pyramid that can be folded, and point out how the three views embody \"length aligned, height level, width equal\".

(3) If the side length of each small triangle is $1$, what is the surface area of the corresponding triangular pyramid?

The figure shows several groups of planar nets made up of four equilateral triangles (different arrangements), asking which ones can be folded into a triangular pyramid (regular tetrahedron).

### Solution to Project 3

(1) The condition for four equilateral triangles to be foldable into a regular tetrahedron (triangular pyramid) is that the four triangles form the surface net of that tetrahedron. In a net that can be folded into a triangular pyramid, the four triangles have a common vertex or are connected into a connected network, and after folding the edges match exactly.

(2) The three views of the regular tetrahedron are as shown in figure (1): the front view is an equilateral triangle (with a vertical dashed line inside indicating the blocked edge), the top view is an equilateral triangle (with a center point inside), and the left view is an equilateral triangle. The side lengths of the three views are equal, embodying length aligned, height level, and width equal.

(3) The area of each equilateral triangle is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\times 1^{2}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$. The triangular pyramid has four faces, so the surface area is

$$S=4\times\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}=\sqrt{3}.\ \blacksquare$$

### Project 4

(Project Learning, Problem 4) The following figure consists of a sector and a circle.

(1) Cut out the above figure and form it into a cone.

(2) Draw the three views of the cone formed.

(3) If the radius of the sector is $13$ and the radius of the circle is $5$, what is the volume of the corresponding cone?

### Solution to Project 4

(1) The sector forms the lateral surface of the cone, and the circle serves as the base, glued together to form the cone.

(2) The three views of the cone are as shown in figure (2): the front view is an isosceles triangle, the top view is a circle with a center, and the left view is an isosceles triangle.

(3) The sector radius is the cone slant height $l=13$, and the base circle radius is $r=5$. The cone height is

$$h=\sqrt{l^{2}-r^{2}}=\sqrt{169-25}=\sqrt{144}=12.$$

The volume is

$$V=\frac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h=\frac{1}{3}\pi\times 25\times 12=100\pi.\ \blacksquare$$
