> **Theorem 13.3.1** (Triangle Interior Angle Sum Theorem) <br>The sum of the interior angles of a triangle equals $180^\circ$.
> 
> **Conclusion 13.3.1** (The Acute Angles of a Right Triangle Are Complementary) <br>The two acute angles of a right triangle are complementary; conversely, a triangle with two complementary angles is a right triangle. A right triangle is denoted $\text{Rt}\triangle$.
> 
> **Definition 13.3.1** (Exterior Angle of a Triangle) <br>The angle formed by one side of a triangle and the extension of another side is called an exterior angle of the triangle.
> 
> **Corollary 13.3.1** (Relationship Between an Exterior Angle and the Non-adjacent Interior Angles) <br>An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior angles not adjacent to it. The sum of the three exterior angles of a triangle equals $360^\circ$.

### Exercise 1

(Exercise 13.3, Problem 1) As shown, the elevation angle from point $A$ to point $C$ is $\angle CAD=30^\circ$, and the elevation angle from point $B$ to point $C$ is $\angle CBD=45^\circ$. What is the viewing angle $\angle ACB$ from point $C$ to points $A$ and $B$?

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Corollary 13.3.1** (Relationship Between an Exterior Angle and the Non-adjacent Interior Angles), $\angle CBD$ is the exterior angle of $\triangle ABC$ at point $B$, and it equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles $\angle CAD$ and $\angle ACB$.

$$
\angle CBD=\angle CAD+\angle ACB.
$$

Substituting $\angle CBD=45^\circ$, $\angle CAD=30^\circ$, we get

$$
\angle ACB=45^\circ-30^\circ=15^\circ.
$$

$\blacksquare$

### Exercise 2

(Exercise 13.3, Problem 2) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A=40^\circ$; find the degree measure of $\angle B+\angle C+\angle ADE+\angle AED$.

### Solution to Exercise 2

By **Theorem 13.3.1** (Triangle Interior Angle Sum Theorem), in $\triangle ABC$,

$$
\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ-\angle A=180^\circ-40^\circ=140^\circ.
$$

In $\triangle ADE$, similarly, since $\angle DAE$ and $\angle A$ are the same angle,

$$
\angle ADE+\angle AED=180^\circ-\angle A=140^\circ.
$$

Therefore

$$
\angle B+\angle C+\angle ADE+\angle AED=140^\circ+140^\circ=280^\circ.
$$

$\blacksquare$

### Exercise 3

(Exercise 13.3, Problem 1) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, $\angle ACB=90^\circ$, $CD\perp AB$ with foot at $D$. What is the relationship between $\angle ACD$ and $\angle B$? Why?

### Solution to Exercise 3

$\angle ACD=\angle B$. The reason is as follows.

By **Conclusion 13.3.1** (The Acute Angles of a Right Triangle Are Complementary), in $\text{Rt}\triangle ABC$, $\angle A+\angle B=90^\circ$. Also in $\text{Rt}\triangle ACD$, $\angle A+\angle ACD=90^\circ$.

Both $\angle ACD$ and $\angle B$ are complementary to $\angle A$, so $\angle ACD=\angle B$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 4

(Exercise 13.3, Problem 2) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, $\angle C=90^\circ$, points $D$ and $E$ lie on sides $AB$ and $AC$ respectively, and $\angle 1=\angle 2$. Is $\triangle ADE$ a right triangle? Why?

### Solution to Exercise 4

$\triangle ADE$ is a right triangle. The reason is as follows.

By **Conclusion 13.3.1** (The Acute Angles of a Right Triangle Are Complementary), in $\text{Rt}\triangle ABC$, $\angle A+\angle B=90^\circ$. Since $\angle 1=\angle 2$ and $\angle 2$ and $\angle B$ are the same pair of corresponding angles, we get $\angle ADE=\angle B$.

Hence $\angle A+\angle ADE=\angle A+\angle B=90^\circ$, so in $\triangle ADE$, $\angle AED=180^\circ-\left(\angle A+\angle ADE\right)=90^\circ$. Therefore $\triangle ADE$ is a right triangle. $\blacksquare$

The figure shows four triangles arranged from left to right: in figure (1), the three interior angles are $39^\circ$, $108^\circ$ and $x$; in figure (2), all three interior angles are $x$ (i.e. $3x$); in figure (3), the two interior angles are $72^\circ$ and $2x$; in figure (4), the three interior angles are $x$, $x-36^\circ$, $x+36^\circ$.

### Problem 2

(Exercise 13.3, Review and Consolidation, Problem 2)

(1) At most how many right angles can a triangle have? Why?

(2) At most how many obtuse angles can a triangle have? Why?

(3) Can an exterior angle of a right triangle be acute? Why?

### Solution to Problem 2

By **Theorem 13.3.1** (Triangle Interior Angle Sum Theorem), the sum of the three interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$.

(1) At most $1$ right angle. If there were two right angles, their sum would already be $180^\circ$, and the third angle would have to be $0^\circ$, which cannot form a triangle.

(2) At most $1$ obtuse angle. If there were two obtuse angles, their sum would already exceed $180^\circ$, contradicting the interior-angle sum of $180^\circ$.

(3) It cannot. In a right triangle, the exterior angle of the right angle is $90^\circ$, and the exterior angles of the two acute angles are respectively $180^\circ$ minus an acute angle, which must be greater than $90^\circ$; hence none of its exterior angles is acute. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 3

(Exercise 13.3, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle B$ is $10^\circ$ greater than $\angle A$, and $\angle C$ is $10^\circ$ greater than $\angle B$. Find the degree measures of the interior angles of $\triangle ABC$.

### Solution to Problem 3

By **Theorem 13.3.1** (Triangle Interior Angle Sum Theorem), let $\angle A=x$; then $\angle B=x+10^\circ$, $\angle C=x+20^\circ$.

$$
x+\left(x+10^\circ\right)+\left(x+20^\circ\right)=180^\circ.
$$

Solving gives $3x=150^\circ$, i.e. $x=50^\circ$. Hence $\angle A=50^\circ$, $\angle B=60^\circ$, $\angle C=70^\circ$. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Problem 4

$$
\angle DAC=90^\circ-\angle C=90^\circ-65^\circ=25^\circ.

In $\text{Rt}\triangle ADB$, $\angle ADB=90^\circ$, so $\angle 1+\angle 2=90^\circ$. From $\angle 1=\angle 2$, we get $\angle 1=45^\circ$.

### Problem 5

(Exercise 13.3, Comprehensive Application, Problem 5) As shown, $AB\parallel CD$, $\angle A=40^\circ$, $\angle D=45^\circ$. Find the degree measures of $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$.

### Solution to Problem 5

Since $AB\parallel CD$, the alternate interior angles cut by line $AC$ are equal, so $\angle 1=\angle A=40^\circ$. Similarly, line $BD$ cuts out $\angle DBA=\angle D=45^\circ$.

In $\triangle AOB$, by **Theorem 13.3.1** (Triangle Interior Angle Sum Theorem),

$$
\angle 2=180^\circ-\angle A-\angle DBA=180^\circ-40^\circ-45^\circ=95^\circ.
$$

Hence $\angle 1=40^\circ$, $\angle 2=95^\circ$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 7

(Exercise 13.3, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) As shown, point $B$ is in the direction $45^\circ$ west of south from point $A$, point $C$ is in the direction $15^\circ$ east of south from point $A$, and point $C$ is in the direction $80^\circ$ east of north from point $B$. Find the degree measure of $\angle ACB$.

### Solution to Problem 7

At point $A$, taking the due-south direction as the reference, $B$ is $45^\circ$ to its west and $C$ is $15^\circ$ to its east, so

$$
\angle BAC=45^\circ+15^\circ=60^\circ.
$$

The due-north/south lines through points $A$ and $B$ are parallel; by equal alternate interior angles, viewing $A$ from $B$ is $45^\circ$ east of north. Also $C$ is $80^\circ$ east of north from $B$, so

$$
\angle ABC=80^\circ-45^\circ=35^\circ.
$$

By **Theorem 13.3.1** (Triangle Interior Angle Sum Theorem),

$$
\angle ACB=180^\circ-\angle BAC-\angle ABC=180^\circ-60^\circ-35^\circ=85^\circ.
$$

$\blacksquare$

### Problem 9

(Exercise 13.3, Comprehensive Application, Problem 9) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A=100^\circ$, $\angle 1=\angle 2$, $\angle 3=\angle 4$. Find the value of $x$.

### Solution to Problem 9

By **Theorem 13.3.1** (Triangle Interior Angle Sum Theorem),

$$
\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ-\angle A=80^\circ.
$$

From $\angle 1=\angle 2$, we get $\angle B=2\angle 2$; from $\angle 3=\angle 4$, we get $\angle C=2\angle 4$. Hence

$$
2\angle 2+2\angle 4=80^\circ,\quad \angle 2+\angle 4=40^\circ.
$$

In the triangle containing $x$,

$$
x=180^\circ-\left(\angle 2+\angle 4\right)=180^\circ-40^\circ=140^\circ.
$$

$\blacksquare$

### Problem 11

(Exercise 13.3, Extension and Exploration, Problem 11) As shown, $CE$ is the bisector of the exterior angle $\angle ACD$ of $\triangle ABC$, and $CE$ meets the extension of $BA$ at point $E$. Prove that $\angle BAC=\angle B+2\angle E$.

### Solution to Problem 11

By **Corollary 13.3.1** (Relationship Between an Exterior Angle and the Non-adjacent Interior Angles), $\angle BAC$ is the exterior angle of $\triangle ACE$ at point $A$, so

$$
\angle BAC=\angle ACE+\angle E.
$$

Also $\angle ACD$ is an exterior angle of $\triangle ABC$, so

$$
\angle ACD=\angle B+\angle BAC.
$$

Since $CE$ bisects $\angle ACD$, we get $\angle ACD=2\angle ACE$. Substituting into the above:

$$
2\angle ACE=\angle B+\angle BAC.
$$

Substituting $\angle ACE=\angle BAC-\angle E$:

$$
2\left(\angle BAC-\angle E\right)=\angle B+\angle BAC,
$$

which simplifies to $\angle BAC=\angle B+2\angle E$. $\blacksquare$
