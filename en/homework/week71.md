> **Conclusion 14.1.1** (Properties of Congruent Triangles) <br>Corresponding sides of congruent triangles are equal, and corresponding angles are equal.
>
> **Basic Fact 14.2.1** (SAS) <br>Two triangles are congruent if two sides and their included angle are respectively equal.
>
> **Basic Fact 14.2.2** (ASA) <br>Two triangles are congruent if two angles and their included side are respectively equal.
>
> **Conclusion 14.2.1** (AAS) <br>Two triangles are congruent if two angles are respectively equal and the opposite side of one pair of equal angles is equal.
>
> **Basic Fact 14.2.3** (SSS) <br>Two triangles are congruent if the three sides are respectively equal.
>
> **Conclusion 14.2.2** (HL) <br>Two right triangles are congruent if the hypotenuse and one leg are respectively equal.
>
> **Conclusion 14.3.1** (Properties of an Angle Bisector) <br>A point on the bisector of an angle is equidistant from the two sides of the angle.
>
> **Conclusion 14.3.2** (Determination of an Angle Bisector) <br>A point inside an angle that is equidistant from the two sides lies on the bisector of the angle.

### Review Problem 1

(Review Problems, Review and Consolidation, Problem 1) There are three squares in the figure; state all the congruent triangles in the figure.

### Solution to Review Problem 1

The three squares have equal side lengths. The triangles cut out by the diagonals can be determined congruent by SAS whenever their two legs are respectively sides of the square or are equal. In the figure, all triangles bounded by equal sides and right angles are pairwise congruent: the two triangles into which one square is divided along its diagonal are congruent, and corresponding triangles with equal side lengths are also congruent. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 2

(Review Problems, Review and Consolidation, Problem 2) As shown, in rectangle $ABCD$, $AF\perp BD$, with foot at $E$, $AF$ meets $BC$ at point $F$, and join $DF$.

(1) Are there congruent triangles in the figure?

(2) Are there triangles of equal area that are not congruent in the figure?

### Solution to Review Problem 2

(1) Yes. For example, $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle CDB$: from $AB=CD$, $AD=CB$, $\angle BAD=\angle DCB=90^\circ$, they can be determined congruent by SAS.

(2) Yes. Taking, for example, triangles on the same base $AD$ or $BC$ with equal heights, such as $\triangle ABF$ and $\triangle DBF$, they share the same base $BF$ and have equal heights, so their areas are equal, but their shapes are not the same; hence they have equal area but are not congruent. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 3

(Review Problems, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) As shown, $AB=AC$, $AD=BD=AE=CE$. Prove that $\angle D=\angle E$.

### Solution to Review Problem 3

In $\triangle ADB$ and $\triangle AEC$, $AD=AE$, $BD=CE$, $AB=AC$; by **Basic Fact 14.2.3** (SSS), $\triangle ADB\cong\triangle AEC$.

By **Conclusion 14.1.1**, the corresponding angles satisfy $\angle D=\angle E$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 4

(Review Problems, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) As shown, $CA=CD$, $\angle 1=\angle 2$, $BC=EC$. Prove that $AB=DE$.

### Solution to Review Problem 4

From $\angle 1=\angle 2$, adding $\angle ACE$ to both sides gives $\angle ACB=\angle DCE$. In $\triangle ACB$ and $\triangle DCE$, $CA=CD$, $\angle ACB=\angle DCE$, $BC=EC$; by **Basic Fact 14.2.1** (SAS), $\triangle ACB\cong\triangle DCE$.

By **Conclusion 14.1.1**, the corresponding sides satisfy $AB=DE$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 5

(Review Problems, Review and Consolidation, Problem 5) As shown, there are two observation points $A$, $B$ on the coast, $B$ is due east of $A$, island $C$ is due north of $A$, and island $D$ is due north of $B$. If $\angle CAD=\angle CBD$, explain why the distances $CA$, $DB$ from islands $C$, $D$ to the coast are equal.

### Solution to Review Problem 5

Let $AD$ and $BC$ intersect at point $O$. From $CA\perp AB$, $DB\perp AB$, we get $\angle CAO=\angle DBO=90^\circ$. In $\triangle ACO$ and $\triangle BDO$, $\angle CAO=\angle DBO$, $\angle AOC=\angle BOD$ (vertical angles), and with the condition on the opposite side of $\angle CAO$ combined with $\angle CAD=\angle CBD$, by **Conclusion 14.2.1** (AAS), $\triangle ACO\cong\triangle BDO$.

By **Conclusion 14.1.1**, the corresponding sides satisfy $CA=DB$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 6

(Review Problems, Review and Consolidation, Problem 6) As shown, $AB=CD$, $AC\perp BD$, with foot at $E$, $AE=CE$. Prove that $BE=DE$.

### Solution to Review Problem 6

From $AC\perp BD$, we get $\angle AEB=\angle CED=90^\circ$. In $\text{Rt}\triangle ABE$ and $\text{Rt}\triangle CDE$, the hypotenuses satisfy $AB=CD$, and the legs satisfy $AE=CE$; by **Conclusion 14.2.2** (HL), $\text{Rt}\triangle ABE\cong\text{Rt}\triangle CDE$.

By **Conclusion 14.1.1**, the corresponding legs satisfy $BE=DE$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 7

(Review Problems, Review and Consolidation, Problem 7) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$, $DE\perp AB$, $DF\perp AC$, with feet at $E$, $F$ respectively, $BE=CF$. Prove that $AD$ is the angle bisector of $\triangle ABC$.

### Solution to Review Problem 7

Since $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$, $BD=CD$. In $\text{Rt}\triangle BDE$ and $\text{Rt}\triangle CDF$, the hypotenuses satisfy $BD=CD$, and the legs satisfy $BE=CF$; by **Conclusion 14.2.2** (HL), $\text{Rt}\triangle BDE\cong\text{Rt}\triangle CDF$.

By **Conclusion 14.1.1**, the corresponding legs satisfy $DE=DF$. That is, point $D$ is equidistant from $AB$, $AC$. By **Conclusion 14.3.2** (Determination of an Angle Bisector), point $D$ lies on the bisector of $\angle A$, so $AD$ is the angle bisector of $\triangle ABC$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 8

(Review Problems, Review and Consolidation, Problem 8) As shown, a resort is to be built on the flat ground enclosed by three highways, so that it is equidistant from the three highways. Where should it be built?

### Solution to Review Problem 8

By **Conclusion 14.3.1** (Properties of an Angle Bisector) and **Conclusion 14.3.3** (Angle Bisectors of a Triangle), the three angle bisectors of a triangle intersect at one point, which is equidistant from the three sides. Hence the resort should be built at the intersection of the three angle bisectors (i.e. the incenter) of the triangle enclosed by the three highways. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 9

(Review Problems, Comprehensive Application, Problem 9) As shown, two cars start simultaneously from the two ends of road segment $AB$, travel along parallel routes at the same speed, and reach $C$, $D$ respectively after the same time. Are the distances $CE$, $DF$ from $C$, $D$ to road segment $AB$ equal? Why?

### Solution to Review Problem 9

They are equal. Since they travel at the same speed for the same time, $AC=BD$. Since the routes are parallel, $\angle CAE=\angle DBF$. Also $CE\perp AB$, $DF\perp AB$, giving $\angle CEA=\angle DFB=90^\circ$. In $\triangle ACE$ and $\triangle BDF$, $\angle CEA=\angle DFB$, $\angle CAE=\angle DBF$, $AC=BD$; by **Conclusion 14.2.1** (AAS), $\triangle ACE\cong\triangle BDF$.

By **Conclusion 14.1.1**, the corresponding sides satisfy $CE=DF$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 10

(Review Problems, Comprehensive Application, Problem 10) As shown, point $C$ is the midpoint of $AB$, $CD\parallel BE$, and $CD=BE$. Prove that $AD\parallel CE$.

### Solution to Review Problem 10

Since $C$ is the midpoint of $AB$, $AC=CB$. From $CD\parallel BE$, we get $\angle ACD=\angle CBE$. In $\triangle ACD$ and $\triangle CBE$, $AC=CB$, $\angle ACD=\angle CBE$, $CD=BE$; by **Basic Fact 14.2.1** (SAS), $\triangle ACD\cong\triangle CBE$.

By **Conclusion 14.1.1**, the corresponding angles satisfy $\angle ADC=\angle CEB$, which are alternate interior angles, so $AD\parallel CE$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 11

(Review Problems, Comprehensive Application, Problem 11) As shown, $\angle ACB=90^\circ$, $AC=BC$, $AD\perp CE$, $BE\perp CE$, with feet at $D$, $E$ respectively, $AD=2.5$, $DE=1.7$. Find the length of $BE$.

### Solution to Review Problem 11

From $AD\perp CE$, $BE\perp CE$, we get $\angle ADC=\angle CEB=90^\circ$. From $\angle ACB=90^\circ$, we get $\angle ACD+\angle BCE=90^\circ$. Also in $\text{Rt}\triangle ADC$, $\angle ACD+\angle CAD=90^\circ$, so $\angle CAD=\angle BCE$.

In $\triangle ACD$ and $\triangle CBE$, $\angle ADC=\angle CEB$, $\angle CAD=\angle BCE$, $AC=CB$; by **Conclusion 14.2.1** (AAS), $\triangle ACD\cong\triangle CBE$.

By **Conclusion 14.1.1**, $CD=BE$, $CE=AD=2.5$. Also $CE=CD+DE$, so $CD=CE-DE=2.5-1.7=0.8$, hence $BE=0.8$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 12

(Review Problems, Comprehensive Application, Problem 12) As shown, in triangular paper sheet $ABC$, $AB=8\ \text{cm}$, $BC=6\ \text{cm}$, $AC=5\ \text{cm}$. Fold along a line through point $B$ so that point $C$ falls on point $E$ on side $AB$; the crease is $BD$. Find the perimeter of $\triangle AED$.

### Solution to Review Problem 12

By the folding, $\triangle BCD\cong\triangle BED$, so $BE=BC=6$, $DE=DC$.

Thus $AE=AB-BE=8-6=2$. The perimeter of $\triangle AED$ is

$$
AE+AD+DE=AE+AD+DC=AE+AC=2+5=7\ \text{cm}.
$$

$\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 13

(Review Problems, Comprehensive Application, Problem 13) As shown, $\triangle ABC\cong\triangle A'B'C'$, and $AD$, $A'D'$ are respectively the medians on the corresponding sides of the two triangles. What relationship do $AD$ and $A'D'$ have? Prove your conclusion.

### Solution to Review Problem 13

$AD=A'D'$. From $\triangle ABC\cong\triangle A'B'C'$, we get $AB=A'B'$, $BC=B'C'$, $\angle B=\angle B'$. Since $AD$, $A'D'$ are medians, $BD=\frac{1}{2}BC$, $B'D'=\frac{1}{2}B'C'$, so $BD=B'D'$.

In $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle A'B'D'$, $AB=A'B'$, $\angle B=\angle B'$, $BD=B'D'$; by **Basic Fact 14.2.1** (SAS), $\triangle ABD\cong\triangle A'B'D'$.

By **Conclusion 14.1.1**, the corresponding sides satisfy $AD=A'D'$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 14

(Review Problems, Extension and Exploration, Problem 14) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, $AD$ is its angle bisector. Prove that $S_{\triangle ABD}:S_{\triangle ACD}=AB:AC$.

### Solution to Review Problem 14

Through point $D$ draw $DE\perp AB$, $DF\perp AC$, with feet at $E$, $F$. Since $AD$ is an angle bisector and by **Conclusion 14.3.1** (Properties of an Angle Bisector), $DE=DF$.

Taking $AB$, $AC$ as the bases, the two triangles share the same height $DE=DF$, so

$$
\frac{S_{\triangle ABD}}{S_{\triangle ACD}}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}\cdot AB\cdot DE}{\frac{1}{2}\cdot AC\cdot DF}=\frac{AB}{AC}.
$$

That is, $S_{\triangle ABD}:S_{\triangle ACD}=AB:AC$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 15

(Review Problems, Extension and Exploration, Problem 15) Prove: if two triangles have two sides respectively equal and the median on one of the sides respectively equal, then the two triangles are congruent.

### Solution to Review Problem 15

Let in $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$, $AB=A'B'$, $BC=B'C'$, $AD$, $A'D'$ be respectively the medians on sides $BC$, $B'C'$, and $AD=A'D'$.

Since $D$, $D'$ are midpoints, $BD=\frac{1}{2}BC=\frac{1}{2}B'C'=B'D'$. In $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle A'B'D'$, $AB=A'B'$, $BD=B'D'$, $AD=A'D'$; by **Basic Fact 14.2.3** (SSS), $\triangle ABD\cong\triangle A'B'D'$, so $\angle B=\angle B'$.

In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$, $AB=A'B'$, $\angle B=\angle B'$, $BC=B'C'$; by **Basic Fact 14.2.1** (SAS), $\triangle ABC\cong\triangle A'B'C'$. $\blacksquare$
