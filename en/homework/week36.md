> **Basic Knowledge: Square Root**<br>If a number $x$ has square equal to $a$, i.e. $x^2=a$, then this number $x$ is called a square root (or second root) of $a$. The operation of finding the square root of a number is called extracting the square root, and extracting the square root and squaring are inverse operations of each other.

> **Basic Knowledge: Arithmetic Square Root**<br>The positive square root $\sqrt a$ of a positive number $a$ is called the arithmetic square root of $a$, and $a$ is called the radicand. It is stipulated that the arithmetic square root of $0$ is $0$, i.e. $\sqrt 0=0$. The square roots of a positive number $a$ are denoted by $\pm\sqrt a$.

## Exercise 1

(Exercise 8.1, Problem 1) True or false.

(1) The square root of $1$ is $1$;

(2) The square root of $-1$ is $-1$;

(3) $0.5$ is a square root of $0.25$;

(4) The square root of $0$ is $0$.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Basic Knowledge: Properties of Square Roots**, a positive number has two square roots that are opposite numbers, the square root of $0$ is $0$, and a negative number has no square root.

(1) The square roots of $1$ are $\pm 1$, not just $1$, so it is wrong.

(2) $-1$ is a negative number, and a negative number has no square root, so it is wrong.

(3) $\because 0.5^2=0.25$, $\therefore 0.5$ is a square root of $0.25$ (the square roots of $0.25$ are $\pm 0.5$), so it is correct.

(4) By **Basic Knowledge: Properties of Square Roots**, the square root of $0$ is $0$, so it is correct. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 2

## Exercise 3

(Exercise 8.1, Problem 3) Find the value of $x$ in each of the following:

(1) $x^2=25$;　(2) $9x^2=4$;　(3) $\left(x-1\right)^2=1$.

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Basic Knowledge: Square Root**, when $x^2=a$, $x=\pm\sqrt a$.

(1) $x^2=25$, so $x=\pm\sqrt{25}=\pm 5$.

(2) $9x^2=4$, i.e. $x^2=\frac{4}{9}$, so $x=\pm\sqrt{\frac{4}{9}}=\pm\frac{2}{3}$.

(3) $\left(x-1\right)^2=1$, so $x-1=\pm\sqrt 1=\pm 1$. When $x-1=1$, $x=2$; when $x-1=-1$, $x=0$. Thus $x=2$ or $x=0$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 6

(Exercise 8.1, Problem 3) A volleyball court is rectangular, its length is $2$ times its width, and its area is $162\,\text{m}^2$. What are its length and width respectively?

### Solution to Exercise 6

Let the width be $x\,\text{m}$; then the length is $2x\,\text{m}$. By the area formula of a rectangle,

$$
x\cdot 2x=162,\quad 2x^2=162,\quad x^2=81.
$$

By **Basic Knowledge: Square Root**, $x=\sqrt{81}=9$ (take the positive value). Thus the width is $9\,\text{m}$ and the length is $2x=18\,\text{m}$. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 8

(1) $\because 2^2=4< 5< 9=3^2$, $\therefore 2< \sqrt 5< 3$, i.e. $\sqrt 5$ lies between $2$ and $3$.

(3) $\because 4^3=64< 83< 125=5^3$, $\therefore 4< \sqrt\left[3\right]{83}< 5$, i.e. $\sqrt\left[3\right]{83}$ lies between $4$ and $5$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 9

(Exercise 8.1, Problem 3) A rectangular sheet of drawing paper has area $700\,\text{cm}^2$, and the ratio of its length to its width is $5:4$. Wang Fang wants to cut a circular sheet of drawing paper of radius $12\,\text{cm}$ from it. Is her idea feasible?

### Solution to Exercise 9

Let the length be $5x\,\text{cm}$ and the width be $4x\,\text{cm}$. From the area,

$$
5x\cdot 4x=700,\quad 20x^2=700,\quad x^2=35.
$$

By **Basic Knowledge: Square Root**, $x=\sqrt{35}\approx 5.92$. Thus the length is $5\sqrt{35}\approx 29.6\,\text{cm}$ and the width is $4\sqrt{35}\approx 23.7\,\text{cm}$.

A circle of radius $12\,\text{cm}$ has diameter $24\,\text{cm}$. The paper is about $23.7\,\text{cm}< 24\,\text{cm}$ wide, so the diameter does not fit along the width. Thus Wang Fang's idea is not feasible. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 8

(Exercise 8.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 8) The pendulum of a pendulum clock swings freely; the time $t$ (unit: $s$) for one complete swing back and forth satisfies $t=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$ in relation to the pendulum length $l$ (unit: $m$). When the pendulum length is $0.25\,m$, how many seconds does one complete swing take? (Take $\pi$ as $3.14$, $g$ as $9.8\,m/s^2$, keep two decimal places.)

### Solution to Problem 8

Substitute $l=0.25$, $g=9.8$, $\pi=3.14$ into $t=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$.

$$
t=2\times 3.14\times\sqrt{\frac{0.25}{9.8}}=6.28\times\sqrt{0.02551\cdots}.
$$

$\sqrt{0.02551\cdots}\approx 0.1597$, so $t\approx 6.28\times 0.1597\approx 1.00$.

That is, one complete swing takes about $1.00\,s$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 9

(Exercise 8.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 9) If the area of a square is enlarged to $4$ times the original, by how many times does its side length become? What if the area is enlarged to $9$ times? What if to $n$ times?

### Solution to Problem 9

Let the original square have side length $a$ and area $a^2$.

When the area is enlarged to $4$ times the original, the new area is $4a^2$ and the new side length is $\sqrt{4a^2}=2a$, i.e. the side length becomes $2$ times the original.

When the area is enlarged to $9$ times the original, the new side length is $\sqrt{9a^2}=3a$, i.e. the side length becomes $3$ times the original.

When the area is enlarged to $n$ times the original, the new side length is $\sqrt{na^2}=a\sqrt n$, i.e. the side length becomes $\sqrt n$ times the original. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 10

(Exercise 8.1, Extension and Exploration, Problem 10)

(1) Find the values of $\left(\sqrt 0\right)^2$, $\left(\sqrt 4\right)^2$, $\left(\sqrt 9\right)^2$, $\left(\sqrt{25}\right)^2$, $\left(\sqrt{36}\right)^2$. For any nonnegative number $a$, what does $\left(\sqrt a\right)^2$ equal?

(2) Find the values of $\sqrt{0^2}$, $\sqrt{2^2}$, $\sqrt{\left(-3\right)^2}$, $\sqrt{5^2}$, $\sqrt{\left(-6\right)^2}$. For any number $a$, what does $\sqrt{a^2}$ equal?

### Solution to Problem 10

(1) $\left(\sqrt 0\right)^2=0$, $\left(\sqrt 4\right)^2=4$, $\left(\sqrt 9\right)^2=9$, $\left(\sqrt{25}\right)^2=25$, $\left(\sqrt{36}\right)^2=36$. By **Basic Knowledge: Relationship Between Squaring and Extracting Roots**, for any nonnegative number $a$,

$$
\left(\sqrt a\right)^2=a.
$$

(2) $\sqrt{0^2}=0$, $\sqrt{2^2}=2$, $\sqrt{\left(-3\right)^2}=\sqrt 9=3$, $\sqrt{5^2}=5$, $\sqrt{\left(-6\right)^2}=\sqrt{36}=6$. It can be seen that when $a\ge 0$, $\sqrt{a^2}=a$, and when $a< 0$, $\sqrt{a^2}=-a$. By **Basic Knowledge: Properties of Arithmetic Square Roots**, for any number $a$,

$$
\sqrt{a^2}=\left|a\right|.
$$

$\blacksquare$

## Problem 11

(Exercise 8.1, Extension and Exploration, Problem 11) Arbitrarily pick a positive number, say $1234$, use a calculator to take its square root, then take the square root of the resulting arithmetic square root, and so on. What do you find?

### Solution to Problem 11

By **Basic Knowledge: Size of the Arithmetic Square Root**, repeatedly take square roots of the positive number.

Repeatedly take square roots of $1234$: $\sqrt{1234}\approx 35.1$, then $\sqrt{35.1}\approx 5.92$, then $\approx 2.43$, then $\approx 1.56$, then $\approx 1.25$, … As the number of square-root extractions increases, the result gradually approaches $1$.

In general, repeatedly taking square roots of a positive number greater than $1$ gradually decreases the result toward $1$. $\blacksquare$
