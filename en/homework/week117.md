> **Definition 23.1.1** (Linear Function) <br>A function of the form $y=kx+b$ ($k$ and $b$ are constants, $k \neq 0$) is called a linear function.
>
> **Conclusion 23.2.2** (Graph and Properties of the Linear Function) <br>The graph of the linear function $y=kx+b$ is a straight line. When $k>0$, $y$ increases as $x$ increases; when $k<0$, $y$ decreases as $x$ increases.
>
> **Conclusion 23.3.3** (Linear Functions and Systems of Linear Equations in Two Variables) <br>The coordinates of the intersection of the graphs of two linear functions are precisely the solution of the corresponding system of linear equations in two variables.

### Review Problem 1

(Review, Review and Consolidation, Problem 1) Given that the graph of the direct proportional function $y=kx$ passes through the point $(-2, 4)$, then $k=\;\_\_\;$, and the graph of this function passes through the $\;\_\_\;$ quadrants.

### Solution to Review Problem 1

Substituting $(-2, 4)$ into $y=kx$ gives $4=-2k$, so $k=-2$.

By **Conclusion 23.2.2** (Graph and Properties of the Linear Function), $k=-2<0$, so the graph passes through the second and fourth quadrants. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 2

(Review, Review and Consolidation, Problem 2) If the graph of the linear function $y=(2-m)x+m-3$ passes through the origin, then $m=\;\_\_\;$.

### Solution to Review Problem 2

The graph passing through the origin means $y=0$ when $x=0$, so $m-3=0$, giving $m=3$. Then $2-m=-1 \neq 0$, which satisfies the definition of a linear function. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 3

(Review, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) The coordinates of the intersection of the graph of the linear function $y=2x+4$ with the $x$-axis are $\;\_\_\;$, and with the $y$-axis are $\;\_\_\;$.

### Solution to Review Problem 3

Setting $y=0$, $2x+4=0$, gives $x=-2$, so the intersection with the $x$-axis is $(-2, 0)$.

Setting $x=0$ gives $y=4$, so the intersection with the $y$-axis is $(0, 4)$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 4

(Review, Comprehensive Application, Problem 4) Given that the graph of a linear function passes through the points $A(1, 3)$ and $B(2, 5)$.

(1) Find the expression of this linear function;

(2) Determine whether the point $C(3, 7)$ lies on the graph of this linear function.

### Solution to Review Problem 4

(1) Let $y=kx+b$. Substituting $(1, 3)$ and $(2, 5)$ gives $k+b=3$, $2k+b=5$. Solving gives $k=2$, $b=1$. Hence $y=2x+1$.

(2) When $x=3$, $y=2\times3+1=7$, which agrees with the y-coordinate of point $C$, so point $C$ lies on the graph. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 5

(Review, Comprehensive Application, Problem 5) The graph of the linear function $y=kx+b$ is shown in the figure. Observing the graph, when $x<0$ the range of $y$ is $\;\_\_\;$.

### Solution to Review Problem 5

From the graph, the line intersects the positive half of the $y$-axis and rises from left to right. When $x=0$, $y=b>0$; when $x<0$, $y<b$. Hence the range of $y$ is $y<b$ (i.e. $y$ is less than the y-coordinate of the intersection with the $y$-axis). $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 6

(Review, Extension and Exploration, Problem 6) A store buys a product at a cost of $30$ yuan per piece. Trial sales show that the daily sales volume $p$ (pieces) of this product and the selling price per piece $x$ (yuan) satisfy $p=100-2x$. If the store wants a daily profit of $200$ yuan from selling this product, what should the selling price per piece be?

### Solution to Review Problem 6

The profit per piece is $(x-30)$ yuan, and the daily sales volume is $(100-2x)$ pieces. The daily profit is $(x-30)(100-2x)=200$.

Expanding: $100x-2x^2-3000+60x=200$, i.e. $-2x^2+160x-3200=0$, $x^2-80x+1600=0$, $(x-40)^2=0$, giving $x=40$.

Hence the selling price per piece should be set at $40$ yuan. $\blacksquare$
