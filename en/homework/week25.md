> **Basic Knowledge: Line**<br>Through two points there is one line, and only one line. In short: two points determine a line.

> **Basic Knowledge: Intersection**<br>When two distinct lines have a common point, we say the two lines intersect, and this common point is called their intersection point.

> **Basic Knowledge: Ray and Segment**<br>A point on a line and the part on one side of it is called a ray, and this point is called the endpoint of the ray. Two points on a line and the part between them is called a segment, and these two points are called the endpoints of the segment.

> **Basic Knowledge: Comparing Segments**<br>To compare the lengths of two segments, one may measure their lengths with a ruler and compare, or move one segment onto the other for comparison.

> **Basic Knowledge: Basic Fact about Segments**<br>Among all the paths connecting two points, the segment is the shortest. In short: between two points, the segment is shortest.

> **Basic Knowledge: Distance**<br>The length of the segment connecting two points is called the distance between these two points.

> **Basic Knowledge: Midpoint of a Segment**<br>Point $M$ divides segment $AB$ into two equal segments $AM$ and $MB$; point $M$ is called the midpoint of segment $AB$.

> **Basic Knowledge: Segment**<br>The part of a line between two points; it has two endpoints.

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Basic Knowledge: Midpoint of a Segment**, $C$ is the midpoint of $AB$, so

$$CD=CB-DB=4-1.5=2.5\ \text{cm}.$$

## Exercise 5

(Exercise 6.2, Problem 2) Point $C$ lies on segment $AB$, and points $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of segments $AC$ and $BC$ respectively. If $MN=6$, find the length of segment $AB$.

### Solution to Exercise 5

By **Basic Knowledge: Midpoint of a Segment**, $M$ is the midpoint of $AC$, so $MC=\frac{1}{2}AC$; $N$ is the midpoint of $BC$, so $CN=\frac{1}{2}BC$.

$$MN=MC+CN=\frac{1}{2}AC+\frac{1}{2}BC=\frac{1}{2}\left(AC+BC\right)=\frac{1}{2}AB.$$

Since $MN=6$, we have $AB=2\times6=12$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 6

(Exercise 6.2, Problem 3) As shown, points $B$ and $C$ divide segment $AD$ into three parts in the ratio $2\!:\!3\!:\!4$, $M$ is the midpoint of $AD$, and $CD=8$. Find the length of $MC$.

### Solution to Exercise 6

By **Basic Knowledge: Midpoint of a Segment**, from the ratio let $AB=2k$, $BC=3k$, $CD=4k$.

Since $CD=8$, we have $4k=8$, $k=2$. Thus $AD=2k+3k+4k=9k=18$.

$M$ is the midpoint of $AD$, so $MD=\frac{1}{2}AD=9$.

$$MC=MD-CD=9-8=1.$$

$\blacksquare$

(1) Find the length of $AC$;

### Solution to Problem 5

$$BM=AB-AM=4-3=1\ \text{cm}.$$

### Solution to Problem 6

If $C$ lies on segment $AB$: $AC=AB-BC=8-3=5\ \text{cm}$.

If $C$ lies on the extension of $AB$: $AC=AB+BC=8+3=11\ \text{cm}$.

## Problem 7

(Problem 6.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 7) As shown, point $C$ lies on segment $AB$, $AC=8\ \text{cm}$, $CB=6\ \text{cm}$, and points $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of $AC$ and $BC$ respectively.

(1) Find the length of segment $MN$;

(2) If $C$ is any point on segment $AB$ satisfying $AC+CB=a\ \text{cm}$, with other conditions unchanged, can you conjecture the length of $MN$? And explain why.

### Solution to Problem 7

By **Basic Knowledge: Midpoint of a Segment**.

(1) $M$ is the midpoint of $AC$, so $MC=\frac{1}{2}AC=4\ \text{cm}$. $N$ is the midpoint of $BC$, so $CN=\frac{1}{2}CB=3\ \text{cm}$.

$$MN=MC+CN=4+3=7\ \text{cm}.$$

(2) If $AC+CB=a$, then

$$MN=\frac{1}{2}AC+\frac{1}{2}CB=\frac{1}{2}\left(AC+CB\right)=\frac{1}{2}a.$$

Hence we conjecture $MN=\frac{1}{2}a\ \text{cm}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 8

(Problem 6.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 8) As shown, given points $A$, $B$, $C$ on the same line, $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of segments $AC$ and $BC$ respectively.

(1) If $AC=8\ \text{cm}$ and $BC=6\ \text{cm}$, find the length of $MN$;

(2) If $AB=10\ \text{cm}$, find the length of $MN$.

### Solution to Problem 8

By **Basic Knowledge: Midpoint of a Segment**.

(1) $M$ is the midpoint of $AC$, $MC=\frac{1}{2}AC=4\ \text{cm}$. $N$ is the midpoint of $BC$, $CN=\frac{1}{2}BC=3\ \text{cm}$. Since $A$, $B$, $C$ are collinear and $B$ lies between $A$ and $C$,

$$MN=MC-CN=4-3=1\ \text{cm}.$$

(2) Regardless of whether $B$ lies between $A$ and $C$ or $C$ lies between $A$ and $B$, $MN=\frac{1}{2}AB=\frac{1}{2}\times10=5\ \text{cm}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 9

(Problem 6.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 9) As shown, extend segment $AB$ to $C$ so that $BC=\frac{1}{2}AB$, then extend $BA$ to $D$ so that $AD=\frac{1}{2}AB$. If $DC=20\ \text{cm}$, find the length of $AB$.

### Solution to Problem 9

By **Basic Knowledge: Segment**.

Let $AB=x$; then $BC=\frac{1}{2}x$ and $AD=\frac{1}{2}x$.

$$DC=AD+AB+BC=\frac{1}{2}x+x+\frac{1}{2}x=2x.$$

Since $DC=20$, we have $2x=20$, $x=10$. That is, $AB=10\ \text{cm}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 10

(Problem 6.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 10) As shown, point $C$ lies on the extension of segment $AB$, and points $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of segments $AC$ and $BC$ respectively. If $MN=5\ \text{cm}$, find the length of segment $AB$.

### Solution to Problem 10

By **Basic Knowledge: Midpoint of a Segment**.

$M$ is the midpoint of $AC$, so $MC=\frac{1}{2}AC$. $N$ is the midpoint of $BC$, so $NC=\frac{1}{2}BC$. Since $C$ lies on the extension of $AB$,

$$MN=MC-NC=\frac{1}{2}AC-\frac{1}{2}BC=\frac{1}{2}\left(AC-BC\right)=\frac{1}{2}AB.$$

Since $MN=5$, we have $AB=2\times5=10\ \text{cm}$. $\blacksquare$

## Reading and Thinking 1

(Reading and Thinking: Measuring Length) Why must units of length be unified?

### Solution to Reading and Thinking 1

By **Basic Knowledge: Distance**, measuring length requires unified units.

Without unified units of length, different regions and different groups would use their own measurement standards, causing confusion in communication and trade. Unified units of length make measurement results comparable and consistent, and are the foundation of scientific research, production, and daily life. $\blacksquare$
