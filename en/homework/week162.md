> **Basic Knowledge: Similar Figures**<br>Figures with the same shape are called similar figures; two figures are similar if one can be obtained from the other by enlarging or reducing it.
> **Basic Knowledge: Similar Polygons**<br>Two polygons with the same number of sides are similar if corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are proportional.

> **Basic Knowledge: Proportional Line Segments** <br>For four line segments $a,b,c,d$, if $\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}$ (i.e. $ad=bc$), then the four line segments are said to be proportional.

> **Basic Knowledge: Similar Polygons** <br>Two polygons with the same number of sides are called similar polygons if their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional; the ratio of corresponding sides of similar polygons is called the similarity ratio. The corresponding angles of similar polygons are equal, and their corresponding sides are proportional.

## Exercise 1

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Basic Knowledge: Similar Figures**, the triangular ruler under the magnifying glass and the original triangular ruler have the same shape, differing only in size, and one can be regarded as obtained by enlarging the other.

Therefore they are similar. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 3

(Exercise 27.1, Problem 1) On a map with scale $1:10\,000\,000$, the distance between Place A and Place B is measured to be $30\ \text{cm}$. Find the actual distance between the two places.

### Solution to Exercise 3

The scale $=$ the map distance $:$ the actual distance, i.e. $1:10\,000\,000=30\ \text{cm}:$ the actual distance.

The actual distance $=30\times10\,000\,000=3\times10^8\ \text{cm}=3000\ \text{km}$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 4

(Exercise 27.1, Problem 2) Are the two triangles shown in the figure similar? Why? (The left triangle has two legs both of length $5$, and the right triangle has two legs both of length $10$; both are isosceles right triangles.)

### Solution to Exercise 4

By **Basic Knowledge: Similar Polygons**, two triangles are similar if their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional.

Both triangles are isosceles right triangles, with three angles $90^\circ,45^\circ,45^\circ$, so the corresponding angles are equal. The side ratios are $5:5:5\sqrt{2}$ and $10:10:10\sqrt{2}$, and the ratio of corresponding sides is $1:2$, so the sides are proportional.

Therefore the two triangles are similar. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 5

(Exercise 27.1, Problem 3) The two pentagons shown in the figure are similar. Find the values of $a,b,c,d$. (The side lengths of the left pentagon are, in order, $3,5,2,c,d$; the corresponding side lengths of the right pentagon are, in order, $b,7.5,a,6,9$.)

### Solution to Exercise 5

By **Basic Knowledge: Similar Polygons**, the corresponding sides are proportional.

The ratio of corresponding sides is $\frac{5}{7.5}=\frac{2}{3}$.

$$
\frac{3}{b}=\frac{2}{3}\Rightarrow b=4.5,\quad
\frac{2}{a}=\frac{2}{3}\Rightarrow a=3,
$$

$$
\frac{c}{6}=\frac{2}{3}\Rightarrow c=4,\quad
\frac{d}{9}=\frac{2}{3}\Rightarrow d=6.
$$

Thus $a=3$, $b=4.5$, $c=4$, $d=6$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 2

(Problem 27.1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 2) Are any two rectangles similar? Why?

### Solution to Problem 2

By **Basic Knowledge: Similar Polygons**, similar polygons require both that the corresponding angles are equal and that the sides are proportional.

All angles of a rectangle are $90^\circ$, so the corresponding angles are equal, but the sides are not necessarily proportional. For example, a square with sides $1\times1$ and a rectangle $2\times1$ both have angles of $90^\circ$, but $1:2\ne1:1$, so the sides are not proportional.

Therefore any two rectangles are not necessarily similar. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 3

(Problem 27.1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) As shown in the figure, $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DEF$ are similar. Find the values of $x,y$. (In $\triangle ABC$, $AB=7$, $BC=8$; in $\triangle DEF$, $FE=4$, $DF=12$, $DE=x$.)

### Solution to Problem 3

By **Basic Knowledge: Similar Polygons**, $\triangle ABC\backsim\triangle DEF$, and the corresponding sides are proportional.

The similarity ratio is $\frac{BC}{FE}=\frac{8}{4}=2$.

$$
\frac{AB}{DE}=2\Rightarrow\frac{7}{x}=2\Rightarrow x=3.5.
$$

$$
\frac{AC}{DF}=2\Rightarrow AC=2\times12=24.
$$

Thus $x=3.5$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 4

(Problem 27.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 4) Try to draw on grid paper a figure similar to the original figure. What method did you use?

The figure shows on grid paper an original figure (a polygon made of several line segments) and its similar figure enlarged by the same factor; the two figures are drawn side by side on the grid paper, with corresponding sides parallel and corresponding angles equal.

### Solution to Problem 4

By **Basic Knowledge: Similar Polygons**, enlarge or reduce each side of the original figure by the same factor, keeping the angles unchanged, to obtain a similar figure. Specifically, count the number of grid squares occupied by each side of the original figure on the grid paper, multiply by the same factor, and draw proportionally, keeping the directions of the angles unchanged. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 5

(Problem 27.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 5) As shown in the figure, $DE\parallel BC$. Given $AD=2$, $DB=4$, $AE=2.5$, $EC=5$, $DE=3$, $BC=9$. (1) Find the values of $\frac{AD}{AB}$, $\frac{AE}{AC}$, $\frac{DE}{BC}$; (2) Prove that $\triangle ADE$ and $\triangle ABC$ are similar.

### Solution to Problem 5

(1) $AB=AD+DB=2+4=6$, $AC=AE+EC=2.5+5=7.5$.

$$
\frac{AD}{AB}=\frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3},\quad
\frac{AE}{AC}=\frac{2.5}{7.5}=\frac{1}{3},\quad
\frac{DE}{BC}=\frac{3}{9}=\frac{1}{3}.
$$

(2) $DE\parallel BC$, so $\angle ADE=\angle B$, $\angle AED=\angle C$, and $\angle A$ is common; thus the corresponding angles are equal; also from (1), the ratios of the three sides are all $\frac{1}{3}$, so the sides are proportional. By **Basic Knowledge**, $\triangle ADE\backsim\triangle ABC$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 6

(Problem 27.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) A rectangular lawn is $30\ \text{m}$ long and $20\ \text{m}$ wide. Around the lawn there is a $1\ \text{m}$ wide circular path. Are the two rectangles formed by the inner and outer edges of the path similar? Give your reason.

### Solution to Problem 6

The inner-edge rectangle has dimensions $30\times20$, and the outer-edge rectangle has dimensions $32\times22$.

$$
\frac{30}{32}=\frac{15}{16},\quad\frac{20}{22}=\frac{10}{11}.
$$

$\frac{15}{16}\ne\frac{10}{11}$, so the sides are not proportional.

By **Basic Knowledge**, the two rectangles are not similar. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 7

(Problem 27.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) If two polygons have only their corresponding angles equal, are they similar? What if only their sides are proportional? If they are not necessarily similar, give a counterexample.

### Solution to Problem 7

By **Basic Knowledge: Similar Polygons**, both equal corresponding angles and proportional sides are required; neither is dispensable.

Having only equal angles does not necessarily imply similarity: for example, a square and a rectangle both have angles of $90^\circ$, but their sides are not proportional.

Having only proportional sides does not necessarily imply similarity: for example, a square and a rhombus may have the same side-length ratio, but their interior angles are not necessarily correspondingly equal. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 8

(Problem 27.1, Extension and Exploration, Problem 8) Fold a rectangular sheet of paper along the line connecting the midpoints of its longer sides. If the two resulting rectangles are both similar to the original rectangle, what is the ratio of length to width of the original rectangle? If this paper is folded repeatedly in this way, are the resulting rectangles all similar?

The figure shows a rectangular sheet of paper folded along the line connecting the midpoints of its longer sides, producing a smaller rectangle; the original rectangle has length $a$ and width $b$, and the folded rectangle has sides $b$ and $\frac a2$.

### Solution to Problem 8

Let the length of the original rectangle be $a$ and its width be $b$ ($a> b$). After folding along the midpoint line of the longer side, the new rectangle has dimensions $b\times\frac{a}{2}$.

Since the folded rectangle is similar to the original, the corresponding sides are proportional:

$$
\frac{a}{b}=\frac{b}{a/2}=\frac{2b}{a}.
$$

i.e. $a^2=2b^2$, and $\frac{a}{b}=\sqrt{2}$.

Each fold splits a rectangle with long-side ratio $\sqrt{2}:1$ by the same ratio, and the new rectangle still has length-to-width ratio $\sqrt{2}$; hence the rectangles obtained by continuing to fold are all similar to the original rectangle. $\blacksquare$
