> **Definition 15.1.1** (Axisymmetric Figure) <br>If a plane figure can be folded along a line so that the parts on both sides of the line coincide with each other, the figure is called an axisymmetric figure, and the line is called its axis of symmetry; the points that coincide after folding are called symmetric points. If folding one figure along a line makes it coincide with another figure, the two figures are said to be symmetric about that line.
> 
> **Conclusion 15.1.1** (Properties of Axis Symmetry) <br>Two figures symmetric about a line are congruent; the axis of symmetry is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining any pair of symmetric points, i.e. the segment joining symmetric points is perpendicularly bisected by the axis of symmetry.
> 
> **Definition 15.1.2** (Perpendicular Bisector of a Segment) <br>The line passing through the midpoint of a segment and perpendicular to it is called the perpendicular bisector of the segment.
> 
> **Conclusion 15.1.2** (Properties of the Perpendicular Bisector) <br>A point on the perpendicular bisector of a segment is equidistant from the two endpoints of the segment.
> 
> **Conclusion 15.1.3** (Determination of the Perpendicular Bisector) <br>A point equidistant from the two endpoints of a segment lies on the perpendicular bisector of the segment. The perpendicular bisector of a segment can be regarded as the set of all points equidistant from the two endpoints.
> 
> **Definition 15.1.3** (Converse Propositions) <br>Two propositions whose hypothesis and conclusion are exactly reversed are called converse propositions; if the converse proposition of a theorem is proved to be true, then the two are converse theorems of each other.

### Exercise 4

(Exercise 15.1, Problem 1) As shown, $AD\perp BC$, $BD=DC$, and point $C$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AE$. What is the relationship among the lengths of $AB$, $AC$, $CE$? What is the relationship between $AB+BD$ and $DE$?

### Solution to Exercise 4

Since $AD\perp BC$ and $BD=DC$, line $AD$ perpendicularly bisects $BC$. Point $A$ lies on $AD$; by **Conclusion 15.1.2** (Properties of the Perpendicular Bisector), $AB=AC$.

Since point $C$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AE$, similarly by **Conclusion 15.1.2**, $AC=CE$. Hence $AB=AC=CE$.

Also $BD=DC$, so

$$
AB+BD=CE+DC=DE.
$$

$\blacksquare$

### Exercise 6

(Exercise 15.1, Problem 3) Write the converse proposition of each of the following propositions, and determine whether these converse propositions hold.

(1) If two lines are parallel, corresponding angles are equal;

(2) if two real numbers are equal, then their absolute values are equal;

(3) corresponding angles of congruent triangles are equal.

### Solution to Exercise 6

(1) Converse proposition: if corresponding angles are equal, then two lines are parallel. Holds.

(2) Converse proposition: if the absolute values of two real numbers are equal, then the two real numbers are equal. Does not hold; for example, the absolute values of $5$ and $-5$ are equal but the numbers themselves are not.

(3) Converse proposition: two triangles with corresponding angles equal are congruent. Does not hold; triangles with three respectively equal angles have the same shape but not necessarily the same size. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 4

(Exercise 15.1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, $DE$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AC$, $AE=3$, and the perimeter of $\triangle ABD$ is $13$. Find the perimeter of $\triangle ABC$.

### Solution to Problem 4

Since $DE$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AC$, $AC=2AE=6$, and point $D$ lies on $DE$; by **Conclusion 15.1.2** (Properties of the Perpendicular Bisector), $DA=DC$.

The perimeter of $\triangle ABD$ is $AB+BD+AD=AB+BD+DC=AB+BC=13$. Hence

$$
C_{\triangle ABC}=AB+BC+AC=13+6=19.
$$

$\blacksquare$

### Solution to Problem 11

If the lines of the corresponding segments $AB$ and $A'B'$ intersect, the intersection point lies on the axis of symmetry $l$; if they are parallel, then the lines of this pair of corresponding segments are parallel to the axis of symmetry $l$. The rule is: for two figures symmetric about a line, if corresponding segments intersect, their intersection point must lie on the axis of symmetry. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 13

(Exercise 15.1, Extension and Exploration, Problem 13) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, the perpendicular bisectors of sides $AB$, $BC$ intersect at point $P$.

(1) Prove that $PA=PB=PC$;

(2) Does point $P$ also lie on the perpendicular bisector of side $AC$? What conclusion can be drawn from this?

### Solution to Problem 13

(1) Since $P$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, by **Conclusion 15.1.2** (Properties of the Perpendicular Bisector), $PA=PB$. Since $P$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, similarly $PB=PC$. Hence $PA=PB=PC$.

(2) From $PA=PC$, point $P$ is equidistant from $A$, $C$; by **Conclusion 15.1.3** (Determination of the Perpendicular Bisector), point $P$ also lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AC$. This yields: the perpendicular bisectors of the three sides of a triangle intersect at one point, which is equidistant from the three vertices. $\blacksquare$
