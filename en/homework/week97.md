> **Rule 19.2.2** (Multiplication in Reverse) <br> \(\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt{a}\cdot\sqrt{b}\) (\(a\ge 0,\ b\ge 0\)).
>
> **Rule 19.2.4** (Division in Reverse) <br> \(\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}}=\frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}\) (\(a\ge 0,\ b>0\)).

### Activity 1

(Mathematics Activity, Activity 1, Problem 1)

Books and paper have fixed standard lengths and widths. Use a calculator to find the ratio of length to width for each paper size in Table 1 and Table 2. What do you observe? What is the relationship among the length-to-width ratios of the various paper sizes?

Calculate by dividing the length of each sheet by its width:

$$
\frac{210}{148}\approx 1.419,\quad \frac{297}{210}\approx 1.414,\quad \frac{420}{297}\approx 1.414,
$$

$$
\frac{594}{420}\approx 1.414,\quad \frac{841}{594}\approx 1.416.
$$

Similarly, for type B paper, \(\frac{257}{182}\approx 1.412\), \(\frac{364}{257}\approx 1.416\), and so on, are all approximately \(1.414\).

By **Rule 19.2.4**, \(\sqrt{2}\approx 1.414\).

It can be seen that the length-to-width ratio of each paper size is approximately \(\sqrt{2}\); that is, they are all rectangles of the same shape, merely scaled proportionally.

\(\blacksquare\)

(Mathematics Activity, Activity 1, Problem 2)

As shown in Figure 1, the length-to-width ratio of the rectangular sheet \(ABCD\) is \(\sqrt{2}\).

(1) As shown in Figure 2, if \(E,F\) are the midpoints of the long sides \(AD,BC\), respectively, and the sheet \(ABCD\) is folded along the line \(EF\), is the resulting rectangle \(ABFE\) still "a rectangle whose length-to-width ratio is \(\sqrt{2}\)"? Why?

(2) If the sheet \(ABCD\) is folded as shown in Figure 3, is the rectangle \(GHID\) still "a rectangle whose length-to-width ratio is \(\sqrt{2}\)"? Why?

![week97-1.svg](../../figures/week97-1.svg){width=100%}

(1) Let the width of the original rectangle be \(a\) and its length be \(b\); by the problem, \(\frac{b}{a}=\sqrt{2}\).

After folding, the width of rectangle \(ABFE\) is the original width \(a\), and its length is half the original length, \(\frac{b}{2}\).

Hence the length-to-width ratio of the new rectangle is

$$
\frac{a}{b/2}=\frac{2a}{b}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}=\sqrt{2}.
$$

Therefore \(ABFE\) is still a rectangle whose length-to-width ratio is \(\sqrt{2}\).

(2) After folding as in Figure 3, one side of the new rectangle \(GHID\) is still the original width \(a\), and the other side is obtained by subtracting \(a\) from the original length \(b\), i.e. \(b-a\).

From \(\frac{b}{a}=\sqrt{2}\), we get \(b=\sqrt{2}a\), so the other side is \((\sqrt{2}-1)a\).

Hence

$$
\frac{a}{b-a}=\frac{a}{\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}-1}=\sqrt{2}+1.
$$

It is not equal to \(\sqrt{2}\), so \(GHID\) is not a rectangle whose length-to-width ratio is \(\sqrt{2}\).

\(\blacksquare\)
