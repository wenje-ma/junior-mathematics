> **Definition 17.2.1** (Method Using the Difference of Squares Formula) <br>$a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)$, i.e. the difference of the squares of two numbers equals the product of the sum of the two numbers and the difference of the two numbers.

> **Definition 17.2.2** (Perfect Square Trinomial) <br>Expressions such as $a^2+2ab+b^2$ and $a^2-2ab+b^2$ are called perfect square trinomials.

> **Definition 17.2.3** (Method Using the Perfect Square Formula) <br>$a^2+2ab+b^2=(a+b)^2$, $a^2-2ab+b^2=(a-b)^2$.

> **Definition 17.2.4** (Method of Using Formulas) <br>Swap the two sides of a multiplication formula, and use the formula to factor a polynomial. Factoring should be carried on until each polynomial factor can no longer be factored.

### Exercise 1

(Exercise 17.2, Problem 1) Can the following polynomials be factored using the difference of squares formula? Why?

(1) $x^2+y^2$;

(2) $x^2-y^2$;

(3) $-x^2+y^2$;

(4) $-x^2-y^2$.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 17.2.1**, the difference of squares formula requires the two terms to have opposite signs and both be squares.

(1) No; the two terms have the same sign.

(2) Yes; $x^2-y^2=(x+y)(x-y)$.

(3) Yes; $-x^2+y^2=y^2-x^2=(y+x)(y-x)$.

(4) No; after factoring out the minus sign it is $-(x^2+y^2)$, with the two terms having the same sign. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 2

(Exercise 17.2, Problem 2) Factor:

(1) $36-m^2$;

(2) $49n^2-1$;

(3) $a^2-\frac{1}{25}b^2$;

(4) $81a^2-16b^4$;

(5) $4b^2-(b+c)^2$;

(6) $(m+n)^2-(m-2n)^2$.

### Solution to Exercise 2

By **Definition 17.2.1**.

(1) $36-m^2=(6+m)(6-m)$.

(2) $49n^2-1=(7n+1)(7n-1)$.

(3) $a^2-\frac{1}{25}b^2=(a+\frac15 b)(a-\frac15 b)$.

(4) $81a^2-16b^4=(9a+4b^2)(9a-4b^2)$.

(5) $4b^2-(b+c)^2=[2b+(b+c)][2b-(b+c)]=(3b+c)(b-c)$.

(6) $(m+n)^2-(m-2n)^2=[(m+n)+(m-2n)][(m+n)-(m-2n)]=(2m-n)(3n)$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 3

(Exercise 17.2, Problem 1) Are the following polynomials perfect square trinomials? Why?

(1) $a^2-4a+4$;

(2) $1+4a^2$;

(3) $4b^2+4b-1$;

(4) $a^2+ab+b^2$.

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Definition 17.2.2**, a perfect square trinomial must be the sum of two squares plus or minus $2$ times their product.

(1) Yes; $a^2-2\cdot a\cdot2+2^2=(a-2)^2$.

(2) No; the middle term equal to $2$ times the product is missing.

(3) No; the constant term should be a positive square number, and the signs do not match.

(4) No; $ab$ should be $2ab$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 4

(Exercise 17.2, Problem 2) Factor:

(1) $a^2+2a+1$;

(2) $x^2-12x+36$;

(3) $4x^2-4x+1$;

(4) $4p^2+12pq+9q^2$;

(5) $(x+y)^2-10(x+y)+25$;

(6) $-2xy-x^2-y^2$.

### Solution to Exercise 4

By **Definition 17.2.3**.

(1) $a^2+2a+1=(a+1)^2$.

(2) $x^2-12x+36=(x-6)^2$.

### Exercise 5

(Exercise 17.2, Problem 1) Factor:

(1) $x^2 y-4y$;

(2) $a^3-2a^2+a$;

(3) $ax^2+2a^2 x+a^3$;

(4) $-a^4+16$;

(5) $3a-6ax+3ax^2$;

(6) $-4bx^2+8bxy-4by^2$.

### Solution to Exercise 5

First extract the common factor, then use the formula; factor until no further factorization is possible.

(1) $x^2 y-4y=y(x^2-4)=y(x+2)(x-2)$.

(2) $a^3-2a^2+a=a(a^2-2a+1)=a(a-1)^2$.

(3) $ax^2+2a^2 x+a^3=a(x^2+2ax+a^2)=a(x+a)^2$.

(4) $-a^4+16=16-a^4=(4+a^2)(4-a^2)=(4+a^2)(2+a)(2-a)$.

(5) $3a-6ax+3ax^2=3a(1-2x+x^2)=3a(x-1)^2$.

(6) $-4bx^2+8bxy-4by^2=-4b(x^2-2xy+y^2)=-4b(x-y)^2$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 6

(Exercise 17.2, Problem 2) Factor:

(1) $(a-b)^2+4ab$;

(2) $(p-4)(p+1)+3p$.

### Solution to Exercise 6

(1) $(a-b)^2+4ab=a^2-2ab+b^2+4ab=a^2+2ab+b^2=(a+b)^2$.

(2) $(p-4)(p+1)+3p=p^2-3p-4+3p=p^2-4=(p+2)(p-2)$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 2

(Exercise 17.2, Review and Consolidation, Problem 2) Factor:

(1) $a^2-4ab+4b^2$;

(2) $-x^2+10xy-25y^2$;

(3) $4+12(x-y)+9(x-y)^2$;

(4) $-(m+n)^2+4(m+n)-4$.

### Solution to Problem 2

(1) $a^2-4ab+4b^2=(a-2b)^2$.

### Problem 3

(Exercise 17.2, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) Factor:

(1) $18y^3-27y^4-3y^2$;

(2) $m^4-18m^2+81$;

(3) $x^4-16y^4$;

(4) $(a^2+b^2-c^2)^2-(a^2-b^2-c^2)^2$.

### Solution to Problem 3

Comprehensively use the method of extracting the common factor and the method of using formulas.

(1) $18y^3-27y^4-3y^2=-3y^2(9y^2-6y+1)=-3y^2(3y-1)^2$.

(2) $m^4-18m^2+81=(m^2-9)^2=(m+3)^2(m-3)^2$.

(3) $x^4-16y^4=(x^2+4y^2)(x^2-4y^2)=(x^2+4y^2)(x+2y)(x-2y)$.

(4) Let $A=a^2-c^2$; the original expression $=(A+b^2)^2-(A-b^2)^2=(2A)(2b^2)=4b^2(a^2-c^2)=4b^2(a+c)(a-c)$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 4

(Exercise 17.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 4) Calculate using factoring:

(1) $103^2+103\times194+97^2$;

(2) $2021^2-2020^2+2010^2-2009^2$.

### Solution to Problem 4

### Problem 5

(Exercise 17.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 5) Given $xy=4$, $x+y=5$, find $x^3 y+2x^2 y^2+xy^3$.

### Solution to Problem 5

$$x^3 y+2x^2 y^2+xy^3=xy(x^2+2xy+y^2)=xy(x+y)^2.$$

Substituting $xy=4$, $x+y=5$, we get $4\times25=100$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 6

(Exercise 17.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) Factor:

(1) $(2ab+1)^2-a^4 b^4$;

(2) $(p+q)^2-6(p^2-q^2)+9(p-q)^2$.

### Solution to Problem 6

(1) $(2ab+1)^2-(a^2 b^2)^2=[(2ab+1)+a^2 b^2][(2ab+1)-a^2 b^2]=(ab+1)^2[-(ab-1)^2+2]$. Note that $2ab+1-a^2 b^2=1+2ab-a^2 b^2$; over the real numbers it suffices to keep the original expression, so the result is $(2ab+1+a^2 b^2)(2ab+1-a^2 b^2)=(ab+1)^2(2ab+1-a^2 b^2)$.

(2) Note that $p^2-q^2=(p+q)(p-q)$; let $A=p+q$, $B=p-q$; the original expression $=A^2-6AB+9B^2=(A-3B)^2=[(p+q)-3(p-q)]^2=(-2p+4q)^2=4(2q-p)^2$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 7

(Exercise 17.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 7) Given that $n$ is a positive integer, prove that $(4n+3)^2-(2n+3)^2$ is divisible by $24$.

### Solution to Problem 7

By the difference of squares formula:

$$(4n+3)^2-(2n+3)^2=[(4n+3)+(2n+3)][(4n+3)-(2n+3)]=(6n+6)(2n)=12n(n+1).$$

$n$ and $n+1$ are consecutive integers, so one of them must be even; hence $n(n+1)=2k$, and the original expression $=12\cdot2k=24k$. Therefore it is divisible by $24$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 8

(Exercise 17.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 8) Given that $4y^2+my+9$ is a perfect square trinomial, find the value of $m$.

### Solution to Problem 8

$4y^2=(2y)^2$, $9=3^2$. The middle term of a perfect square trinomial should be $\pm2\cdot2y\cdot3=\pm12y$. Hence $m=\pm12$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 9

(Exercise 17.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 9) Observe the following expressions. What conclusion can you draw? Can you prove your conclusion?

$1^2+1^2\times2^2+2^2=(1+1+1)^2$,

$2^2+2^2\times3^2+3^2=(4+2+1)^2$,

$3^2+3^2\times4^2+4^2=(9+3+1)^2$,

$\cdots\cdots$

### Solution to Problem 9

Conjecture: $n^2+n^2(n+1)^2+(n+1)^2=[n^2+n+1]^2$.

Proof: the left side $=n^2+(n+1)^2+n^2(n+1)^2$. Expand the right side:

$$(n^2+n+1)^2=n^4+n^2+1+2n^3+2n^2+2n=n^4+2n^3+3n^2+2n+1.$$

Expanding the left side: $n^2+n^2+2n+1+n^2(n^2+2n+1)=2n^2+2n+1+n^4+2n^3+n^2=n^4+2n^3+3n^2+2n+1$. The two sides are equal, so the conjecture holds. $\blacksquare$

### Reading and Thinking 1

(Reading and Thinking, Factoring of the form $x^2+(p+q)x+pq$) Use $x^2+(p+q)x+pq=(x+p)(x+q)$ to factor:

(1) $x^2+7x+10$;

(2) $x^2-2x-8$;

(3) $y^2-7y+12$;

(4) $x^2+7x-18$.

### Solution to Reading and Thinking 1

By $x^2+(p+q)x+pq=(x+p)(x+q)$, find two numbers whose sum is the coefficient of the linear term and whose product is the constant term.

(1) $10=2\times5$, $2+5=7$, hence $x^2+7x+10=(x+2)(x+5)$.

(2) $-8=2\times(-4)$, $2+(-4)=-2$, hence $x^2-2x-8=(x+2)(x-4)$.

(3) $12=(-3)\times(-4)$, $-3+(-4)=-7$, hence $y^2-7y+12=(y-3)(y-4)$.

(4) $-18=9\times(-2)$, $9+(-2)=7$, hence $x^2+7x-18=(x+9)(x-2)$. $\blacksquare$
