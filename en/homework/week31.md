> **Basic Knowledge: Parallel**<br>In the same plane, two lines that do not intersect are called parallel lines, denoted $a\parallel b$. In the same plane, two distinct lines have only two possible positional relationships: intersecting and parallel.

> **Basic Knowledge: Transitivity of Parallelism**<br>If two lines are both parallel to a third line, then these two lines are also parallel to each other. That is, if $b\parallel a$, $c\parallel a$, then $b\parallel c$.

> **Basic Knowledge: Criterion 2**<br>If two lines are cut by a third line and the alternate interior angles are equal, then the two lines are parallel. In short: equal alternate interior angles imply parallel lines.

> **Basic Knowledge: Criterion 3**<br>If two lines are cut by a third line and the consecutive interior angles are supplementary, then the two lines are parallel. In short: supplementary consecutive interior angles imply parallel lines.


> **Basic Knowledge: Parallel Axiom**<br>Through a point outside a given line, there is exactly one line parallel to the given line.
> **Basic Knowledge: Criterion 1**<br>When two lines are cut by a transversal, if the corresponding angles are equal, then the two lines are parallel. In short: corresponding angles equal, two lines parallel.
> **Basic Knowledge: Perpendicular**<br>Two lines are perpendicular if one of the four angles formed at their intersection is a right angle.
> **Basic Knowledge: Property 1**<br>Property 1.
> **Basic Knowledge: Property 2**<br>Property 2.
> **Basic Knowledge: Property 3**<br>Property 3.
> **Basic Knowledge: Vertical Angles Are Equal**<br>Vertical angles are equal.

(1) If $\angle B=\angle DCG$, which two lines can be judged to be parallel? Why?

(2) If $\angle D=\angle DCG$, which two lines can be judged to be parallel? Why?

(3) If $\angle D+\angle DFE=180^\circ$, which two lines can be judged to be parallel? Why?

### Solution to Exercise 2

## Exercise 6

(Exercise 7.2, Problem 1) As shown in the figure, line $a\parallel b$, $\angle 1=54^\circ$. How many degrees are $\angle 2$, $\angle 3$, and $\angle 4$?

The figure shows two parallel lines $a$, $b$ cut by a transversal: at the intersection of $a$ and the transversal are marked $\angle 1$ and its vertical angle $\angle 2$; at the intersection of $b$ and the transversal are marked $\angle 3$ (alternate interior to $\angle 1$) and $\angle 4$ (consecutive interior to $\angle 1$).

### Solution to Exercise 6

By **Basic Knowledge: Property 1**, **Basic Knowledge: Property 2**, and **Basic Knowledge: Property 3**, the relationships among the corresponding angles when two lines are parallel are as follows.

$\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$ are vertical angles; by **Basic Knowledge: Vertical Angles Are Equal**, $\angle 2=\angle 1=54^\circ$.

$\angle 1$ and $\angle 3$ are the alternate interior angles formed when lines $a$, $b$ are cut by a transversal; by **Basic Knowledge: Property 2**, parallel lines give equal alternate interior angles, so $\angle 3=\angle 1=54^\circ$.

$\angle 1$ and $\angle 4$ are consecutive interior angles; by **Basic Knowledge: Property 3**, parallel lines give supplementary consecutive interior angles, so $\angle 4=180^\circ-\angle 1=180^\circ-54^\circ=126^\circ$.

Thus $\angle 2=54^\circ$, $\angle 3=54^\circ$, $\angle 4=126^\circ$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 8

(Exercise 7.2, Problem 3) Place a right-angle set square and a strip of paper with parallel sides as shown in the figure. Then which of the following conclusions is correct? _______ (fill in the numbers).

(1) $\angle 1=\angle 2$;　(2) $\angle 4+\angle 5=180^\circ$;　(3) $\angle 1+\angle 4=90^\circ$;　(4) $\angle 4+90^\circ=\angle 3$.

### Solution to Exercise 8

The two sides of the strip are parallel. The right angle of the set square is $90^\circ$.

$\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$ are the corresponding angles formed when the two parallel sides are cut by one edge of the set square; by **Basic Knowledge: Property 1**, $\angle 1=\angle 2$, so (1) is correct.

$\angle 4$ and $\angle 5$ are the consecutive interior angles formed when the two parallel sides are cut by the other edge of the set square; by **Basic Knowledge: Property 3**, parallel lines give supplementary consecutive interior angles, so $\angle 4+\angle 5=180^\circ$, and (2) is correct.

The right angle of the set square together with $\angle 1$ and $\angle 4$ forms a straight angle, i.e. $\angle 1+90^\circ+\angle 4=180^\circ$, so $\angle 1+\angle 4=90^\circ$, and (3) is correct.

The adjacent supplementary angle of $\angle 3$ and $\angle 4$ forms a corresponding-angle relationship, giving $\angle 3=180^\circ-\angle 4$, i.e. $\angle 4+\angle 3=180^\circ$, which does not agree with $\angle 4+90^\circ=\angle 3$, so (4) is wrong.

In summary, the correct ones are (1)(2)(3). $\blacksquare$

(Exercise 7.2, Problem 1) As shown in the figure, if line $a\parallel b$ and $\angle 1+\angle 2=180^\circ$, then are lines $b$ and $c$ parallel? Why?

### Solution to Exercise 9

Line $b\parallel c$.

Since $a\parallel b$, the vertical angle (or corresponding angle) of $\angle 1$ equals the corresponding angle formed when $a$, $b$ are cut. $\angle 1+\angle 2=180^\circ$, and the adjacent supplementary angle of $\angle 1$ is also supplementary to $\angle 1$, so $\angle 2$ equals the vertical angle of the adjacent supplementary angle of $\angle 1$. It follows that the corresponding angles (or consecutive interior angles) formed when lines $b$, $c$ are cut by a third line satisfy the parallel condition.

Specifically, from $a\parallel b$ the corresponding angle of $\angle 1$ equals $\angle 1$, and it is supplementary to $\angle 2$, i.e. $\angle 1+\angle 2=180^\circ$ gives that the consecutive interior angles formed when $b$, $c$ are cut are supplementary. By **Basic Knowledge: Criterion 3**, supplementary consecutive interior angles imply parallel lines, so $b\parallel c$. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 10

$BE\parallel CF$.

Since $AB\parallel CD$, take $BC$ as the transversal; then $\angle ABC=\angle BCD$ (parallel lines give equal alternate interior angles). Also $\angle 1=\angle 2$, so

$$
\angle ABC-\angle 1=\angle BCD-\angle 2,
$$

i.e. $\angle EBC=\angle BCF$. These two angles are the alternate interior angles formed when lines $BE$, $CF$ are cut by line $BC$. By **Basic Knowledge: Criterion 2**, equal alternate interior angles imply parallel lines, so $BE\parallel CF$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 4

(Exercise 7.2, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) As shown in the figure, among the following conditions, the one that can determine line $a\parallel b$ is (　　).

(1) $\angle 2+\angle 5=180^\circ$　(2) $\angle 2=\angle 4$　(3) $\angle 4+\angle 5=180^\circ$　(4) $\angle 1=\angle 3$

The figure shows two lines $a$, $b$ cut by a transversal, with five angles $\angle 1$, $\angle 2$, $\angle 3$, $\angle 4$, $\angle 5$ marked at the two intersections.

### Solution to Problem 4

Using **Basic Knowledge: Criterion 1**, **Basic Knowledge: Criterion 2**, and **Basic Knowledge: Criterion 3**, judge item by item what kind of positional angles each pair is when lines $a$, $b$ are cut by a transversal.

$\angle 2$ and $\angle 5$ are not criterion angles formed when lines $a$, $b$ are cut by the same transversal, so their sum being $180^\circ$ cannot determine $a\parallel b$; thus (1) cannot.

$\angle 2$ and $\angle 4$ are the alternate interior angles formed when lines $a$, $b$ are cut by a transversal; by **Basic Knowledge: Criterion 2**, equal alternate interior angles imply parallel lines, so $\angle 2=\angle 4$ can determine $a\parallel b$; thus (2) can.

$\angle 4$ and $\angle 5$ are the consecutive interior angles formed when lines $a$, $b$ are cut by a transversal; by **Basic Knowledge: Criterion 3**, supplementary consecutive interior angles imply parallel lines, so $\angle 4+\angle 5=180^\circ$ can determine $a\parallel b$; thus (3) can.

$\angle 1$ and $\angle 3$ are not criterion angles between lines $a$, $b$, so $\angle 1=\angle 3$ cannot determine $a\parallel b$; thus (4) cannot.

In summary, the ones that can determine line $a\parallel b$ are (2)(3). $\blacksquare$

## Problem 14

(Exercise 7.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 14) As shown in the figure, the two mirrors in a periscope are placed parallel to each other. When light rays reflect off the mirrors, $\angle 1=\angle 2$, $\angle 3=\angle 4$. What is the relationship between $\angle 2$ and $\angle 3$? Why is the ray entering the periscope parallel to the ray leaving the periscope?

The figure shows a cross-section of a periscope: two mirrors placed parallel to each other, one above the other; an incoming ray enters from one side, reflects off the first mirror, strikes the second mirror, and reflects out the other side. At the first mirror are marked $\angle 1$ (angle between incident ray and mirror) and $\angle 2$ (angle between reflected ray and mirror); at the second mirror are marked the corresponding $\angle 3$ and $\angle 4$.

### Solution to Problem 14

The two mirrors are parallel to each other. The two reflected rays between them are cut by the mirrors (lines); $\angle 2$ and $\angle 3$ are the alternate interior angles formed when the two parallel mirrors are cut by the middle ray. By **Basic Knowledge: Property 2**, parallel lines give equal alternate interior angles, so $\angle 2=\angle 3$.

Also given $\angle 1=\angle 2$, $\angle 3=\angle 4$, so $\angle 1=\angle 2=\angle 3=\angle 4$. Thus the alternate interior angles formed when the ray entering the periscope and the ray leaving the periscope are cut by the reflected ray between the mirrors

$$
\angle 1+\angle 2=\angle 3+\angle 4
$$

hold. By **Basic Knowledge: Criterion 2**, equal alternate interior angles imply parallel lines, so the ray entering the periscope is parallel to the ray leaving the periscope. $\blacksquare$
