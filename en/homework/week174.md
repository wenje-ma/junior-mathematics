# Assignment 174

> **Definition 29.2.2** (Front View, Top View, Left View) <br>The view obtained in the front plane by observing from front to back is called the front view; the view obtained in the horizontal plane by observing from top to bottom is called the top view; the view obtained in the side plane by observing from left to right is called the left view.
>
> **Conclusion 29.2.1** (Projection Rules for the Three Views) <br>Length aligned, height level, width equal. Visible outlines are drawn as solid lines, and invisible outlines as dashed lines.

### Activity 1

(Math Activity, Activity 1) Observe an object and draw its three views. Choose an object of simple shape, observe it from different directions, draw the three views, and ask a classmate to state the shape of the object based on the views.

### Solution to Activity 1

By **Definition 29.2.2** and **Conclusion 29.2.1**, observe the object from the three directions of the front, the top, and the left, and draw the front view, top view, and left view, taking care that the three views are length-aligned, height-level, and width-equal. If a classmate cannot state the object's shape from the views, it means the views are not expressed clearly enough and the drawing needs improvement. $\blacksquare$

### Activity 2

(Math Activity, Activity 2) Design a solid and make a model.

(1) Each student designs a solid and draws its three views;

(2) Students exchange the three-view drawings with each other and each makes a solid model according to the three views in hand;

(3) Exchange and see: is the model made consistent with the designer's idea?

### Solution to Activity 2

By **Conclusion 29.2.1**, imagine the solid shape from the three views: the front view determines the front, the top view determines the top layout, and the left view determines the side. If the model is not consistent with the designer's idea, it means some shape information in the three views is not sufficiently expressed and needs to be completed by adding dashed lines or explanations. $\blacksquare$

### Activity 3

(Math Activity, Activity 3) Design and make a pen holder. Design a pen holder you like, draw its three views and net, and make a pen holder model.

### Solution to Activity 3

A pen holder is usually cylindrical or prismatic. When designing, first determine the base shape (a circle or a polygon) and the height, draw the three views (the front view is a rectangle, the top view is a circle or a polygon, the left view is a rectangle), then draw the net (the lateral face unfolds into a rectangle, plus the base). Cut the cardboard according to the net and glue it, and you obtain the pen holder model. This process embodies the transformation relationship among the three views, the net, and the physical object. $\blacksquare$
