> **Definition 28.1.1** (Rotation of a Figure) <br>Rotating a plane figure about a point $O$ in the plane through an angle is called rotation of the figure; the point $O$ is called the center of rotation, and the angle turned through is called the angle of rotation.
> 
> **Definition 28.1.2** (Corresponding Point) <br>If a point $P$ on a figure becomes the point $P'$ after rotation, then these two points are called corresponding points of this rotation.
> 
> **Property 28.1.1** (Properties of Rotation) <br>The figure before and after rotation is congruent; in a figure and the figure obtained by rotating it, the distances from corresponding points to the center of rotation are equal; the angles formed by the segments joining corresponding points to the center of rotation are equal, each equal to the angle of rotation.

### Exercise 1

(Exercise 28.1, Problem 1) Give some examples of rotation in real life and production, and identify the center of rotation and the angle of rotation in each.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 28.1.1** (Rotation of a Figure), rotation is the turning of a figure about a fixed point through an angle.

The hands of a clock turn about the center of the dial: the center of rotation is the center of the dial, and the angle of rotation is the angle the hand turns through; the blades of a windmill turn about the center of the axle: the center of rotation is the center of the axle, and the angle of rotation is the angle between adjacent blades; a steering wheel turns about its central axis: the center of rotation is the center of the axis, and the angle of rotation is the angle the wheel turns through. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 3

(Exercise 28.1, Problem 3) As shown in the figure, a boy sits on a swing, and the swing rotates through $80^\circ$. In the figure, choose any point $P$ on the boy's body and, using the properties of rotation, mark the corresponding point of $P$.

(1) What is the relationship between the distances of these two points to the center of rotation?

(2) How many degrees is the angle formed by the segments joining these two points to the center of rotation?

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Property 28.1.1** (Properties of Rotation), corresponding points are equidistant from the center of rotation, and the angle formed by the segments joining corresponding points to the center of rotation equals the angle of rotation.

The suspension point of the swing is the center of rotation $O$. Take a point $P$ on the boy's body; on the ray obtained by rotating $OP$ about $O$ through $80^\circ$, take $P'$ such that $OP'=OP$; then $P'$ is the corresponding point of $P$.

(1) By **Property 28.1.1**, $OP=OP'$, i.e. these two points are equidistant from the center of rotation.

(2) By **Property 28.1.1**, $\angle POP'$ equals the angle of rotation $80^\circ$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 2

(Exercise 28.1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 2) Rotate the five-pointed star pattern in the figure about its center $O$. Through what minimum angle of rotation will the rotated five-pointed star coincide with itself? Discuss an equilateral triangle similarly.

The figure shows a five-pointed star pattern with center labeled $O$, together with an equilateral triangle; both are to be rotated about their respective centers until they coincide with themselves.

### Solution to Problem 2

By **Property 28.1.1** (Properties of Rotation), for the rotated figure to coincide with itself, the central angle subtended by two adjacent characteristic parts must equal the angle of rotation.

A five-pointed star has $5$ equal angles, and the central angle subtended by two adjacent angles is $\frac{360^\circ}{5}=72^\circ$; hence when the angle of rotation is at least $72^\circ$, the five-pointed star coincides with itself.

An equilateral triangle has $3$ equal angles, and the central angle subtended by two adjacent angles is $\frac{360^\circ}{3}=120^\circ$; hence when the angle of rotation is at least $120^\circ$, the equilateral triangle coincides with itself. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 5

(Exercise 28.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 5) As shown in the figure, $\triangle ABC$ is an equilateral triangle, $D$ is the midpoint of the side $BC$, and rotating $\triangle ADC$ about the point $A$ clockwise through $60^\circ$ gives $\triangle AEB$. Find the measures of $\angle ABE$, $\angle DAE$, and $\angle BED$.

The figure shows equilateral triangle $ABC$ with $D$ the midpoint of side $BC$; rotating $\triangle ADC$ about point $A$ clockwise through $60^\circ$ gives $\triangle AEB$, with point $E$ lying outside $\triangle ABC$, and $BE$ connected to $BD$.

### Solution to Problem 5

By **Property 28.1.1** (Properties of Rotation), $\triangle ADC\cong\triangle AEB$, and the angle of rotation is $60^\circ$.

$\triangle ABC$ is equilateral, so $\angle ACB=60^\circ$, $\angle BAC=60^\circ$.

$\angle ABE=\angle ACD=60^\circ$.

The angle of rotation is $\angle DAE=60^\circ$.

Also, $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$; in an equilateral triangle, $AD\perp BC$, $\angle ADC=90^\circ$, and after rotation $\angle AEB=\angle ADC=90^\circ$. In $\triangle ADC$, $\angle DAC=30^\circ$, and after rotation $\angle BAE=30^\circ$. Calculating in quadrilateral $A$, $B$, $D$, $E$ gives $\angle BED=30^\circ$.

Hence $\angle ABE=60^\circ$, $\angle DAE=60^\circ$, $\angle BED=30^\circ$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 8

(Exercise 28.1, Extension and Exploration, Problem 8) As shown in the figure, $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle AEC$ are both equilateral triangles. What is the relationship between $BE$ and $DC$? Can you use the properties of rotation to explain why this relationship holds? Pose a similar problem.

### Solution to Problem 8

By **Property 28.1.1** (Properties of Rotation), we can rotate $\triangle ADC$ about the point $A$.

$\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle AEC$ are both equilateral, so $AD=AB$, $AC=AE$, $\angle DAB=\angle CAE=60^\circ$. Thus

$$
\angle DAC=\angle DAB+\angle BAC=60^\circ+\angle BAC,
$$

$$
\angle BAE=\angle BAC+\angle CAE=\angle BAC+60^\circ.
$$

Hence $\angle DAC=\angle BAE$. Rotating $\triangle ADC$ about the point $A$ clockwise through $60^\circ$, $D$ coincides with $B$ and $C$ coincides with $E$, so $\triangle ADC\cong\triangle ABE$, and therefore $DC=BE$. $\blacksquare$

### Reading and Thinking 1

(Reading and Thinking, Congruence Transformations) Translation, axial symmetry, and rotation are all geometric transformations; under these three transformations the shape and size of a figure do not change, i.e. the figures before and after the transformation are congruent. Such a transformation that changes only the position of a figure without changing its shape or size is called a congruence transformation. Under a congruence transformation, the distance between any two points on the figure is preserved. Taking the transformation of a triangle as an example, show that the length of a segment and the size of an angle are preserved after rotation.

### Solution to Reading and Thinking 1

By **Property 28.1.1** (Properties of Rotation), let $\triangle ABC$ be rotated about the point $O$ to obtain $\triangle A'B'C'$.

By the properties of rotation, $OA=OA'$, $OB=OB'$, and $\angle AOA'=\angle BOB'$, hence $\angle AOB=\angle A'OB'$, and therefore $\triangle AOB\cong\triangle A'OB'$, so $AB=A'B'$. Similarly, $AC=A'C'$, $BC=B'C'$, hence $\triangle ABC\cong\triangle A'B'C'$, and correspondingly the angles $\angle ABC=\angle A'B'C'$, $\angle BAC=\angle B'A'C'$, $\angle ACB=\angle A'C'B'$.

That is, during rotation, the distance between any two points is preserved, and the angle formed by any three points is preserved. $\blacksquare$
