> **Definition 8.2.1** (Cube Root) <br>If a number $x$ has cube equal to $a$, i.e. $x^3=a$, then this number $x$ is called a cube root (or third root) of $a$, denoted $\sqrt[3]{a}$, where $3$ is the index of the root. The operation of finding the cube root of a number is called extracting the cube root, and extracting the cube root and cubing are inverse operations of each other.
>
> **Property 8.2.1** (Properties of Cube Roots) <br>The cube root of a positive number is positive, the cube root of a negative number is negative, and the cube root of $0$ is $0$.
>
> **Property 8.2.2** (Cube Root of a Negative Number) <br>$\sqrt[3]{-a}=-\sqrt[3]{a}$.
>
> **Property 8.2.3** (Relationship Between Cubing and Extracting Cube Roots) <br>For any number $a$, $(\sqrt[3]{a})^3=a$; $\sqrt[3]{a^3}=a$.

### Exercise 1

(Exercise 8.2, Problem 1) True or false.

(1) $-3$ is a cube root of $-27$;

(2) $\pm 3$ are cube roots of $27$;

(3) The cube root of $(-1)^3$ is $-1$;

(4) The cube root of $\sqrt[3]{-8}$ is $-2$.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Property 8.2.1** (Properties of Cube Roots), every number has exactly one cube root.

(1) $\because(-3)^3=-27$, $\therefore -3$ is a cube root of $-27$, correct.

(2) The cube root of $27$ is $3$ (unique), and $-3$ is not a cube root of $27$, so it is wrong.

(3) $(-1)^3=-1$, and the cube root of $-1$ is $-1$, so it is correct.

(4) $\sqrt[3]{-8}=-2$, and the cube root of $-2$ is $\sqrt[3]{-2}$, not $-2$, so it is wrong. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 2

(Exercise 8.2, Problem 2) Find the cube roots of the following numbers:

(1) $-1$;　(2) $0.008$;　(3) $-\frac{64}{27}$.

### Solution to Exercise 2

By **Definition 8.2.1** (Cube Root), find the number whose cube equals the given number.

(1) $\because(-1)^3=-1$, $\therefore\sqrt[3]{-1}=-1$.

(2) $\because 0.2^3=0.008$, $\therefore\sqrt[3]{0.008}=0.2$.

(3) $\because\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)^3=-\frac{64}{27}$, $\therefore\sqrt[3]{-\frac{64}{27}}=-\frac{4}{3}$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 3

(Exercise 8.2, Problem 3) As shown in the figure, there is a Rubik's cube in the shape of a cube, with volume $216\,\text{cm}^3$. What is its edge length?

### Solution to Exercise 3

Let the edge length be $x\,\text{cm}$; by the volume formula of a cube, $x^3=216$.

By **Definition 8.2.1** (Cube Root), $x=\sqrt[3]{216}$. $\because 6^3=216$, $\therefore x=6$.

That is, the edge length of the Rubik's cube is $6\,\text{cm}$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 6

(1) $\sqrt[3]{7}$;　(2) $\sqrt[3]{99}$;　(3) $\sqrt[3]{635}$;　(4) $\sqrt[3]{-28}$.

### Solution to Exercise 6

Compare with cubes.

### Problem 3

(Exercise 8.2, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) Compare the sizes of each group:

(1) $\sqrt[3]{7}$ and $2$;　(2) $\sqrt[3]{9}$ and $2.5$;　(3) $-\sqrt[3]{3}$ and $-\sqrt[3]{2}$.

### Solution to Problem 3

The cube root increases as the radicand increases.

(1) $\because 2^3=8>7$, $\therefore\sqrt[3]{7}<\sqrt[3]{8}=2$, i.e. $\sqrt[3]{7}<2$.

(2) $\because 2.5^3=15.625>9$, $\therefore\sqrt[3]{9}<\sqrt[3]{15.625}=2.5$, i.e. $\sqrt[3]{9}<2.5$.

(3) $\because 3>2$, $\therefore\sqrt[3]{3}>\sqrt[3]{2}$; taking opposites of both sides gives $-\sqrt[3]{3}<-\sqrt[3]{2}$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 4

(Exercise 8.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 4) Find the value of $x$ in each of the following:

(1) $x^3=-0.064$;　(2) $x^3-3=\frac{3}{8}$;　(3) $(x+1)^3=8$.

### Solution to Problem 4

By **Definition 8.2.1** (Cube Root).

(1) $x=\sqrt[3]{-0.064}$. $\because 0.4^3=0.064$, $\therefore x=-0.4$.

(2) $x^3=3+\frac{3}{8}=\frac{27}{8}$, $x=\sqrt[3]{\frac{27}{8}}=\frac{3}{2}$.

(3) $x+1=\sqrt[3]{8}=2$, so $x=1$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 5

(Exercise 8.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 5) Melt a cuboid iron block with length, width, and height of $21\,dm$, $20\,dm$, $19\,dm$ respectively and recast it into a cube iron block (ignoring loss). What is the edge length of this cube in decimeters (keep two decimal places)?

### Solution to Problem 5

The volume of the cuboid is $21\times 20\times 19=7980\,dm^3$. When melted and recast into a cube, the volume is unchanged; let the edge length of the cube be $x\,dm$, then $x^3=7980$.

By **Definition 8.2.1** (Cube Root), $x=\sqrt[3]{7980}$. $\because 19^3=6859<7980<8000=20^3$, continue estimating: $19.98^3\approx 7976$. Thus $x\approx 19.98\,dm$.

That is, the edge length of the cube is about $19.98\,dm$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 6

(Exercise 8.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) As shown in the figure, there is a cylindrical retractable bollard with volume $22600\,\text{cm}^3$; its height $h$ is $5.48$ times the base radius $r$. What is the base radius $r$ in centimeters? (Take $\pi$ as $3.14$, keep two decimal places.)

### Solution to Problem 6

The volume of a cylinder is $V=\pi r^2 h$. From $h=5.48r$,

$$
V=\pi r^2\cdot 5.48r=5.48\pi r^3.
$$

Substitute $V=22600$, $\pi=3.14$:

$$
r^3=\frac{22600}{5.48\times 3.14}=\frac{22600}{17.2072}\approx 1313.4.
$$

By **Definition 8.2.1** (Cube Root), $r=\sqrt[3]{1313.4}\approx 10.95$.

That is, the base radius $r$ is about $10.95\,\text{cm}$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 7

(Exercise 8.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) If the volume of a cube is enlarged to $8$ times the original, by how many times does its edge length become? What if enlarged to $27$ times? What if to $n$ times?

### Solution to Problem 7

Let the original cube have edge length $a$ and volume $a^3$.

When the volume is enlarged to $8$ times the original, the new volume is $8a^3$ and the new edge length is $\sqrt[3]{8a^3}=2a$, i.e. the edge length becomes $2$ times the original.

When the volume is enlarged to $27$ times the original, the new edge length is $\sqrt[3]{27a^3}=3a$, i.e. the edge length becomes $3$ times the original.

When the volume is enlarged to $n$ times the original, the new edge length is $\sqrt[3]{n\,a^3}=a\sqrt[3]{n}$, i.e. the edge length becomes $\sqrt[3]{n}$ times the original. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 8

(Exercise 8.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 8)

(1) Find the values of $(\sqrt[3]{0})^3$, $(\sqrt[3]{8})^3$, $(\sqrt[3]{-8})^3$, $(\sqrt[3]{27})^3$, $(\sqrt[3]{-27})^3$. For any number $a$, what does $(\sqrt[3]{a})^3$ equal?

(2) Find the values of $\sqrt[3]{0^3}$, $\sqrt[3]{2^3}$, $\sqrt[3]{(-2)^3}$, $\sqrt[3]{(-3)^3}$, $\sqrt[3]{4^3}$. For any number $a$, what does $\sqrt[3]{a^3}$ equal?

### Solution to Problem 8

(1) $(\sqrt[3]{0})^3=0$, $(\sqrt[3]{8})^3=8$, $(\sqrt[3]{-8})^3=-8$, $(\sqrt[3]{27})^3=27$, $(\sqrt[3]{-27})^3=-27$. By **Property 8.2.3** (Relationship Between Cubing and Extracting Cube Roots), for any number $a$,

$$
(\sqrt[3]{a})^3=a.
$$

(2) $\sqrt[3]{0^3}=0$, $\sqrt[3]{2^3}=2$, $\sqrt[3]{(-2)^3}=-2$, $\sqrt[3]{(-3)^3}=-3$, $\sqrt[3]{4^3}=4$. By **Property 8.2.3**, for any number $a$,

$$
\sqrt[3]{a^3}=a.
$$

$\blacksquare$

### Problem 9

(Exercise 8.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 9) Arbitrarily pick a number, say $1234$, use a calculator to extract its cube root, then extract the cube root of the resulting cube root, and so on. What do you find?

### Solution to Problem 9

By the behavior of the cube root as the radicand changes, repeatedly extract cube roots of $1234$: $\sqrt[3]{1234}\approx 10.7$, then extract the cube root again $\approx 2.2$, then again $\approx 1.3$, then again $\approx 1.09$, … The result gradually approaches $1$.

In general, repeatedly extracting cube roots of a number greater than $1$ gradually decreases the result toward $1$. $\blacksquare$
