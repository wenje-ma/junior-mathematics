> **Definition 16.3.1** (Difference of Squares Formula) <br>The product of the sum of two numbers and the difference of the same two numbers equals the difference of their squares, i.e. $(a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2$.

> **Definition 16.3.2** (Perfect Square Formula) <br>The square of the sum (or difference) of two numbers equals the sum of their squares plus (or minus) $2$ times their product, i.e. $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2$, $(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2$.

> **Definition 16.3.3** (Rule for Adding Parentheses) <br>When the sign before the parentheses is positive, all terms enclosed keep their signs; when the sign before the parentheses is negative, all terms enclosed change their signs, i.e. $a+b+c=a+(b+c)$, $a-b-c=a-(b+c)$.

### Exercise 1

(Exercise 16.3, Problem 1) Is each of the following calculations correct? If not, how should it be corrected?

(1) $(x+2)(x-2)=x^2-2$;

(2) $(2a-5)(2a+5)=4a^2-25$;

(3) $(-3ab-1)(3ab-1)=1-9a^2 b^2$.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 16.3.1** (Difference of Squares Formula).

(1) Incorrect. $(x+2)(x-2)=x^2-4$; the square of $2$ was mistakenly omitted.

(2) Correct, $(2a-5)(2a+5)=(2a)^2-5^2=4a^2-25$.

(3) Correct, taking $-1$ as the common term and $3ab$ as the opposite term, $(-1)^2-(3ab)^2=1-9a^2 b^2$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 1

(Exercise 16.3, Review and Consolidation, Problem 1) Can each of the following expressions be computed by the difference of squares formula? If so, write out the result:

(1) $(x+y)(x-y)$;

(2) $(x-y)(y-x)$;

(3) $(x+y)(-x-y)$;

(4) $(x-y)(-x-y)$.

### Solution to Problem 1

By **Definition 16.3.1**, the difference of squares formula requires one pair of identical and one pair of opposite terms among the two factors.

(1) Yes, $(x+y)(x-y)=x^2-y^2$.

(2) No; both terms are opposite, $-(x-y)^2$.

(3) No; both terms are opposite, $-(x+y)^2$.

(4) Yes, $(x-y)(-x-y)=(-y+x)(-y-x)=(-y)^2-x^2=y^2-x^2$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 6

(Exercise 16.3, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) As shown in the figure, a circular steel plate of diameter $a+b$ has two circles of diameters $a$ and $b$ cut out from it. Find the area of the remaining steel plate.

![week80-1.svg](../../figures/week80-1.svg){width=50%}

### Solution to Problem 6

The large circle has diameter $a+b$, radius $\frac{a+b}{2}$, and area $\pi(\frac{a+b}{2})^2$. The two cut-out circles have radii $\frac a2$ and $\frac b2$, with total area $\pi(\frac a2)^2+\pi(\frac b2)^2$.

The remaining area is

$$S=\pi\frac{(a+b)^2}{4}-\pi\frac{a^2}{4}-\pi\frac{b^2}{4}=\pi\frac{a^2+2ab+b^2-a^2-b^2}{4}=\frac{\pi ab}{2}.$$

$\blacksquare$

### Problem 7

(Exercise 16.3, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) First simplify, then evaluate: $(2x-y)(y+2x)-(2y+x)(2y-x)$, where $x=1$, $y=2$.

### Solution to Problem 7

By **Definition 16.3.1**.

$$(2x-y)(2x+y)-(2y+x)(2y-x)=(4x^2-y^2)-(4y^2-x^2)=5x^2-5y^2.$$

Substituting $x=1,y=2$, we get $5-20=-15$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 9

(Exercise 16.3, Extension and Exploration, Problem 9) Calculate: $2(3+1)(3^2+1)(3^4+1)\cdots(3^{32}+1)+1$.

### Solution to Problem 9

Repeatedly apply **Definition 16.3.1** (Difference of Squares Formula). Write the leading $2$ as $(3-1)$, then

$$(3-1)(3+1)=3^2-1,$$

$$(3^2-1)(3^2+1)=3^4-1,$$

and so on repeatedly; finally,

$$(3^{32}-1)(3^{32}+1)=3^{64}-1.$$

Hence the original expression $=(3^{64}-1)+1=3^{64}$. $\blacksquare$
