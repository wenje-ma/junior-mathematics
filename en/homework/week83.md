> **Definition 17.1.1** (Factoring) <br>Writing a polynomial as a product of several integral expressions is called factoring this polynomial, also called decomposing the polynomial into factors. Factoring and polynomial multiplication are inverse transformations.

> **Definition 17.1.2** (Common Factor) <br>A common factor shared by all terms of a polynomial is called the common factor of the terms of this polynomial.

> **Definition 17.1.3** (Method of Extracting the Common Factor) <br>If the terms of a polynomial have a common factor, this common factor can be extracted, writing the polynomial as the product of the common factor and another factor.

### Exercise 1

(Exercise 17.1, Problem 1) Which of the following transformations from left to right are factorizations? Which are not? Why?

(1) $4a(a+2b)=4a^2+8ab$;

(2) $a^2-4=(a+2)(a-2)$;

(3) $x^2-3x+2=x(x-3)+2$.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 17.1.1**, the result of a factorization must be a product of several integral expressions.

(1) No; it is polynomial multiplication, going from a product to a sum.

(2) Yes; $a^2-4$ is written as the product of two integral expressions, $(a+2)(a-2)$.

(3) No; the right side $x(x-3)+2$ is still in the form of a sum, not a product. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 2

(1) $ax-ay$;

(2) $a^2-2a$;

(3) $a^2+ab$;

(4) $xy-y^2+yz$.

### Solution to Exercise 2

(1) $ax-ay=a(x-y)$.

(2) $a^2-2a=a(a-2)$.

(3) $a^2+ab=a(a+b)$.

(4) $xy-y^2+yz=y(x-y+z)$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 3

(Exercise 17.1, Problem 3) Calculate using factoring:

(1) $1.99^2+1.99\times0.01$;

(2) $49\times20.2^2+52\times20.2^2-20.2^2$;

(3) $5\times3^4+4\times3^4+9\times3^2$.

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Definition 17.1.3**.

(1) $1.99^2+1.99\times0.01=1.99(1.99+0.01)=1.99\times2=3.98$.

(2) $49\times20.2^2+52\times20.2^2-20.2^2=20.2^2(49+52-1)=20.2^2\times100=40804$.

(3) $5\times3^4+4\times3^4+9\times3^2=9\times3^4+9\times3^2=9\times3^2(9+1)=81\times10=810$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 4

(Exercise 17.1, Problem 1) Factor:

(1) $8m^2 n+2mn$;

(2) $4a^2 b+10ab-ab^2$;

(3) $p(a^2+b^2)-q(a^2+b^2)$;

(4) $2a(y-z)^3-4b(z-y)^3$.

### Solution to Exercise 4

By **Definition 17.1.3**.

(1) $8m^2 n+2mn=2mn(4m+1)$.

(2) $4a^2 b+10ab-ab^2=ab(4a+10-b)$.

(3) $p(a^2+b^2)-q(a^2+b^2)=(a^2+b^2)(p-q)$.

(4) Since $(z-y)^3=-(y-z)^3$, we have $2a(y-z)^3-4b(z-y)^3=(y-z)^3(2a+4b)=2(y-z)^3(a+2b)$. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 5

(1) $2mn^2+mn$;

(3) $6a^2 b+9ab^2-15ab$;

(4) $3m^2 n-3mn+6n$;

### Problem 5

(Exercise 17.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 5) First factor, then evaluate:

(1) $(a-2)^2-6(2-a)$, where $a=-2$;

(2) $4x(y+4)-x(y+4)^2$, where $x=2$, $y=5$.

### Solution to Problem 5

(1) $(a-2)^2-6(2-a)=(a-2)^2+6(a-2)=(a-2)(a-2+6)=(a-2)(a+4)$. Substituting $a=-2$, we get $(-4)(2)=-8$.

(2) $4x(y+4)-x(y+4)^2=x(y+4)[4-(y+4)]=x(y+4)(-y)=-xy(y+4)$. Substituting $x=2,y=5$, we get $-2\cdot5\cdot9=-90$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 6

(Exercise 17.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) Given $ab=2$, $a-4b=-5$, find the value of $a^2 b-4ab^2+ab$.

### Solution to Problem 6

$$a^2 b-4ab^2+ab=ab(a-4b+1).$$

Substituting $ab=2$, $a-4b=-5$, we get $2(-5+1)=-8$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 7

(Exercise 17.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) Let $n$ be an odd number. Prove that the remainder of $n^2$ divided by $8$ is $1$.

### Solution to Problem 7

Let $n=2k+1$ ($k$ is an integer); then

$$n^2=(2k+1)^2=4k^2+4k+1=4k(k+1)+1.$$

$k$ and $k+1$ are two consecutive integers, so one of them must be even; hence $k(k+1)$ is even, say $k(k+1)=2t$. Then

$$n^2=4\cdot2t+1=8t+1.$$

Hence $n^2$ leaves a remainder of $1$ when divided by $8$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 8

(Exercise 17.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 8) Given that the three side lengths of a triangle are $a,b,c$, and $ab-ac=b^2-bc$, prove that this triangle is isosceles.

### Solution to Problem 8

Rearrange terms and extract the common factor:

$$ab-ac-b^2+bc=0,$$

$$a(b-c)-b(b-c)=0,$$

$$(b-c)(a-b)=0.$$

Hence $b=c$ or $a=b$, i.e. at least two sides of the triangle are equal; therefore this triangle is isosceles. $\blacksquare$
