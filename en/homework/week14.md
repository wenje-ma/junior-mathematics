> **Definition 3.1.1** (Algebraic Expression) <br>An expression formed by connecting numbers, or letters representing numbers, with operation symbols is called an algebraic expression.
> 
> **Definition 3.1.2** (Writing Algebraic Expressions) <br>Expressing the quantitative relationships in a problem using expressions containing numbers, letters and operation symbols.
> 
> **Definition 3.1.3** (Quantities in Inverse Proportion) <br>Two related quantities whose product is constant, i.e. $xy=k$ ($k$ is a fixed value, $k\ne 0$), are in an inverse proportion relationship.
> 
> **Definition 3.2.1** (Value of an Algebraic Expression) <br>The result obtained by substituting numerical values for the letters in an algebraic expression and computing according to the operations in the expression.

### Review Problem 1

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 1) Express the following using algebraic expressions:

(1) The number that is $4$ less than $3$ times $a$.

(2) The square of the sum of half of $a$ and $b$.

(3) A close-defense gun can fire $10000$ shells per minute. How many shells can the gun fire in $t\ \text{min}$?

(4) How much does it cost to buy $n$ items priced at $c$ yuan each? If you pay $1000$ yuan and have change left, how much change should you receive?

### Solution to Review Problem 1

By **Definition 3.1.2** (Writing Algebraic Expressions).

(1) $3$ times $a$ is $3a$, and the number that is $4$ less than it is $3a-4$.

(2) Half of $a$ is $\frac{a}{2}$, and the square of its sum with $b$ is $\left(\frac{a}{2}+b\right)^{2}$.

(3) In $t\ \text{min}$ it fires $10000t$ shells.

(4) It costs $nc$ yuan; paying $1000$ yuan, the change is $\left(1000-nc\right)$ yuan. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 2

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 2) Express the following using algebraic expressions:

(1) On a winter day in a certain place, the temperature range is $15\,^\circ\text{C}$, and the lowest temperature that day is $t\,^\circ\text{C}$. What is the highest temperature?

(2) The original price of a certain item is $b$ yuan each. The first discount is 20% off, and the second discount reduces the price by another $10$ yuan each. What is the selling price after the first discount? After the second discount?

(3) Over $30$ days, Li Ming's cumulative long-distance running reached $45000\ \text{m}$, and Liu Wei's cumulative running was $a\ \text{m}$. How many meters did Li Ming and Liu Wei run per day on average? If Liu Wei's cumulative running is more than Li Ming's, how many more meters per day does Liu Wei run on average than Li Ming?

### Solution to Review Problem 2

By **Definition 3.1.2** (Writing Algebraic Expressions).

(1) The highest temperature $=$ the lowest temperature $+$ the temperature range, which is $\left(t+15\right)\,^\circ\text{C}$.

(2) After the first 20% discount the price is $0.8b$ yuan; after the second discount of another $10$ yuan, the price is $\left(0.8b-10\right)$ yuan.

(3) Li Ming runs $\frac{45000}{30}=1500\ \text{m}$ per day on average; Liu Wei runs $\frac{a}{30}\ \text{m}$ per day on average. Liu Wei runs $\left(\frac{a}{30}-1500\right)\ \text{m}$ more per day than Li Ming. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 3

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) (1) If the length of a rectangle is $p$ and its width is $q$, what does $2\left(p+q\right)$ represent? (2) Give two examples illustrating the quantitative relationship in real problems represented by the algebraic expression $3a+2b$.

### Solution to Review Problem 3

By **Definition 3.1.1** (Algebraic Expression).

(1) $2\left(p+q\right)$ represents the perimeter of this rectangle.

(2) Example 1: A buys $a$ kilograms of apples at $3$ yuan per kilogram, and B buys $b$ kilograms of bananas at $2$ yuan per kilogram, costing a total of $\left(3a+2b\right)$ yuan. Example 2: a car travels at $a\ \text{km/h}$ for $3\ \text{h}$, then at $b\ \text{km/h}$ for $2\ \text{h}$, traveling a total of $\left(3a+2b\right)\ \text{km}$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 4

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) A rope is used to form a rectangle, with the lengths of two adjacent sides being $x\ \text{m}$ and $y\ \text{m}$ respectively.

(1) When the rope is $12\ \text{m}$ long, express the relationship between $y$ and $x$ with an equation.

(2) When the area of the rectangle is $12\ \text{m}^{2}$, express the relationship between $y$ and $x$ with an equation.

(3) When the perimeter of the rectangle is constant, are the lengths of the adjacent sides in inverse proportion? What if the area is constant? Why?

### Solution to Review Problem 4

By **Definition 3.1.3** (Quantities in Inverse Proportion).

(1) The perimeter is $2\left(x+y\right)=12$, hence $y=6-x$.

(2) The area is $xy=12$, hence $y=\frac{12}{x}$.

(3) When the perimeter is constant, $x+y=6$ (the sum is constant), not the product, so the adjacent sides are not in inverse proportion. When the area is constant, $xy=12$ (the product is constant); by **Definition 3.1.3** (Quantities in Inverse Proportion), the adjacent sides are in inverse proportion. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 5

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 5) Given the following values of $a$ and $b$, find the values of the algebraic expressions $a^{2}-b^{2}$ and $\left(a-b\right)^{2}$ respectively:

(1) $a=-1$, $b=-3$;

(2) $a=2$, $b=-\frac{1}{2}$.

### Solution to Review Problem 5

By **Definition 3.2.1** (Value of an Algebraic Expression).

(1) When $a=-1$ and $b=-3$, $a^{2}-b^{2}=1-9=-8$, $\left(a-b\right)^{2}=\left(-1+3\right)^{2}=4$.

(2) When $a=2$ and $b=-\frac{1}{2}$, $a^{2}-b^{2}=4-\frac{1}{4}=\frac{15}{4}$, $\left(a-b\right)^{2}=\left(2+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{25}{4}$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 6

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) The $1$-st row of an auditorium has $a$ seats, and each row after that has $1$ more seat than the previous row.

(1) How many seats are in the $2$-nd row? The $3$-rd row? Express the number of seats in the $n$-th row using an algebraic expression.

(2) When $a=20$, calculate the number of seats in the $19$-th row.

### Solution to Review Problem 6

By **Definition 3.1.2** (Writing Algebraic Expressions) and **Definition 3.2.1** (Value of an Algebraic Expression).

(1) The $2$-nd row has $\left(a+1\right)$ seats, and the $3$-rd row has $\left(a+2\right)$ seats. Since each row has $1$ more seat than the previous row, the $n$-th row has $\left(a+n-1\right)$ seats.

(2) When $a=20$, the $19$-th row has $20+19-1=38$ seats. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 7

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) As shown in the figure, the side length of square $ABCD$ is $a$. Point $E$ lies on side $DC$, with $DE=b$, and the shaded region is triangle $ADE$.

(1) Based on the data in the figure, express the area $S$ of the shaded region using an algebraic expression containing $a$ and $b$;

(2) When $a=6$ and $b=2$, find the area of the shaded region.

### Solution to Review Problem 7

By **Definition 3.1.2** (Writing Algebraic Expressions).

(1) Triangle $ADE$ is a right triangle, with the two legs being $AD=a$ and $DE=b$, hence

$$S=\frac{1}{2}ab.$$

(2) By **Definition 3.2.1** (Value of an Algebraic Expression), when $a=6$ and $b=2$,

$$S=\frac{1}{2}\times 6\times 2=6.$$

Hence the area of the shaded region is $6$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 8

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 8) As shown in the figure, a set of square-shaped figures is made with counters: the $1$-st uses $4$ counters, the $2$-nd uses $8$ counters, and the $3$-rd uses $12$ counters. Continuing in this way, how many counters are needed to make the $n$-th figure?

### Solution to Review Problem 8

By **Definition 3.1.2** (Writing Algebraic Expressions).

The $1$-st uses $4=4\times 1$ counters, the $2$-nd $8=4\times 2$ counters, the $3$-rd $12=4\times 3$ counters; that is, each figure uses $4$ more counters than the previous one. Hence $4n$ counters are needed to make the $n$-th figure. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 9

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 9) Tanghulu. (1) If each bamboo skewer holds $5$ hawthorns, how many hawthorns are needed to make $n$ skewers of tanghulu? What kind of proportional relationship is there between the total number of hawthorns needed and the number of tanghulu skewers? (2) If $200$ hawthorns are used to make $b$ skewers of tanghulu, with the same number of hawthorns on each skewer, how many hawthorns are on each skewer? (3) If there are $a$ hawthorns and, under the rule that each skewer has the same number of hawthorns, $b$ skewers are made with $c$ hawthorns left over, how many hawthorns are on each skewer? When $a=130$, $b=16$, $c=2$, find the number of hawthorns on each skewer.

### Solution to Review Problem 9

By **Definition 3.1.2** (Writing Algebraic Expressions) and **Definition 3.1.3** (Quantities in Inverse Proportion).

(1) Making $n$ skewers requires $5n$ hawthorns. The ratio of the total number of hawthorns to the number of skewers is $5$ (constant), so the two are in direct proportion.

(2) The number of hawthorns per skewer is $\frac{200}{b}$.

(3) A total of $\left(a-c\right)$ hawthorns were used, giving $\frac{a-c}{b}$ per skewer. When $a=130$, $b=16$, $c=2$, $\frac{a-c}{b}=\frac{130-2}{16}=8$, i.e. $8$ hawthorns per skewer. $\blacksquare$
