> **Definition 29.1.1** (Projection) <br>The shadow obtained by shining light on an object and projecting it onto a certain plane is called the projection of the object.
> 
> **Definition 29.1.4** (Orthogonal Projection) <br>The projection produced when the projection lines are perpendicular to the projection plane is called orthogonal projection.
> 
> **Definition 29.2.2** (Front View, Top View, Left View) <br>The front view is observed from front to back, the top view from top to bottom, and the left view from left to right.
> 
> **Conclusion 29.2.1** (Projection Rules for the Three Views) <br>Length aligned, height level, width equal. Visible outlines are drawn as solid lines, and invisible outlines as dashed lines.

### Review Problem 6

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) Based on the net, draw the three views of this object, and find the volume and surface area of this object. The net is a rectangle (height $20$, width $10$), with a circle of diameter $10$ on each of the top and bottom.

The figure shows the surface net of a cylinder: in the middle is a rectangle of height $20$ and width $10$, with a circle of diameter $10$ attached to each of the top and bottom.

### Solution to Review Problem 6

The net consists of one rectangle and two equal circles, so the object is a cylinder. The width of the rectangle is the base diameter $d=10$, the radius $r=5$; the height of the rectangle is the cylinder height $h=20$.

Three views: the front view is a rectangle ($10\times 20$), the top view is a circle of diameter $10$, and the left view is a rectangle ($10\times 20$).

The volume is $V=\pi r^{2}h=\pi\times 25\times 20=500\pi$.

The surface area is $S=2\pi r^{2}+2\pi rh=2\pi\times 25+2\pi\times 5\times 20=50\pi+200\pi=250\pi$. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 7

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) Based on the three views, find the surface area of the solid and draw its net. Three views: both the front view and the left view are a rectangle below (height $20$) with a triangle above (height $5$); the top view is a circle of diameter $10$.

The figure shows a set of three views: both the front view and the left view are a rectangle below (height $20$) with a triangle above (height $5$), and the top view is a circle of diameter $10$, corresponding to a composite solid of a cylinder below and a cone on top.

### Solution to Review Problem 7

Since the top view is a circle and the front view is a triangle above a rectangle, the solid is a composite of a lower cylinder (diameter $10$, height $20$) and an upper cone (base diameter $10$, height $5$).

The cylinder base radius is $r=5$, the cylinder height is $h_{1}=20$, and the cone height is $h_{2}=5$.

The cone slant height is $l=\sqrt{r^{2}+h_{2}^{2}}=\sqrt{25+25}=5\sqrt{2}$.

The surface area of the composite $=$ the lateral area of the cylinder $+$ the base area of the cylinder $+$ the lateral area of the cone (the upper base of the cylinder coincides with the base of the cone and is not counted):

$$S=2\pi rh_{1}+\pi r^{2}+\pi rl=2\pi\times 5\times 20+\pi\times 25+\pi\times 5\times 5\sqrt{2}$$

$$=200\pi+25\pi+25\sqrt{2}\,\pi=25\pi\left(9+\sqrt{2}\right).$$

The net is: a rectangle ($2\pi r\times h_{1}=10\pi\times 20$) as the lateral face of the cylinder, a circle of radius $r=5$ as the base, and a sector of radius $l=5\sqrt{2}$ and arc length $10\pi$ as the lateral face of the cone. $\blacksquare$

### Review Problem 8

(Review Problem, Extension and Exploration, Problem 8) Based on the following three views, find the volume of the solid they represent (dimensions are marked in the figure).

The figure shows two sets of dimensioned three views, each corresponding to a different solid, asking to calculate the volume from the labeled dimensions.

(1) Both the front view and the left view are a large rectangle (height $8$) with a small rectangle stacked on top (height $2$, width $4$); the top view is two concentric circles, the outer circle of diameter $8$ and the inner circle of diameter $4$.

(2) The front view is a rectangle (width $4$, height $6$) with a semicircle on top (radius $2$); the left view is a narrow rectangle (width $2$, height $6$); the top view is a rectangle ($4\times 2$).

### Solution to Review Problem 8

(1) Since the top view is concentric circles and both the front and left views are stacked rectangles, the solid is two coaxial cylinders: the lower large cylinder of diameter $8$ (radius $4$), height $8$; the upper small cylinder of diameter $4$ (radius $2$), height $2$.

$$V=\pi\times 4^{2}\times 8+\pi\times 2^{2}\times 2=128\pi+8\pi=136\pi.$$

(2) From the front view, left view, and top view, the lower part of the solid is a rectangular box ($4\times 2\times 6$), and the upper part is a half-cylinder (radius $r=2$, length $2$).

$$V=4\times 2\times 6+\frac{1}{2}\pi\times 2^{2}\times 2=48+4\pi.\ \blacksquare$$
