> **Definition 28.2.1** (Central Symmetry) <br>If a figure is rotated about a point through $180^\circ$ and the rotated figure can coincide with another figure, then the two figures are said to be symmetric about this point, or centrally symmetric; this point is called the center of symmetry, and the points that coincide after rotation are called symmetric points.
>
> **Property 28.2.1** (Properties of Central Symmetry) <br>For two figures that are centrally symmetric, the segments joining symmetric points pass through the center of symmetry and are bisected by it; the two centrally symmetric figures are congruent.
>
> **Definition 28.2.2** (Centrally Symmetric Figure) <br>If a figure is rotated about a point through $180^\circ$ and the rotated figure can coincide with the original figure, then the figure is called a centrally symmetric figure, and this point is its center of symmetry.
>
> **Property 28.2.2** (Coordinates of Points Symmetric about the Origin) <br>When two points are symmetric about the origin, their coordinates have opposite signs, i.e. the point symmetric to $P(x,y)$ about the origin is $P'(-x,-y)$.

### Problem 8

### Solution to Problem 8

The area of the rectangular plot is $10\times6=60\,\text{m}^2$, and the area of the flower bed is $30\,\text{m}^2$.

Scheme 1: taking the center of the rectangle as the center of symmetry, take the flower bed to be a small rectangle concentric with the large one, with area half that of the large rectangle.

### Problem 9

(Exercise 28.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 9) In the rectangular coordinate system, construct the point $B$ symmetric to the point $A(-3,2)$ about the $x$-axis, then construct the point $C$ symmetric to the point $B$ about the $y$-axis. Write the coordinates of the point $C$. What is the relationship between the point $A$ and the point $C$? What can you conclude from this?

### Solution to Problem 9

A point symmetric about the $x$-axis keeps its x-coordinate and takes the opposite y-coordinate, so $B(-3,-2)$. A point symmetric about the $y$-axis keeps its y-coordinate and takes the opposite x-coordinate, so $C(3,-2)$.

The coordinates of $C(3,-2)$ and $A(-3,2)$ have opposite signs, so $A$ and $C$ are symmetric about the origin. It follows: reflecting a point first about the $x$-axis and then about the $y$-axis is equivalent to central symmetry of the point about the origin. $\blacksquare$

### Reading and Thinking 1

(Reading and Thinking, Rotationally Symmetric Figures) If a plane figure is rotated about a point $O$ in the plane through an angle $\alpha$ ($0<\alpha<360^\circ$) and the resulting new figure coincides exactly with the original, then the figure is called a rotationally symmetric figure. A centrally symmetric figure is a special kind of rotationally symmetric figure. Think about it: are all regular polygons rotationally symmetric? What rotational symmetry do they have?

### Solution to Reading and Thinking 1

By **Definition 28.2.2** (Centrally Symmetric Figure) and the concept of a rotationally symmetric figure.

A regular $n$-gon coincides with itself after being rotated about its center through $\frac{360^\circ}{n}$, so a regular $n$-gon is a rotationally symmetric figure, with angle of rotation an integer multiple of $\frac{360^\circ}{n}$. A regular hexagon coincides with itself after rotating through $60^\circ$, and a regular pentagon after rotating through $72^\circ$. When $n$ is even, rotating through $180^\circ$ makes it coincide with itself, and in this case the regular $n$-gon is also a centrally symmetric figure. $\blacksquare$

### Information Technology Application 1

(Information Technology Application, Exploring the Symmetry of the Graph of an Inverse Proportional Function) Draw the graph of the inverse proportional function $y=\frac{k}{x}$ and investigate its symmetry.

### Solution to Information Technology Application 1

By **Property 28.2.2** (Coordinates of Points Symmetric about the Origin).

Take any point $A(x,y)$ on the graph of $y=\frac{k}{x}$; then $xy=k$. Its point symmetric about the origin is $A'(-x,-y)$, and since $(-x)(-y)=xy=k$, $A'$ is also on the graph. Therefore the graph of the inverse proportional function $y=\frac{k}{x}$ is centrally symmetric about the origin, with the origin as its center of symmetry. $\blacksquare$
