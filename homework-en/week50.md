## Exercise 1

(Exercise 22.2, Problem 1) A landscaping team is greening a park and takes a break in the middle. The functional relation between the greened area $S$ and the working time $t$ is shown in the figure.

(1) Before the break, how long did the team work? What was the greened area?

(2) How long was the break?

(3) After the break, how much area did the team green per hour?

### Solution to Exercise 1

By Graph of a Function, the x-coordinate of a point on the graph is the working time $t$, and the y-coordinate is the greened area $S$.

(1) From the graph, from $t=0$ to $t=1\ \text{h}$, the greened area increases from $0$ to $60\ \text{m}^2$, so the team was working. Thus before the break the team worked for $1\ \text{h}$, and the greened area was $60\ \text{m}^2$.

(2) From $t=1\ \text{h}$ to $t=2\ \text{h}$, the greened area stays at $60\ \text{m}^2$; the graph is a segment parallel to the $t$-axis, showing the team was resting. Thus the break lasted $2-1=1\ \text{h}$.

(3) From $t=2\ \text{h}$ to $t=4\ \text{h}$, the greened area increases from $60\ \text{m}^2$ to $160\ \text{m}^2$. After the break the team worked $4-2=2\ \text{h}$ and greened $160-60=100\ \text{m}^2$. Thus the area greened per hour is $100 \div 2 = 50\ \text{m}^2/\text{h}$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 6

(Exercise 22.2, Problem 3) A boat moves at a steady speed along a straight line toward a dock. At $0\ \text{min}$, $2\ \text{min}$, $4\ \text{min}$, $6\ \text{min}$, the distances from the boat to the dock are measured as $200\ \text{m}$, $150\ \text{m}$, $100\ \text{m}$, $50\ \text{m}$. Is the distance $s$ (unit: m) from the boat to the dock a function of time $t$ (unit: min)? If so, write the analytic expression, draw the graph, and calculate how long it takes the boat to reach the dock.

### Solution to Exercise 6

By Function, for each definite value of time $t$, the distance $s$ from the boat to the dock has a unique definite value, so $s$ is a function of $t$.

The boat moves at a steady speed of $\left(200-150\right)\div 2 = 25\ \text{m/min}$. The initial distance is $200\ \text{m}$, so the analytic expression is

$$
s = 200 - 25t \quad \left(0 \leq t \leq 8\right).
$$

The figure shows a plane rectangular coordinate system with horizontal axis time $t$ (unit: min) and vertical axis distance $s$ (unit: m); a line segment joining points $\left(0,200\right)$ and $\left(8,0\right)$, namely the graph of $s=200-25t$ ($0\leq t\leq8$).

When the boat reaches the dock, $s=0$, i.e. $200-25t=0$, giving $t=8\ \text{min}$. Thus the boat takes $8\ \text{min}$ to reach the dock. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 6

(Exercise 22.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) A bank deposit has an annual interest rate of $2\%$; $10000$ yuan of principal is invested. If the annual interest is not compounded into the principal, find the analytic expression for the sum of principal and interest $y$ (unit: yuan) as a function of the number of years $x$, and calculate the sum after $4$ years.

### Solution to Problem 6

The annual interest is $10000 \times 2\% = 200$ yuan. The total interest after $x$ years is $200x$ yuan.

The sum of principal and interest equals principal plus interest, so the analytic expression is

$$
y = 10000 + 200x.
$$

When $x=4$, $y=10000+200\times4=10800$ yuan. Thus after $4$ years the sum is $10800$ yuan. $\blacksquare$

## Activity 1

(Mathematical Activity, Activity 1, Problem 1) Body fat percentage is the proportion of fat mass in body weight. At different times, a person's waist circumference (denoted $l$, unit: cm) and weight (denoted $w$, unit: kg) change; from these variables, the body fat percentage at different times can be calculated. The specific calculation process is as follows:

(1) Calculate $a$; $a$ is a function of the waist circumference $l$, $a=0.74l$;

(2) Calculate $b$; $b$ is a function of weight $w$; for males $b=0.082w+44.74$, for females $b=0.082w+34.89$;

(3) Calculate total fat mass $d$, $d=a-b$;

(4) Calculate body fat percentage $p$, $p=\frac{d}{w}\times100\%$.

Measure and record each student's waist circumference $l$ and weight $w$, fill in the body fat measurement table, and analyze the results.

### Solution to Activity 1

(3) From $d=a-b$, substitute the $a$ and $b$ obtained in (1) and (2) to obtain the total fat mass $d$.

(4) From $p=\frac{d}{w}\times100\%$, substitute $d$ and $w$ to obtain the body fat percentage $p$.

## Review Problem 1

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 1) Places $A$ and $B$ are $10\ \text{km}$ apart; Li Ming sets out from $A$ by bicycle at $20\ \text{km/h}$ toward $B$. Let $x$ (unit: h) be the cycling time and $y$ (unit: km) be Li Ming's distance from $B$. Identify the constants and variables, the independent variable and the function, and write the analytic expression and the range of the independent variable.

### Solution to Review Problem 1

By Constants and Variables, the distance $10\ \text{km}$ between $A$ and $B$ and the cycling speed $20\ \text{km/h}$ remain constant, so they are constants. The cycling time $x$ and the distance $y$ from $B$ change, so $x$ and $y$ are variables.

By Function, for each definite value of the cycling time $x$, the distance $y$ from $B$ has a unique definite value, so $y$ is a function of $x$, where $x$ is the independent variable.

After cycling for $x\ \text{h}$, Li Ming has traveled $20x\ \text{km}$, and his distance from $B$ is $10-20x$. So the analytic expression is $y=10-20x$.

The cycling time cannot be negative, and once Li Ming reaches $B$ the distance is $0$, i.e. $10-20x \geq 0$, giving $x \leq 0.5$. So the range of the independent variable $x$ is $0 \leq x \leq 0.5$. $\blacksquare$

## Review Problem 3

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) Determine whether the following points lie on the graph of $y=\frac{1}{2}x+1$.

$\left(-2, -1\right)$, $\left(-1, 0\right)$, $\left(0, 1\right)$, $\left(1, 2\right)$.

### Solution to Review Problem 3

By Graph of a Function, the coordinates of a point on the graph satisfy the analytic expression.

Substitute $x=-2$ into $y=\frac{1}{2}x+1$: $y=\frac{1}{2}\times\left(-2\right)+1=0 \neq -1$, so $\left(-2, -1\right)$ is not on the graph.

Substitute $x=-1$: $y=\frac{1}{2}\times\left(-1\right)+1=\frac{1}{2} \neq 0$, so $\left(-1, 0\right)$ is not on the graph.

Substitute $x=0$: $y=\frac{1}{2}\times0+1=1$, which matches the y-coordinate, so $\left(0, 1\right)$ is on the graph.

Substitute $x=1$: $y=\frac{1}{2}\times1+1=\frac{3}{2} \neq 2$, so $\left(1, 2\right)$ is not on the graph. $\blacksquare$

## Review Problem 5

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 5) The perimeter of an isosceles triangle is $20$. Write the analytic expression for the base length $y$ as a function of the leg length $x$, state the range of the independent variable in the analytic expression, and draw its graph in the plane rectangular coordinate system.

### Solution to Review Problem 5

The two legs of the isosceles triangle are both $x$, the base is $y$, and the perimeter is $2x+y=20$. So

$$
y = 20 - 2x.
$$

By the triangle inequality (the sum of two sides greater than the third), $2x > y = 20-2x$, giving $x > 5$. Also the side lengths are positive, $y=20-2x > 0$, giving $x < 10$. So the range of the independent variable $x$ is $5 < x < 10$.

The figure shows a plane rectangular coordinate system with horizontal axis leg length $x$ and vertical axis base length $y$; a line segment joining the open endpoints $\left(5,10\right)$ and $\left(10,0\right)$ (endpoints excluded), namely the graph of $y=20-2x$ ($5< x< 10$).

$\blacksquare$
