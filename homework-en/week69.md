> **Definition 27.1.3 (Similar Polygons)**
> 
> Two polygons with the same number of sides are called similar polygons if their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional; the ratio of corresponding sides of similar polygons is called the similarity ratio. The corresponding angles of similar polygons are equal, and their corresponding sides are proportional.

> **Criterion Theorem 27.2.2 (SSS)**
> 
> Two triangles whose three sides are proportional are similar.

> **Property 27.2.2 (Area Ratio)**
> 
> The ratio of the areas of similar triangles equals the square of the similarity ratio.

> **Criterion Theorem 27.2.3 (SAS)**
> 
> Two triangles with two sides proportional and the included angle equal are similar.

> **Criterion Theorem 27.2.4 (AA)**
> 
> Two triangles with two pairs of corresponding angles equal are similar.

> **Definition 27.3.1 (Homothetic Figures)**
> 
> If the points $A,B,\ldots,P,\ldots$ on one figure and the points $A',B',\ldots,P',\ldots$ on another figure correspond respectively, and the connecting lines $AA',BB',\ldots,PP',\ldots$ all pass through the same point $O$, with $\frac{OA'}{OA}=\frac{OB'}{OB}=\cdots=\frac{OP'}{OP}=\cdots$, then the two figures are called homothetic figures, and point $O$ is the center of homothety. Homothetic figures are special similar figures, and their corresponding sides are parallel to each other.

### Solution to Review Problem 3

## Review Problem 6

(Review Problem 27, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) It is known that the outer diameter of a part is $a$. A crossing caliper (with two arms $AC$ and $BD$ of equal length) is used to measure the inner hole diameter $AB$ of the part. If $OA:OC=OB:OD=n$, and $CD=b$ is measured, find $AB$ and the part thickness $x$.

### Solution to Review Problem 6

$\angle{A}OB=\angle{C}OD$ (vertical angles), and $\frac{OA}{OC}=\frac{OB}{OD}=n$; by **Criterion Theorem 27.2.3** (SAS), $\triangle{O}AB\backsim\triangle{O}CD$.

$$
\frac{AB}{CD}=\frac{OA}{OC}=n\Rightarrow{A}B=nb.
$$

The outer diameter of the part is $a$, and the thickness is $x=\frac{a-AB}{2}=\frac{a-nb}{2}$. $\blacksquare$

## Review Problem 11

(Review Problem 27, Extension and Exploration, Problem 12) To find the height $AB$ of a mountain peak on an island, sighting poles $CD$ and $EF$ are erected at points $D$ and $F$; both poles are $3$ zhang high, $D$ and $F$ are $1000$ bu apart ($1$ zhang $=10$ chi, $1$ bu $=6$ chi), and $AB$, $CD$, and $EF$ lie in the same plane. From point $G$, $123$ bu back from pole $CD$, the summit $A$ and the top $C$ of the pole are visible in a straight line; from point $H$, $127$ bu back from pole $EF$, the summit $A$ and the top $E$ of the pole are visible in a straight line. What are the height $AB$ of the peak and its horizontal distance $BD$ from pole $CD$, in bu?

### Solution to Review Problem 11

The pole height is $3$ zhang $=30$ chi $=5$ bu. Let $AB=h$, $BD=d$.

By similar triangles, from point $G$: $\frac{h-5}{d+123}=\frac{5}{123}$. From point $H$: $\frac{h-5}{d+1000+127}=\frac{5}{127}$.

Dividing the two equations to eliminate $h-5$:

$$
\frac{d+1127}{d+123}=\frac{127}{123}.
$$

$$
123\left(d+1127\right)=127\left(d+123\right)\Rightarrow123d+138621=127d+15621.
$$

$$
4d=123000\Rightarrow{d}=30750\,\text{步}.
$$

Substituting into $\frac{h-5}{30750+123}=\frac{5}{123}$:

$$
h-5=\frac{5\times30873}{123}=1255.
$$

$$
h=1255+5=1260\,\text{步}.
$$

Thus the peak height is $AB=1260$ bu, and the horizontal distance is $BD=30750$ bu. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 1

(Exercise 28.1, Problem 1) As shown in the figure, in $\text{Rt}\triangle{A}BC$, $\angle{C}=90^{\circ}$, find the values of $\sin{A}$ and $\sin{B}$.

(1) $AC=5$, $BC=3$;

(2) $AC=1$, $AB=\sqrt{5}$.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 28.1.1 (Sine)**, $\sin{A}$ equals the ratio of the opposite side to the hypotenuse of $\angle{A}$, and $\sin{B}$ equals the ratio of the opposite side to the hypotenuse of $\angle{B}$.

(1) In $\text{Rt}\triangle{A}BC$, $\angle{C}=90^{\circ}$, $AC=5$, $BC=3$. By the Pythagorean theorem,

$$AB=\sqrt{AC^{2}+BC^{2}}=\sqrt{25+9}=\sqrt{34}.$$

Thus

$$\sin{A}=\frac{BC}{AB}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{34}}=\frac{3\sqrt{34}}{34},\quad\sin{B}=\frac{AC}{AB}=\frac{5}{\sqrt{34}}=\frac{5\sqrt{34}}{34}.$$

(2) In $\text{Rt}\triangle{A}BC$, $\angle{C}=90^{\circ}$, $AC=1$, $AB=\sqrt{5}$. By the Pythagorean theorem,

$$BC=\sqrt{AB^{2}-AC^{2}}=\sqrt{5-1}=2.$$

Thus

$$\sin{A}=\frac{BC}{AB}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}=\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{5},\quad\sin{B}=\frac{AC}{AB}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}=\frac{\sqrt{5}}{5}.\,\blacksquare$$

(Exercise 28.1, Problem 1) Find the sine, cosine, and tangent of the two acute angles in each of the following right triangles.

(1) $AB=13$, $AC=12$, $\angle{C}=90^{\circ}$;

(2) $BC=3$, $AC=2$, $\angle{C}=90^{\circ}$.

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Definition 28.1.1 (Sine)**, **Definition 28.1.2 (Cosine)**, and **Definition 28.1.3 (Tangent)**, first use the Pythagorean theorem to find the unknown side, then substitute into the definitions.

(1) In $\text{Rt}\triangle{A}BC$, $\angle{C}=90^{\circ}$, $AB=13$, $AC=12$, so

$$BC=\sqrt{AB^{2}-AC^{2}}=\sqrt{169-144}=5.$$

Therefore

$$\sin{A}=\frac{BC}{AB}=\frac{5}{13},\quad\cos{A}=\frac{AC}{AB}=\frac{12}{13},\quad\tan{A}=\frac{BC}{AC}=\frac{5}{12};$$

$$\sin{B}=\frac{AC}{AB}=\frac{12}{13},\quad\cos{B}=\frac{BC}{AB}=\frac{5}{13},\quad\tan{B}=\frac{AC}{BC}=\frac{12}{5}.$$

(2) In $\text{Rt}\triangle{A}BC$, $\angle{C}=90^{\circ}$, $BC=3$, $AC=2$, so

$$AB=\sqrt{AC^{2}+BC^{2}}=\sqrt{4+9}=\sqrt{13}.$$

Therefore

$$\sin{A}=\frac{BC}{AB}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{13}}=\frac{3\sqrt{13}}{13},\quad\cos{A}=\frac{AC}{AB}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{13}}=\frac{2\sqrt{13}}{13},\quad\tan{A}=\frac{BC}{AC}=\frac{3}{2};$$

$$\sin{B}=\frac{AC}{AB}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{13}}=\frac{2\sqrt{13}}{13},\quad\cos{B}=\frac{BC}{AB}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{13}}=\frac{3\sqrt{13}}{13},\quad\tan{B}=\frac{AC}{BC}=\frac{2}{3}.\,\blacksquare$$

## Exercise 4

(Exercise 28.1, Problem 2) In $\text{Rt}\triangle{A}BC$, $\angle{C}=90^{\circ}$. If each side length is enlarged to $2$ times the original, do the sine, cosine, and tangent of $\angle{A}$ change? Explain your reasoning.

### Solution to Exercise 4

By Determinacy of Acute Angle Trigonometric Function Values, the trigonometric function values of an acute angle depend only on the size of the angle, not on the side lengths of the right triangle.

After each side length is enlarged to $2$ times the original, the ratio of the three sides of the new triangle to the original is unchanged, i.e. the two triangles are similar, so the size of $\angle{A}$ is unchanged. By **Definition 28.1.1 (Sine)**, **Definition 28.1.2 (Cosine)**, and **Definition 28.1.3 (Tangent)**, $\sin{A}$, $\cos{A}$, $\tan{A}$ are each the ratio of two sides; after all sides are multiplied by $2$, the ratios are unchanged, so the sine, cosine, and tangent of $\angle{A}$ all remain unchanged. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 5

(Exercise 28.1, Problem 1) Find the value of each of the following expressions:

(1) $1-2\sin30^{\circ}\cos30^{\circ}$;

(2) $3\tan30^{\circ}-\tan45^{\circ}+2\sin60^{\circ}$;

(3) $\left(\cos^{2}30^{\circ}+\sin^{2}30^{\circ}\right)\times\tan60^{\circ}$.

### Solution to Exercise 5

By Trigonometric Function Values of Special Angles, substitute the function values of the special angles and compute.

(1) Substituting $\sin30^{\circ}=\frac{1}{2}$, $\cos30^{\circ}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$:

$$1-2\sin30^{\circ}\cos30^{\circ}=1-2\times\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}=1-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

(2) Substituting $\tan30^{\circ}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, $\tan45^{\circ}=1$, $\sin60^{\circ}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$:

$$3\tan30^{\circ}-\tan45^{\circ}+2\sin60^{\circ}=3\times\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}-1+2\times\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}=\sqrt{3}-1+\sqrt{3}=2\sqrt{3}-1.$$

(3) Since $\cos^{2}30^{\circ}+\sin^{2}30^{\circ}=\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{4}=1$, we have

$$\left(\cos^{2}30^{\circ}+\sin^{2}30^{\circ}\right)\times\tan60^{\circ}=1\times\sqrt{3}=\sqrt{3}.\,\blacksquare$$
