## Problem 1

(Exercise 15.1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) As shown, $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ are symmetric about line $l$, $\angle B=90^\circ$, $A'B'=6$. Find the degree measure of $\angle B'$ and the length of $AB$.

### Solution to Problem 1

By Properties of Axis Symmetry, two figures symmetric about a line are congruent, so corresponding angles and corresponding sides are equal. Hence $\angle B'=\angle B=90^\circ$, $AB=A'B'=6$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 2

(Exercise 15.1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, $DE$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AC$, $AE=3$, and the perimeter of $\triangle ABD$ is $13$. Find the perimeter of $\triangle ABC$.

### Solution to Problem 2

Since $DE$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AC$, $AC=2AE=6$, and point $D$ lies on $DE$; by Properties of the Perpendicular Bisector, $DA=DC$.

The perimeter of $\triangle ABD$ is $AB+BD+AD=AB+BD+DC=AB+BC=13$. Hence

$$
C_{\triangle ABC}=AB+BC+AC=13+6=19.
$$

$\blacksquare$

## Problem 4

(Exercise 15.1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 6) Write the converse proposition of each of the following propositions; do these converse propositions hold?

(1) If same-side interior angles are supplementary, then two lines are parallel;

(2) if two real numbers are equal, then their squares are equal;

(3) corresponding sides of congruent triangles are equal.

### Solution to Problem 4

(1) Converse proposition: if two lines are parallel, then same-side interior angles are supplementary. Holds.

(2) Converse proposition: if the squares of two real numbers are equal, then the two real numbers are equal. Does not hold; for example, the squares of $3$ and $-3$ are equal but the numbers themselves are not.

(3) Converse proposition: two triangles with corresponding sides equal are congruent. Holds (namely SSS). $\blacksquare$

## Problem 9

(Exercise 15.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 11) As shown, $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ are symmetric about line $l$; do the lines of the corresponding segments $AB$ and $A'B'$ intersect? What is the relationship between the intersection point and the axis of symmetry $l$?

### Solution to Problem 9

If the lines of the corresponding segments $AB$ and $A'B'$ intersect, the intersection point lies on the axis of symmetry $l$; if they are parallel, then the lines of this pair of corresponding segments are parallel to the axis of symmetry $l$. The rule is: for two figures symmetric about a line, if corresponding segments intersect, their intersection point must lie on the axis of symmetry. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 11

(Exercise 15.1, Extension and Exploration, Problem 13) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, the perpendicular bisectors of sides $AB$, $BC$ intersect at point $P$.

(1) Prove that $PA=PB=PC$;

(2) Does point $P$ also lie on the perpendicular bisector of side $AC$? What conclusion can be drawn from this?

### Solution to Problem 11

(1) Since $P$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, by Properties of the Perpendicular Bisector, $PA=PB$. Since $P$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, similarly $PB=PC$. Hence $PA=PB=PC$.

(2) From $PA=PC$, point $P$ is equidistant from $A$, $C$; by Determination of the Perpendicular Bisector, point $P$ also lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AC$. This yields: the perpendicular bisectors of the three sides of a triangle intersect at one point, which is equidistant from the three vertices. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Problem 18

## Exercise 6

(Exercise 15.3, Problem 1) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, $AB=AD=DC$, $\angle BAD=26^\circ$. Find the degree measures of $\angle B$ and $\angle C$.

### Solution to Exercise 6

From $AB=AD$, $\triangle ABD$ is isosceles; by Equal Sides Opposite Equal Angles,

$$
\angle B=\angle ADB=\frac{180^\circ-26^\circ}{2}=77^\circ.
$$

From $AD=DC$, $\triangle ADC$ is isosceles, so $\angle C=\angle DAC$. Also $\angle ADB$ is the exterior angle of $\triangle ADC$, so $\angle ADB=\angle C+\angle DAC=2\angle C$, hence $\angle C=77^\circ/2=38.5^\circ$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 8

(Exercise 15.3, Problem 3) Prove: if the median on one side of a triangle equals half of that side, then the triangle is a right triangle.

### Solution to Exercise 8

Suppose in $\triangle ABC$, $AD$ is the median on side $BC$, and $AD=\frac{1}{2}BC$. By the definition of median, $BD=DC=\frac{1}{2}BC$, so $AD=BD=DC$.

From $AD=BD$, $\triangle ABD$ is isosceles, $\angle B=\angle BAD$. From $AD=DC$, $\triangle ADC$ is isosceles, $\angle C=\angle CAD$.

Thus

$$
\angle BAC=\angle BAD+\angle CAD=\angle B+\angle C.
$$

Also $\angle BAC+\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ$, so $2\angle BAC=180^\circ$, i.e. $\angle BAC=90^\circ$. Therefore the triangle is a right triangle. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 13

(Exercise 15.3, Problem 2) As shown, in equilateral triangle $ABC$, $AD$ is the altitude on $BC$, $\angle BDE=\angle CDF=60^\circ$. Which segments in the figure are equal to $BD$?

### Solution to Exercise 13

Since the triangle is equilateral and by Three Lines in One, $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$, $BD=DC$, and $AD\perp BC$. ## Exercise 14

## Problem 20

(Exercise 15.3, Review and Consolidation, Problem 1)

(1) One angle of an isosceles triangle is $110^\circ$; how many degrees are its other two angles?

(2) One side of an isosceles triangle has length $8$ and the perimeter is $18$; what are the lengths of its other two sides?

### Solution to Problem 20

(1) $110^\circ$ can only be the vertex angle (a base angle cannot be greater than or equal to $90^\circ$), so the other two base angles are both

$$
\frac{180^\circ-110^\circ}{2}=35^\circ.
$$

(2) If the leg is $8$, then the base is $18-2\times 8=2$; if the base is $8$, then both legs are $\left(18-8\right)/2=5$. The other two sides have length $8$, $2$ or $5$, $5$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 22

(Exercise 15.3, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) A five-pointed star contains five congruent isosceles triangles, each with vertex angle $36^\circ$. Find the degree measure of $\angle AMB$.

### Solution to Problem 22

Each isosceles triangle has vertex angle $36^\circ$, so its base angles are

$$
\frac{180^\circ-36^\circ}{2}=72^\circ.
$$

$\angle AMB$ is formed from the relationship between adjacent base angles; it is $180^\circ-72^\circ-72^\circ=36^\circ$, or by the interior-angle relation of the five-pointed star $\angle AMB=108^\circ$; combined with the five-pointed-star pointed angle of $36^\circ$, we have $\angle AMB=108^\circ$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 23

(Exercise 15.3, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) As shown, in $\triangle ABC$, points $D$, $E$ lie on side $BC$, $AB=AC$, $AD=AE$. Prove that $BD=CE$.

### Solution to Problem 23

Through $A$ draw $AF\perp BC$, with foot $F$. From $AB=AC$ and Three Lines in One, $F$ is the midpoint of $BC$, $BF=CF$. From $AD=AE$, similarly $F$ is also the midpoint of $DE$, $DF=EF$.

Subtracting the two equations: $BF-DF=CF-EF$, i.e. $BD=CE$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 24

(Exercise 15.3, Review and Consolidation, Problem 5) As shown, $\angle A=\angle B=60^\circ$, $CE\parallel DA$, and $CE$ meets $AB$ at point $E$. Prove that $\triangle CEB$ is an equilateral triangle.
