> **Definition 8.1.1 (Square Root)**
> 
> If a number $x$ has square equal to $a$, i.e. $x^2=a$, then this number $x$ is called a square root (or second root) of $a$. The operation of finding the square root of a number is called extracting the square root, and extracting the square root and squaring are inverse operations of each other.

> **Definition 8.1.2 (Arithmetic Square Root)**
> 
> The positive square root $\sqrt{a}$ of a positive number $a$ is called the arithmetic square root of $a$, and $a$ is called the radicand. It is stipulated that the arithmetic square root of $0$ is $0$, i.e. $\sqrt0=0$. The square roots of a positive number $a$ are denoted by $\pm\sqrt{a}$.

> **Property 8.1.3 (Relationship Between Squaring and Extracting Roots)**
> 
> For any nonnegative number $a$, $\left(\sqrt{a}\right)^2=a$; for any number $a$, $\sqrt{a^2}=\left|a\right|$.

> **Definition 8.2.1 (Cube Root)**
> 
> If a number $x$ has cube equal to $a$, i.e. $x^3=a$, then this number $x$ is called a cube root (or third root) of $a$, denoted $\sqrt\left[3\right]{a}$, where $3$ is the index of the root. The operation of finding the cube root of a number is called extracting the cube root, and extracting the cube root and cubing are inverse operations of each other.

> **Property 8.2.2 (Cube Root of a Negative Number)**
> 
> $\sqrt\left[3\right]{-a}=-\sqrt\left[3\right]{a}$.

> **Property 8.3.1 (Real Numbers and the Number Line)**
> 
> There is a one-to-one correspondence between real numbers and points on the number line. The real number represented by a point to the right on the number line is always larger than that represented by a point to the left.

> **Property 8.3.2 (Opposite Numbers and Absolute Value)**
> 
> The opposite number of a real number $a$ is $-a$. The absolute value of a positive real number is itself, the absolute value of a negative real number is its opposite number, and the absolute value of $0$ is $0$. The operation rules and properties of rational numbers also apply to real numbers.

> **Definition 9.1.1 (Rectangular Coordinate System)**
> 
> Draw two number lines perpendicular to each other in a plane with the same origin to form a rectangular coordinate system.

> **Property 9.1.1 (One-to-One Correspondence Between Points and Coordinates)**
> 
> Points in the coordinate plane are in one-to-one correspondence with ordered pairs of real numbers.

> **Definition 9.1.2 (Coordinates of a Point)**
> 
> Through a point in the plane draw perpendiculars to the $x$-axis and $y$-axis respectively; the coordinate of the foot on the $x$-axis is the x-coordinate of the point, and the coordinate of the foot on the $y$-axis is the y-coordinate of the point. The ordered pair $\left(x,y\right)$ is called the coordinates of this point.

> **Definition 9.1.3 (Quadrants)**
> 
> The coordinate plane is divided by the two coordinate axes into four parts, called in order starting from the upper right the first, second, third, and fourth quadrants. The first quadrant $\left(+,+\right)$, the second quadrant $\left(-,+\right)$, the third quadrant $\left(-,-\right)$, the fourth quadrant $\left(+,-\right)$. Points on the coordinate axes do not belong to any quadrant.

> **Method 9.2.1 (Representing Geographic Locations with Coordinates)**
> 
> Establish a coordinate system by choosing an appropriate reference point as the origin and determining the positive directions of the $x$-axis and $y$-axis; determine the unit length according to the problem; plot each point in the coordinate plane and write down its coordinates and the name of the location. One can also represent the position of an object using a direction angle and a distance.

> **Property 9.2.1 (Representing Translation with Coordinates)**
> 
> In a rectangular coordinate system, translating point $\left(x,y\right)$ to the right (left) by $a$ units of length gives $\left(x+a,y\right)$ ($\left(x-a,y\right)$); translating it up (down) by $b$ units of length gives $\left(x,y+b\right)$ ($\left(x,y-b\right)$). Adding (subtracting) $a$ to the x-coordinate of every point of a figure is equivalent to translating the figure to the right (left) by $a$; adding (subtracting) $b$ to the y-coordinate of every point is equivalent to translating the figure up (down) by $b$.

## Review Exercise 1

(Review Exercise, Extension and Exploration, Problem 10) A cube iron block is completely immersed in the water of a cylindrical glass, and the water level rises by $58\,mm$. If the inner base radius of the glass is $95\,mm$, what is the edge length of the cube in millimeters? (Take $\pi$ as $3.14$, take integers.)

### Solution to Review Exercise 1

The cylindrical volume of the raised water level equals the volume of the cube. The volume of the raised part is

$$
V=\pi{r}^2h=3.14\times95^2\times58.
$$

Compute: $95^2=9025$, $V=3.14\times9025\times58=1645553\;\text{mm}^3$.

Let the edge length of the cube be $x$; $x^3=1645553$, $x=\sqrt\left[3\right]{1645553}\approx118$.

That is, the edge length of the cube is about $118\,mm$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 8

(Exercise 9.1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 8) In the rectangular coordinate system, choose some points whose x- and y-coordinates satisfy the following conditions, mark their positions, and see in which quadrant or on which coordinate axis they lie:

(1) The coordinates of point $P\left(x,y\right)$ satisfy $xy>0$;

(2) The coordinates of point $P\left(x,y\right)$ satisfy $xy<0$;

(3) The coordinates of point $P\left(x,y\right)$ satisfy $xy=0$.

### Solution to Problem 8

By the signs of the x- and y-coordinates in each quadrant in **Definition 9.1.3 (Quadrants)**.

(1) $xy>0$ means $x$, $y$ have the same sign: both positive is the first quadrant, both negative is the third quadrant. Thus these points lie in the first or third quadrant.

(2) $xy<0$ means $x$, $y$ have opposite signs: $x$ positive and $y$ negative is the fourth quadrant, $x$ negative and $y$ positive is the second quadrant. Thus these points lie in the second or fourth quadrant.

(3) $xy=0$ means $x=0$ or $y=0$: points with $x=0$ lie on the $y$-axis, and points with $y=0$ lie on the $x$-axis. Thus these points lie on the coordinate axes. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 9

(Exercise 9.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 9) Given points $O\left(0,0\right)$, $B\left(1,2\right)$, point $A$ lies on a coordinate axis, and $S_{\triangle{O}AB}=2$. Find the coordinates of point $A$ satisfying the conditions.

### Solution to Problem 9

Point $A$ lies on a coordinate axis; there are two cases.

If $A$ is on the $x$-axis, let $A\left(a,0\right)$. Taking $OA$ as the base, of length $\left|a\right|$, the height is the distance from point $B$ to the $x$-axis, namely the y-coordinate of $B$, which is $2$. By the triangle area formula,

$$
S_{\triangle{O}AB}=\frac{1}{2}\left|a\right|\cdot2=\left|a\right|=2,
$$

so $a=\pm2$, i.e. $A\left(2,0\right)$ or $A\left(-2,0\right)$.

If $A$ is on the $y$-axis, let $A\left(0,b\right)$. Taking $OA$ as the base, of length $\left|b\right|$, the height is the distance from point $B$ to the $y$-axis, namely the x-coordinate of $B$, which is $1$. Then

$$
S_{\triangle{O}AB}=\frac{1}{2}\left|b\right|\cdot1=2,
$$

so $\left|b\right|=4$, $b=\pm4$, i.e. $A\left(0,4\right)$ or $A\left(0,-4\right)$.

In summary, the points $A$ satisfying the conditions are $\left(2,0\right)$, $\left(-2,0\right)$, $\left(0,4\right)$, $\left(0,-4\right)$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 11

(Exercise 9.2, Problem 2) As shown in the figure, through what kind of translation can figure Ⅱ be obtained from figure Ⅰ? What changes in the coordinates of the corresponding points?

### Solution to Exercise 11

By Representing Translation with Coordinates, take a known point on figure Ⅰ and its corresponding point on figure Ⅱ, and compare the coordinates. The change in x-coordinate determines the left-right translation, and the change in y-coordinate determines the up-down translation. An increase in x-coordinate means moving right, a decrease means moving left; an increase in y-coordinate means moving up, a decrease means moving down. The coordinate changes of the corresponding points are exactly the same. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 12

(Exercise 9.2, Problem 3) In the rectangular coordinate system, given points $A\left(0,-2\right)$, $B\left(3,0\right)$. First translate segment $AB$ to the left by $2$ units of length and up by $3$ units of length to obtain segment $CD$; then translate segment $CD$ to the left by $3$ units of length and down by $2$ units of length to obtain segment $EF$. Write down the coordinates of points $C$, $D$, $E$, $F$.

### Solution to Exercise 12

By Representing Translation with Coordinates.

Segment $AB$ shifted left by $2$ (x-coordinate minus $2$) and up by $3$ (y-coordinate plus $3$) gives $CD$: $C=A$ translated $=\left(0-2,-2+3\right)=\left(-2,1\right)$, $D=B$ translated $=\left(3-2,0+3\right)=\left(1,3\right)$.

Segment $CD$ shifted left by $3$ (x-coordinate minus $3$) and down by $2$ (y-coordinate minus $2$) gives $EF$: $E=C$ translated $=\left(-2-3,1-2\right)=\left(-5,-1\right)$, $F=D$ translated $=\left(1-3,3-2\right)=\left(-2,1\right)$.

That is, $C\left(-2,1\right)$, $D\left(1,3\right)$, $E\left(-5,-1\right)$, $F\left(-2,1\right)$. $\blacksquare$
