> **Definition 14.1.1 (Congruent Figures and Congruent Triangles)**
> 
> Two figures that can coincide completely are called congruent figures; two triangles that can coincide completely are called congruent triangles, denoted $\triangle{A}BC\cong\triangle{A}'B'C'$.

> **Conclusion 14.1.1 (Properties of Congruent Triangles)**
> 
> Corresponding sides of congruent triangles are equal, and corresponding angles are equal.

> **Definition 14.1.2 (Corresponding Elements)**
> 
> Putting two congruent triangles together to coincide, the coinciding vertices are called corresponding vertices, the coinciding sides are called corresponding sides, and the coinciding angles are called corresponding angles.

> **Basic Fact 14.2.1 (Side-Angle-Side, SAS)**
> 
> Two triangles are congruent if two sides and their included angle are respectively equal; abbreviated as "side-angle-side" or SAS.

> **Conclusion 14.2.1 (Angle-Angle-Side, AAS)**
> 
> Two triangles are congruent if two angles are respectively equal and the opposite side of one pair of equal angles is equal; abbreviated as "angle-angle-side" or AAS.

> **Basic Fact 14.2.2 (Angle-Side-Angle, ASA)**
> 
> Two triangles are congruent if two angles and their included side are respectively equal; abbreviated as "angle-side-angle" or ASA.

> **Conclusion 14.2.2 (Hypotenuse-Leg, HL)**
> 
> Two right triangles are congruent if the hypotenuse and one leg are respectively equal; abbreviated as "hypotenuse-leg" or HL.

> **Basic Fact 14.2.3 (Side-Side-Side, SSS)**
> 
> Two triangles are congruent if the three sides are respectively equal; abbreviated as "side-side-side" or SSS; this shows that a triangle has stability.

## Problem 3

(Exercise 14.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 3) There are two congruent triangles in the figure, where the letters denote the side lengths of the triangles; then what is the degree measure of $\angle1$?

### Solution to Problem 3

The two triangles are congruent, and their interior angles correspond to equal values. Given that two interior angles are $60^\circ$ and $54^\circ$, by the triangle interior angle sum theorem the third interior angle is

$$
180^\circ-60^\circ-54^\circ=66^\circ.
$$

Hence $\angle1=66^\circ$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 4

(Exercise 14.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 4) As shown, $\triangle{E}FG\cong\triangle{N}MH$, and $\angle{F}$ and $\angle{M}$ are corresponding angles. In $\triangle{E}FG$, $FG$ is the longest side; in $\triangle{N}MH$, $MH$ is the longest side, and $EF=2.1$, $EH=1.1$, $NH=3.3$.

(1) Write out the other corresponding sides and corresponding angles;

(2) Find the lengths of segment $NM$ and segment $HG$.

### Solution to Problem 4

From $\angle{F}\leftrightarrow\angle{M}$ and the longest sides $FG\leftrightarrow{M}H$, the vertex correspondence is $E\leftrightarrow{N}$, $F\leftrightarrow{M}$, $G\leftrightarrow{H}$.

(1) The other corresponding sides are $EF$ and $NM$, $EG$ and $NH$; the other corresponding angles are $\angle{E}$ and $\angle{N}$, $\angle{G}$ and $\angle{H}$.

(2) By Properties of Congruent Triangles, $NM=EF=2.1$, $EG=NH=3.3$. Also point $H$ lies on segment $EG$, so

$$
HG=EG-EH=3.3-1.1=2.2.
$$

Hence $NM=2.1$, $HG=2.2$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 5

(Exercise 14.1, Extension and Exploration, Problem 5) As shown, $\triangle{A}BC\cong\triangle{D}EC$, where $CA$ and $CD$, and $CB$ and $CE$, are corresponding sides. Are $\angle{A}CD$ and $\angle{B}CE$ equal? Why?

### Solution to Problem 5

They are equal. By Properties of Congruent Triangles, the corresponding angles satisfy $\angle{A}CB=\angle{D}CE$.

Subtracting the common part $\angle{D}CB$ from both sides gives

$$
\angle{A}CD=\angle{A}CB-\angle{D}CB=\angle{D}CE-\angle{D}CB=\angle{B}CE.
$$

Hence $\angle{A}CD=\angle{B}CE$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 3

(Exercise 14.2, Problem 1) As shown, there is a pond; to measure the distance between its two ends $A$ and $B$, first take a point $C$ on the flat ground, join $AC$ and extend it to point $D$ so that $CD=CA$, join $BC$ and extend it to point $E$ so that $CE=CB$, and join $DE$. The measured length of $DE$ is the distance between $A$ and $B$. Why?

### Solution to Exercise 3

In $\triangle{A}BC$ and $\triangle{D}EC$, $CA=CD$, $CB=CE$, and $\angle{A}CB$ and $\angle{D}CE$ are vertical angles and equal. By SAS, $\triangle{A}BC\cong\triangle{D}EC$.

By Properties of Congruent Triangles, the corresponding sides satisfy $AB=DE$, so the measured length of $DE$ is the distance between $A$ and $B$. $\blacksquare$

By Properties of Congruent Triangles, the corresponding angles satisfy $\angle{A}=\angle{D}$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 9

(Exercise 14.2, Problem 1) As shown, $C$ is the midpoint of road segment $AB$. Two people start from $C$ at the same time, walk along two straight lines at the same speed, and reach $D$, $E$ at the same time, with $DA\perp{A}B$, $EB\perp{A}B$. Are the distances from $D$, $E$ to road segment $AB$ equal? Why?

### Solution to Exercise 9

They are equal. Since $C$ is the midpoint of $AB$, $CA=CB$. The two people have the same speed and arrive at the same time, so $CD=CE$. In $\text{Rt}\triangle{A}CD$ and $\text{Rt}\triangle{B}CE$, the hypotenuses satisfy $CD=CE$, and the legs satisfy $CA=CB$; by HL, $\text{Rt}\triangle{A}CD\cong\text{Rt}\triangle{B}CE$.

By Basic Knowledge, the corresponding legs satisfy $AD=BE$, i.e. the distances from $D$, $E$ to road segment $AB$ are equal. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 21
