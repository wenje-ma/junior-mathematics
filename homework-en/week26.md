> **Definition 11.1.1 (Inequality)**
> 
> An expression using the symbols "$< $" or "$> $" to denote size/order relations.

> **Property 11.1.1 (Property 1 of Inequalities)**
> 
> $a> b\Rightarrow a\pm c> b\pm c$.

> **Definition 11.1.2 (Solution of an Inequality)**
> 
> A value of the unknown that makes the inequality hold.

> **Property 11.1.2 (Property 2 of Inequalities)**
> 
> $a> b,\ c> 0\Rightarrow ac> bc$.

> **Definition 11.1.3 (Solution Set of an Inequality)**
> 
> The set consisting of all the solutions of an inequality containing an unknown.

> **Property 11.1.3 (Property 3 of Inequalities)**
> 
> $a> b,\ c< 0\Rightarrow ac< bc$.

> **Definition 11.2.1 (Linear Inequality in One Variable)**
> 
> An inequality that contains only one unknown, all expressions containing the unknown are integral expressions, and the degree of the unknown is $1$.

> **Method 11.2.2 (Solving a Linear Inequality in One Variable)**
> 
> Clear denominators, remove parentheses, transpose terms, combine like terms, and make the coefficient $1$; when multiplying or dividing by a negative number, the direction of the inequality sign reverses.

> **Definition 11.3.1 (System of Linear Inequalities in One Variable)**
> 
> A system of inequalities formed by combining several linear inequalities in one variable.

> **Definition 11.3.2 (Solution Set of a System of Inequalities)**
> 
> The common part of the solution sets of the several inequalities.

> **Method 11.3.3 (Solving a System of Inequalities)**
> 
> First solve each inequality separately, then use a number line to determine the common part of the solution sets.

## Problem 8

(Exercise 11.1, Extension and Exploration, Problem 12) Given three positive integers $a< b< c$ satisfying $\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}+\frac{1}{c}=1$. Find $a$, $b$, $c$.

### Solution to Problem 8

Since $a< b< c$ are positive integers, $a\ge2$ (if $a=1$ then $\frac{1}{a}=1$ and the other two terms are positive, making the sum greater than $1$). Also $\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}+\frac{1}{c}< \frac{3}{a}$, so $\frac{3}{a}> 1$, i.e. $a< 3$. Hence $a=2$.

Then $\frac{1}{b}+\frac{1}{c}=\frac{1}{2}$. From $b< c$ we get $\frac{2}{b}> \frac{1}{2}$, i.e. $b< 4$; and $b> a=2$, so $b=3$. Then $\frac{1}{c}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1}{6}$, $c=6$.

Verification: $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{6}=1$, and $2< 3< 6$. Hence $a=2$, $b=3$, $c=6$. $\blacksquare$

## Reading and Thinking 1

(Reading and Thinking: Comparing Sizes by Taking the Difference) To make a certain product, Plan 1 uses $50$ sheets of type-$A$ steel plate and $70$ sheets of type-$B$ steel plate; Plan 2 uses $80$ sheets of type-$A$ steel plate and $30$ sheets of type-$B$ steel plate. Let each sheet of type-$A$ steel plate cost $a$ yuan and each sheet of type-$B$ steel plate cost $b$ yuan. Use the difference method to compare the material costs of the two plans.

### Solution to Reading and Thinking 1

The cost of Plan 1 is $50a+70b$, and the cost of Plan 2 is $80a+30b$. Taking the difference:

$$
\left(50a+70b\right)-\left(80a+30b\right)=40b-30a=10\left(4b-3a\right).
$$

When $4b> 3a$, the difference is positive, so Plan 1 costs more than Plan 2; when $4b< 3a$, Plan 1 costs less than Plan 2; when $4b=3a$, the two plans cost the same. $\blacksquare$

Second inequality: $3+x> \frac{1}{2}x+1$, i.e. $\frac{1}{2}x> -2$, $x> -4$.

Common part $-4< x< 2$, with integers $x=-3,-2,-1,0,1$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 22

(Exercise 11.3, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) For which integer values of $x$ does $2\le3x-7< 8$ hold?

### Solution to Problem 22

From $2\le3x-7< 8$, add $7$ to each part: $9\le3x< 15$; divide by $3$: $3\le x< 5$. The integers are $x=3,4$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 23

(Exercise 11.3, Comprehensive Application, Problem 5) Some books are distributed to several students: if each student gets $3$ books, $8$ books are left over; if each student gets $5$ books, the last student gets some books but fewer than $3$. How many books are there? How many students are there in total?

### Solution to Problem 23

Let there be $n$ students in total and $m$ books. By the statement $m=3n+8$.

When each student gets $5$ books, the first $n-1$ students together get $5\left(n-1\right)$ books, and the last student gets $m-5\left(n-1\right)$ books, with $0< m-5\left(n-1\right)< 3$.

Substituting: $0< 3n+8-5\left(n-1\right)< 3$, i.e. $0< 13-2n< 3$. From $13-2n> 0$ we get $n< 6.5$; from $13-2n< 3$ we get $n> 5$. Hence $n=6$, $m=3\times6+8=26$.

Hence there are $26$ books and $6$ students in total. $\blacksquare$
