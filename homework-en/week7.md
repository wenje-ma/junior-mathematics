## Problem 1

(Exercise 3.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 10) (1) Let $n$ denote any integer. Use an algebraic expression containing $n$ to represent any even number and any odd number. (2) The units digit of a three-digit number is $a$, the tens digit is $b$, and the hundreds digit is $c$. Use an algebraic expression containing $a$, $b$, and $c$ to represent this three-digit number.

### Solution to Problem 1

By Forming Algebraic Expressions.

(1) Even numbers are all multiples of $2$, so any even number is $2n$; odd numbers are $1$ greater than the adjacent even number, so any odd number is $2n+1$.

(2) The hundreds digit $c$ represents $100c$, the tens digit $b$ represents $10b$, and the units digit $a$ represents $a$, so this three-digit number is $100c+10b+a$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 2

(Exercise 3.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 11) $3$ teams play a single round-robin tournament (each pair of teams plays one match). What is the total number of matches? What about $4$ teams? $5$ teams? $n$ teams?

### Solution to Problem 2

By Forming Algebraic Expressions.

For $3$ teams: $\frac{3\times 2}{2}=3$ matches; for $4$ teams: $\frac{4\times 3}{2}=6$ matches; for $5$ teams: $\frac{5\times 4}{2}=10$ matches.

Each team plays one match against each of the remaining $\left(n-1\right)$ teams, giving $n\left(n-1\right)$ matches, but each match is counted once by each of the two teams, so for $n$ teams there are $\frac{n\left(n-1\right)}{2}$ matches in total. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 1

By Value of an Algebraic Expression, substitute each value of $a$ into $2-3a$.

Hence the outputs in turn are $-28$, $-10$, $2$, $11$, $-4$. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 2

If the radius of the large circle is $R$, the radius of the small circle is $r$, and the length of the steel pipe is $a$, express the volume $V$ of this section of steel pipe in an algebraic expression. ## Problem 9

(Exercise 3.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) $A$ and $B$ are $s\ \text{km}$ apart. Two people, Jia and Yi, drive from $A$ to $B$ at speeds of $a\ \text{km/h}$ and $b\ \text{km/h}$ respectively, and Jia takes less time.

(1) Express in an algebraic expression how much less time Jia takes than Yi;

(2) When $s=180$, $a=72$, and $b=60$, find the value of the algebraic expression in (1), and explain the practical meaning of this value.

### Solution to Problem 9

By Forming Algebraic Expressions, time $=$ distance $\div$ speed.

(1) Jia takes $\frac{s}{a}\ \text{h}$, Yi takes $\frac{s}{b}\ \text{h}$, and Jia takes $\left(\frac{s}{b}-\frac{s}{a}\right)\ \text{h}$ less than Yi.

(2) By Value of an Algebraic Expression, when $s=180$, $a=72$, and $b=60$,

$$\frac{s}{b}-\frac{s}{a}=\frac{180}{60}-\frac{180}{72}=3-2.5=0.5.$$

Hence Jia takes $0.5\ \text{h}$ less than Yi, that is, Jia arrives half an hour earlier than Yi. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 10

(Exercise 3.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 8) The conversion relationship between the Celsius scale $C$ and the Fahrenheit scale $F$ is $C=\frac{5}{9}\left(F-32\right)$. (1) Convert $25\,^\circ\text{C}$ to degrees Fahrenheit; (2) convert $-4\,^\circ\text{F}$ to degrees Celsius.

### Solution to Problem 10

By Value of an Algebraic Expression.

(1) Inverting $C=\frac{5}{9}\left(F-32\right)$ gives $F=\frac{9}{5}C+32$. When $C=25$, $F=\frac{9}{5}\times 25+32=45+32=77$, that is, $77\,^\circ\text{F}$.

(2) When $F=-4$, $C=\frac{5}{9}\left(-4-32\right)=\frac{5}{9}\times\left(-36\right)=-20$, that is, $-20\,^\circ\text{C}$. $\blacksquare$

## Activity 1

(Math Activity, Activity 1, Puzzle-Building Game, Problem (1)) As shown in the figure, matchsticks are used to build a row of figures made up of triangles. If the figure contains $2$, $3$, or $4$ triangles, how many matchsticks are needed respectively? If the figure contains $n$ triangles, how many matchsticks are needed?

The figure shows a row of triangular figures built from matchsticks: triangles are placed side by side in a row, with adjacent triangles sharing a common side (the figure shows the cases with $2$, $3$, and $4$ triangles).

### Solution to Activity 1

By Forming Algebraic Expressions.

$1$ triangle requires $3$ matchsticks; each additional triangle shares one side with the previous triangle, so only $2$ more matchsticks are needed. Hence $2$ triangles require $3+2=5$ matchsticks, $3$ triangles require $5+2=7$ matchsticks, and $4$ triangles require $7+2=9$ matchsticks.

Following this pattern, $n$ triangles require $3+2\left(n-1\right)=2n+1$ matchsticks. $\blacksquare$

## Activity 2

(Math Activity, Activity 1, Puzzle-Building Game, Problem (2)) As shown in the figure, identical small squares are used to build a large square. Building the $1$-st square requires $4$ small squares, building the $2$-nd square requires $9$ small squares, and building the $3$-rd square requires $16$ small squares. Build and think: by this method, how many more small squares does the $n$-th square require than the $\left(n-1\right)$-th square?

The figure shows the $1$-st, $2$-nd, and $3$-rd squares built respectively from $2\times2$, $3\times3$, and $4\times4$ small squares.

### Solution to Activity 2

By Forming Algebraic Expressions and Value of an Algebraic Expression.

The $1$-st square is $2\times 2=4$ small squares, the $2$-nd is $3\times 3=9$, and the $3$-rd is $4\times 4=16$. Hence the $n$-th square consists of $\left(n+1\right)^{2}$ small squares, and the $\left(n-1\right)$-th square consists of $n^{2}$ small squares.

The difference between the two is

$$\left(n+1\right)^{2}-n^{2}=n^{2}+2n+1-n^{2}=2n+1.$$

Hence the $n$-th square requires $2n+1$ more small squares than the $\left(n-1\right)$-th square. $\blacksquare$

## Review Problem 4

(Review Problem, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) A rope is used to form a rectangle, with the lengths of two adjacent sides being $x\ \text{m}$ and $y\ \text{m}$ respectively.

(1) When the rope is $12\ \text{m}$ long, express the relationship between $y$ and $x$ with an equation.

(2) When the area of the rectangle is $12\ \text{m}^{2}$, express the relationship between $y$ and $x$ with an equation.

(3) When the perimeter of the rectangle is constant, are the lengths of the adjacent sides in inverse proportion? What if the area is constant? Why?

### Solution to Review Problem 4

By Quantities in Inverse Proportion.

(1) The perimeter is $2\left(x+y\right)=12$, hence $y=6-x$.

(2) The area is $xy=12$, hence $y=\frac{12}{x}$.

(3) When the perimeter is constant, $x+y=6$ (the sum is constant), not the product, so the adjacent sides are not in inverse proportion. When the area is constant, $xy=12$ (the product is constant); by Quantities in Inverse Proportion, the adjacent sides are in inverse proportion. $\blacksquare$

## Review Problem 9

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 9) Tanghulu. (1) If each bamboo skewer holds $5$ hawthorns, how many hawthorns are needed to make $n$ skewers of tanghulu? What kind of proportional relationship is there between the total number of hawthorns needed and the number of tanghulu skewers? ## Exercise 7

(Exercise 4.1, Problem 1) Fill in the table:

| Monomial | $2a^{2}$ | $-1.2h$ | $xy^{2}$ | $-t^{2}$ | $-2vt^{3}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coefficient | $\;\_\_\;$ | $\;\_\_\;$ | $\;\_\_\;$ | $\;\_\_\;$ | $\;\_\_\;$ |
| Degree | $\;\_\_\;$ | $\;\_\_\;$ | $\;\_\_\;$ | $\;\_\_\;$ | $\;\_\_\;$ |

### Solution to Exercise 7

By Coefficient of a Monomial, the numerical factor in a monomial is the coefficient; by Degree of a Monomial, the sum of the exponents of all letters is the degree.

For $2a^{2}$, the numerical factor is $2$, the exponent of the letter $a$ is $2$, so the coefficient is $2$ and the degree is $2$.

For $-1.2h$, the numerical factor is $-1.2$, the exponent of the letter $h$ is $1$, so the coefficient is $-1.2$ and the degree is $1$.

For $xy^{2}$, the numerical factor is $1$ (omitted), the exponent of $x$ is $1$, the exponent of $y$ is $2$, the degree is $1+2=3$, so the coefficient is $1$ and the degree is $3$.

For $-t^{2}$, the numerical factor is $-1$ (omitted), the exponent of $t$ is $2$, so the coefficient is $-1$ and the degree is $2$.

For $-2vt^{3}$, the numerical factor is $-2$, the exponent of $v$ is $1$, the exponent of $t$ is $3$, the degree is $1+3=4$, so the coefficient is $-2$ and the degree is $4$. $\blacksquare$
