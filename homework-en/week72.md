## Review Problem 1

(Review Problem, Extension and Exploration, Problem 13) It is known that the radius of a circle is $R$.

(1) Find the perimeter and area of the regular $n$-gon inscribed in this circle;

(2) As the number of sides of a regular polygon inscribed in a circle increases, what is the trend of change of the perimeter (and area) of the regular polygon?

### Solution to Review Problem 1

(1) The central angle subtended by each side of the regular $n$-gon inscribed in the circle is $\frac{2\pi}{n}$. The side length is $a_{n}=2R\sin\frac{\pi}{n}$.

The perimeter is $P_{n}=na_{n}=2nR\sin\frac{\pi}{n}$.

Area: the regular $n$-gon can be divided into $n$ congruent isosceles triangles, each of area $\frac{1}{2}R^{2}\sin\frac{2\pi}{n}$.

$$S_{n}=\frac{n}{2}R^{2}\sin\frac{2\pi}{n}.$$

(2) As $n$ increases, $\sin\frac{\pi}{n}\approx\frac{\pi}{n}$, so $P_{n}\to 2\pi R$ (the circumference of the circle); $\sin\frac{2\pi}{n}\approx\frac{2\pi}{n}$, so $S_{n}\to\pi R^{2}$ (the area of the circle). That is, as the number of sides increases, the perimeter of the regular polygon approaches the circumference of the circle, and the area approaches the area of the circle. $\blacksquare$

## Review Problem 2

(Review Problem, Extension and Exploration, Problem 14) As shown in the figure, in an acute triangle $\triangle ABC$, investigate the relationship among $\frac{a}{\sin A}$, $\frac{b}{\sin B}$, and $\frac{c}{\sin C}$.

### Solution to Review Problem 2

Draw the altitude $h_{a}$ to side $BC$ through $A$. In $\text{Rt}\triangle ABH$, $h_{a}=c\sin B$; in $\text{Rt}\triangle ACH$, $h_{a}=b\sin C$. Hence $c\sin B=b\sin C$, i.e. $\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}$.

Similarly, drawing the altitude to side $AC$ through $B$, we get $c\sin A=a\sin C$, i.e. $\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{c}{\sin C}$.

Thus

$$\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}.\ \blacksquare$$

## Problem 4

(Problem 29.1, Comprehensive Application, Problem 4) The axial cross-section of a cone is parallel to the projection plane, and the orthogonal projection of the cone is an equilateral triangle of side $3$. Find the volume and surface area of the cone.

### Solution to Problem 4

When the axial cross-section is parallel to the projection plane, the orthogonal projection is the axial cross-section itself, an equilateral triangle of side $3$. Thus the base diameter of the cone is $3$, the radius $r=\frac{3}{2}$, and the slant height $l=3$.

The height of the cone is $h=\sqrt{l^{2}-r^{2}}=\sqrt{9-\frac{9}{4}}=\sqrt{\frac{27}{4}}=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

The volume is $V=\frac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h=\frac{1}{3}\pi\times\frac{9}{4}\times\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}=\frac{9\sqrt{3}\pi}{8}$.

The surface area is $S=\pi r^{2}+\pi rl=\pi\times\frac{9}{4}+\pi\times\frac{3}{2}\times 3=\frac{9\pi}{4}+\frac{9\pi}{2}=\frac{27\pi}{4}$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 6

(Exercise 29.2, Problem 2) A factory is processing a batch of bottomless tents, and the designer has given the three views of the tent. ### Solution to Exercise 6

The tent has no bottom, so the surface area $=$ the lateral area of the cylinder $+$ the lateral area of the cone.

The cylinder base diameter is $240$, the radius $r=120$, the height $h=200$. The lateral area of the cylinder $=2\pi rh=2\pi\times 120\times 200=48000\pi$.

The cone slant height is $l=300$, the base radius $r=120$. The lateral area of the cone $=\pi rl=\pi\times 120\times 300=36000\pi$.

The total surface area $=48000\pi+36000\pi=84000\pi\approx 263894\ \text{cm}^{2}$. $\blacksquare$

## Reading and Reflection 1

(Reading and Reflection: The Origin and Application of Views) Why do the three views have wider application than the two views? Can you give an example of two objects of different shape that have the same two views?

### Solution to Reading and Reflection 1

The two views consist of the front view and the top view, and can only reflect the shape of the object in two directions. For example, a cone and a frustum of a cone, when their dimensions are appropriate, may in some cases have front views (an isosceles triangle or an isosceles trapezoid) and top views (concentric circles or a circle) that cannot distinguish the front-back difference. Two objects of different shape (such as a cylinder and a square prism, under specific dimensions) can have the same front view and top view.

The three views add the left view, supplementing the shape information of the object from a third direction and reducing ambiguity, so they have wider application than the two views. $\blacksquare$

## Review Problem 9

(Review Problem, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) Based on the three views, find the surface area of the solid and draw its net. Three views: both the front view and the left view are a rectangle below (height $20$) with a triangle above (height $5$); the top view is a circle of diameter $10$.

The figure shows a set of three views: both the front view and the left view are a rectangle below (height $20$) with a triangle above (height $5$), and the top view is a circle of diameter $10$, corresponding to a composite solid of a cylinder below and a cone on top.

### Solution to Review Problem 9

Since the top view is a circle and the front view is a triangle above a rectangle, the solid is a composite of a lower cylinder (diameter $10$, height $20$) and an upper cone (base diameter $10$, height $5$).

The cylinder base radius is $r=5$, the cylinder height is $h_{1}=20$, and the cone height is $h_{2}=5$.

The cone slant height is $l=\sqrt{r^{2}+h_{2}^{2}}=\sqrt{25+25}=5\sqrt{2}$.

The surface area of the composite $=$ the lateral area of the cylinder $+$ the base area of the cylinder $+$ the lateral area of the cone (the upper base of the cylinder coincides with the base of the cone and is not counted):

$$S=2\pi rh_{1}+\pi r^{2}+\pi rl=2\pi\times 5\times 20+\pi\times 25+\pi\times 5\times 5\sqrt{2}$$

$$=200\pi+25\pi+25\sqrt{2}\,\pi=25\pi\left(9+\sqrt{2}\right).$$

The net is: a rectangle ($2\pi r\times h_{1}=10\pi\times 20$) as the lateral face of the cylinder, a circle of radius $r=5$ as the base, and a sector of radius $l=5\sqrt{2}$ and arc length $10\pi$ as the lateral face of the cone. $\blacksquare$

## Review Problem 10

(Review Problem, Extension and Exploration, Problem 8) Based on the following three views, find the volume of the solid they represent (dimensions are marked in the figure).

The figure shows two sets of dimensioned three views, each corresponding to a different solid, asking to calculate the volume from the labeled dimensions.

(1) Both the front view and the left view are a large rectangle (height $8$) with a small rectangle stacked on top (height $2$, width $4$); the top view is two concentric circles, the outer circle of diameter $8$ and the inner circle of diameter $4$.

(2) The front view is a rectangle (width $4$, height $6$) with a semicircle on top (radius $2$); the left view is a narrow rectangle (width $2$, height $6$); the top view is a rectangle ($4\times 2$).

### Solution to Review Problem 10

(1) Since the top view is concentric circles and both the front and left views are stacked rectangles, the solid is two coaxial cylinders: the lower large cylinder of diameter $8$ (radius $4$), height $8$; the upper small cylinder of diameter $4$ (radius $2$), height $2$.

$$V=\pi\times 4^{2}\times 8+\pi\times 2^{2}\times 2=128\pi+8\pi=136\pi.$$

(2) From the front view, left view, and top view, the lower part of the solid is a rectangular box ($4\times 2\times 6$), and the upper part is a half-cylinder (radius $r=2$, length $2$).

$$V=4\times 2\times 6+\frac{1}{2}\pi\times 2^{2}\times 2=48+4\pi.\ \blacksquare$$
