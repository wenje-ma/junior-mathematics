> **Definition 16.2.1 (Multiplication of a Monomial by a Monomial)**
> 
> Multiply their coefficients and their powers with the same base separately to form factors of the product; for a letter appearing in only one monomial, take it together with its exponent as a factor of the product.

> **Definition 16.2.2 (Multiplication of a Monomial by a Polynomial)**
> 
> Multiply each term of the polynomial by the monomial, then add the resulting products, i.e. $p\left(a+b+c\right)=pa+pb+pc$.

> **Definition 16.2.3 (Multiplication of a Polynomial by a Polynomial)**
> 
> First multiply each term of one polynomial by each term of the other polynomial, then add the resulting products, i.e. $\left(a+b\right)\left(p+q\right)=ap+aq+bp+bq$.

> **Definition 16.2.4 (Division of Powers with the Same Base)**
> 
> $a^m\div{a}^n=a^{m-n}$ ($a\ne0$, $m,n$ are both positive integers, $m>{}n$); that is, when dividing powers with the same base, the base is unchanged and the exponents are subtracted.

> **Definition 16.2.5 (Zero Exponent Power)**
> 
> $a^0=1$ ($a\ne0$); that is, the $0$-th power of any number not equal to $0$ equals $1$.

> **Definition 16.2.6 (Division of a Monomial by a Monomial)**
> 
> Divide the coefficients and the powers with the same base separately to form factors of the quotient; for a letter appearing only in the dividend, take it together with its exponent as a factor of the quotient.

> **Definition 16.2.7 (Division of a Polynomial by a Monomial)**
> 
> First divide each term of this polynomial by the monomial, then add the resulting quotients.

> **Definition 16.3.1 (Difference of Squares Formula)**
> 
> $\left(a+b\right)\left(a-b\right)=a^2-b^2$, that is, the product of the sum of two numbers and the difference of the same two numbers equals the difference of their squares.

> **Definition 16.3.2 (Perfect Square Formula)**
> 
> The square of the sum (or difference) of two numbers equals the sum of their squares plus (or minus) $2$ times their product, i.e. $\left(a+b\right)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2$, $\left(a-b\right)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2$.

> **Definition 16.3.3 (Rule for Adding Parentheses)**
> 
> When the sign before the parentheses is positive, all terms enclosed keep their signs; when the sign before the parentheses is negative, all terms enclosed change their signs, i.e. $a+b+c=a+\left(b+c\right)$, $a-b-c=a-\left(b+c\right)$.

## Exercise 1

(Exercise 16.2, Problem 2) Calculate:

(1) $\left(x+2\right)\left(x+3\right)$;

(2) $\left(x-4\right)\left(x+1\right)$;

(3) $\left(x+4\right)\left(x-2\right)$;

(4) $\left(x-5\right)\left(x-3\right)$.

From the above results find a pattern; observe the figure and fill in the blank: $\left(x+p\right)\left(x+q\right)=\left(\;\;\right)^2+\left(\;\;\right)x+\left(\;\;\right)$.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 16.2.3 (Multiplication of a Polynomial by a Polynomial)**.

(1) $\left(x+2\right)\left(x+3\right)=x^2+5x+6$.

(2) $\left(x-4\right)\left(x+1\right)=x^2-3x-4$.

(3) $\left(x+4\right)\left(x-2\right)=x^2+2x-8$.

(4) $\left(x-5\right)\left(x-3\right)=x^2-8x+15$.

Observing the expressions: the coefficient of the quadratic term is $1$, the coefficient of the linear term equals the sum of the two constants, and the constant term equals the product of the two constants. Hence

$$\left(x+p\right)\left(x+q\right)=x^2+\left(p+q\right)x+pq.$$

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 2

(Exercise 16.2, Problem 3) First simplify, then evaluate: $\left(x-y\right)\left(x^2+xy+y^2\right)-\left(x+y\right)\left(x^2-y^2\right)$, where $x=5$, $y=5$.

### Solution to Exercise 2

By **Definition 16.2.3 (Multiplication of a Polynomial by a Polynomial)**.

$$\left(x-y\right)\left(x^2+xy+y^2\right)=x^3-y^3,$$

$$\left(x+y\right)\left(x^2-y^2\right)=\left(x+y\right)\left(x+y\right)\left(x-y\right)=\left(x+y\right)^2\left(x-y\right).$$

Substituting $x=5,y=5$, we have $x-y=0$, so the original expression $=0-\left(x+y\right)\cdot0=0$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 4

(Exercise 16.2, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) Calculate:

(1) $\left(a^3\right)^2\div\left(a^2\right)^3$;

(2) $\left(ab^2\right)^3\div\left(-ab\right)^2$;

(3) $\left(25a^3b^2\right)\div\left(5ab\right)^2$;

(4) $\left(14m^2n\right)^2\div\left(7m^2\right)\div\left(7m\right)$;

(5) $\left(-5a^3b^5c+35a^2b^3c^2-abc\right)\div\left(-abc\right)$;

(6) $\left(-81x^{n+5}+15x^{n+1}-3x^{n-1}\right)\div\left(-3x^{n-1}\right)$.

### Solution to Problem 4

(1) $\left(a^3\right)^2\div\left(a^2\right)^3=a^6\div{a}^6=1$.

(2) $\left(ab^2\right)^3\div\left(-ab\right)^2=a^3b^6\div\left(a^2b^2\right)=ab^4$.

## Problem 5

(Exercise 16.2, Review and Consolidation, Problem 5) Calculate:

(1) $\left(-3x^2y\right)^2\cdot\left(-\frac23xyz\right)\div\left(\frac34xy^2\right)$;

(2) $\left(-4ab^3-18ab\right)\div\left(-12ab^2\right)$;

(3) $\left(x-3\right)\left(x-2\right)-6\left(x^2+x-1\right)$;

(4) $\left(-4ab^3+8a^2b^2\right)\div\left(4ab\right)-\left(2a+b\right)\left(a-b\right)$.

### Solution to Problem 5

In mixed operations, take powers first, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction.

(1) $\left(-3x^2y\right)^2\cdot\left(-\frac23xyz\right)\div\left(\frac34xy^2\right)=9x^4y^2\cdot\left(-\frac23xyz\right)\div\left(\frac34xy^2\right)=-6x^5y^3z\div\left(\frac34xy^2\right)=-8x^4yz$.

(2) $\left(-4ab^3-18ab\right)\div\left(-12ab^2\right)=\frac13b+\frac{3}{2b}$.

(3) $\left(x-3\right)\left(x-2\right)-6\left(x^2+x-1\right)=x^2-5x+6-6x^2-6x+6=-5x^2-11x+12$.

(4) $\left(-4ab^3+8a^2b^2\right)\div\left(4ab\right)-\left(2a+b\right)\left(a-b\right)=-b^2+2ab-\left(2a^2-2ab+ab-b^2\right)=-b^2+2ab-2a^2+ab+b^2=-2a^2+3ab$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 8

(Exercise 16.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 8) Given $3x-4y=3$ ($x,y$ are positive integers), find $27^x\div9^{2y}$.

### Solution to Problem 8

Reduce to powers with the same base. $27^x=\left(3^3\right)^x=3^{3x}$, $9^{2y}=\left(3^2\right)^{2y}=3^{4y}$.

$$27^x\div9^{2y}=3^{3x}\div3^{4y}=3^{3x-4y}=3^3=27.$$

$\blacksquare$

## Problem 9

(Exercise 16.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 9) Calculate the area of the green shaded region shown in the figure (length unit: $\text{m}$).

The figure shows a large rectangle whose horizontal width is divided, from left to right, into segments of width $a$, $2a$, $2a$, $2a$, $a$ (total width $8a$), and whose total vertical height is $2.5a+1.5a=4a$; at the bottom there are two side-by-side blank small rectangles, each of size $2a\times2.5a$, and the remaining region is the green shaded area.

### Solution to Problem 9

From the figure, the width of the entire outer rectangle is $a+2a+2a+2a+a=8a$ and its height is $2.5a+1.5a=4a$, so the outer area is $8a\cdot4a=32a^2$.

The two blank small rectangles at the bottom are each $2a\times2.5a$, totaling $2\cdot\left(2a\cdot2.5a\right)=10a^2$.

Hence the area of the shaded green region is

$$32a^2-10a^2=22a^2\,\left(\text{m}^2\right).$$

$\blacksquare$

## Problem 10

(Exercise 16.2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 10) As shown in the figure, there is a rectangular cardboard; an equal square is cut off from each of its four corners, and then the protruding portions on all sides are folded up to make an open box in the shape of a cuboid with height $a\;\text{cm}$. If the volume of the box is $4a^2b\;\text{cm}^3$ and one side of the rectangular base has length $b$ ($b<4a$) $\text{cm}$, find the length and width of the original rectangular cardboard.

(The figure is a development diagram of the rectangular cardboard after small squares are cut from its four corners and it is folded up.)

### Solution to Problem 10

The height of the box is $a$, so the side length of the square cut from each corner is $a$. Let the other side of the box base be $x$; then the base area is $bx$. From the volume

$$bx\cdot{a}=4a^2b,$$

solving gives $x=4a$.

Hence the two sides of the box base are $b$ and $4a$. The original cardboard's length exceeds the base length by $2a$, and its width exceeds the base width by $2a$. Since $b<4a$, taking the long base side $4a$ to correspond to the long cardboard side, the original cardboard has length $4a+2a=6a\;\text{cm}$ and width $b+2a\;\text{cm}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 11

(Exercise 16.2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 11) Determine the value of $m$ in each of the following expressions (where $p,q$ are positive integers):

(1) $\left(x+4\right)\left(x+9\right)=x^2+mx+36$;

(2) $\left(x-2\right)\left(x-18\right)=x^2+mx+36$;

(3) $\left(x+3\right)\left(x+p\right)=x^2+mx+36$;

(4) $\left(x-6\right)\left(x-p\right)=x^2+mx+36$;

(5) $\left(x+p\right)\left(x+q\right)=x^2+mx+36$.

### Solution to Problem 11

From $\left(x+p\right)\left(x+q\right)=x^2+\left(p+q\right)x+pq$, the coefficient of the linear term is the sum of the two constants.

(1) $\left(x+4\right)\left(x+9\right)=x^2+13x+36$, hence $m=13$.

(2) $\left(x-2\right)\left(x-18\right)=x^2-20x+36$, hence $m=-20$.

(3) $3p=36$, giving $p=12$, hence $m=3+p=15$.

(4) $\left(-6\right)\left(-p\right)=36$, giving $p=6$, hence $m=-6-p=-12$.

(5) $pq=36$, $m=p+q$. Since $p,q$ are positive integers, take $\left(p,q\right)=\left(1,36\right),\left(2,18\right),\left(3,12\right),\left(4,9\right),\left(6,6\right)$, giving respectively $m=37,20,15,13,12$. $\blacksquare$
