(Exercise 19.3, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7)

Given \(a+\frac{1}{a}=\sqrt{10}\), find the value of \(a-\frac{1}{a}\).

$$
\left(a+\frac{1}{a}\right)^{2}=a^{2}+2+\frac{1}{a^{2}}=10,

As shown in Figure 1, the length-to-width ratio of the rectangular sheet \(ABCD\) is \(\sqrt{2}\).

(2) If the sheet \(ABCD\) is folded as shown in Figure 3, is the rectangle \(GHID\) still "a rectangle whose length-to-width ratio is \(\sqrt{2}\)"? Why?

The figure shows three schematic rectangles arranged from left to right: Figure 1 is rectangle \(ABCD\) with its long sides vertical, \(A\) at upper-left, \(B\) at lower-left, \(C\) at lower-right, \(D\) at upper-right; Figure 2 is the same rectangle with \(E\), \(F\) being the midpoints of the long sides \(AD\), \(BC\) respectively, and a dashed line \(EF\) joining \(E\), \(F\) indicating the fold line; Figure 3 is the small rectangle \(GHID\) obtained after folding in some way.

(1) Let the width of the original rectangle be \(a\) and its length be \(b\); by the problem, \(\frac{b}{a}=\sqrt{2}\).

After folding, the width of rectangle \(ABFE\) is the original width \(a\), and its length is half the original length, \(\frac{b}{2}\).

Hence the length-to-width ratio of the new rectangle is

$$
\frac{a}{b/2}=\frac{2a}{b}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}=\sqrt{2}.
$$

Therefore \(ABFE\) is still a rectangle whose length-to-width ratio is \(\sqrt{2}\).

(2) After folding as in Figure 3, one side of the new rectangle \(GHID\) is still the original width \(a\), and the other side is obtained by subtracting \(a\) from the original length \(b\), i.e. \(b-a\).

From \(\frac{b}{a}=\sqrt{2}\), we get \(b=\sqrt{2}a\), so the other side is \((\sqrt{2}-1)a\).

Hence

$$
\frac{a}{b-a}=\frac{a}{\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}-1}=\sqrt{2}+1.
$$

It is not equal to \(\sqrt{2}\), so \(GHID\) is not a rectangle whose length-to-width ratio is \(\sqrt{2}\).

(Review Problem, Extension and Exploration, Problem 9)

Given that \(n\) is a positive integer and \(\sqrt{189n}\) is an integer, find the minimum value of \(n\).

Factor \(189\) into primes: \(189=3^{3}\times 7\).

For \(189n\) to be a perfect square, the exponents of all prime factors must be even; hence \(n\) must supply at least one more \(3\) and one more \(7\).

Then \(189n=3969=63^{2}\), so the minimum value of \(n\) is \(21\).

(Review Problem, Extension and Exploration, Problem 11)

Determine whether the following equalities hold:

$$
\sqrt{2+\frac{2}{3}}=2\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}},\quad \sqrt{3+\frac{3}{8}}=3\sqrt{\frac{3}{8}},\quad \sqrt{4+\frac{4}{15}}=4\sqrt{\frac{4}{15}}.
$$

If they hold, can you see the pattern? Express this pattern using letters, and give a proof.

Verify the first: the left side is \(\sqrt{\frac{8}{3}}\), and the right side \(2\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}=\sqrt{\frac{8}{3}}\), so it holds.

Similarly, the second \(\sqrt{\frac{27}{8}}=3\sqrt{\frac{3}{8}}\) and the third \(\sqrt{\frac{64}{15}}=4\sqrt{\frac{4}{15}}\) both hold.

Pattern: for an integer \(n\) greater than \(1\),

$$
\sqrt{n+\frac{n}{n^{2}-1}}=n\sqrt{\frac{n}{n^{2}-1}}.
$$

Proof: the radicand on the left side

$$
n+\frac{n}{n^{2}-1}=\frac{n\left(n^{2}-1\right)+n}{n^{2}-1}=\frac{n^{3}}{n^{2}-1}=n^{2}\cdot\frac{n}{n^{2}-1}.
$$

By Basic Knowledge, taking the square root gives \(n\sqrt{\frac{n}{n^{2}-1}}\), which equals the right side.
